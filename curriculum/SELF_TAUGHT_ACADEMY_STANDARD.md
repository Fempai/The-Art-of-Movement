# The Art of Movement — Self-Taught Academy: Final-Draft Acceptance Standard

**This site is the primary instructor.** The learner must not need a human teacher to explain the words on the page, supply missing lesson steps, interpret an assignment, or reveal the answer to a knowledge question. However, a website cannot safely substitute for in-person supervision of high-risk physical skills.

## Completion definition — required for each of 80 lessons

1. **Teach, do not merely outline.** Explain the central concept in original learner-facing prose, define vocabulary on first use, contrast correct and incorrect examples, connect theory to dance practice, and state limitations. No template-only filler.
2. **Explain the demonstration.** Include setup, body orientation, weight-bearing limb, count-by-count actions, tempo, expected sensations, common errors, modifications for no barre, and clear stop conditions. No reliance on a teacher's unwritten explanation.
3. **Independent feedback.** Include observable success criteria, troubleshooting if the exercise fails, how to simplify it, and what must be repeatable before progression. Do not imply that self-assessment proves medical safety.
4. **Academic evidence.** Every substantive factual claim that is not elementary or explicitly an original teaching choice should be traceable to a specific source. Use author, year, title, publication, stable DOI/URL, source type, and accurate description of what the source actually supports. A general source list without claim mapping is insufficient.
5. **Knowledge checks.** Provide varied, nontrivial questions with plausible distractors, explanation for each answer, retake opportunity, and separate skill self-checks. Do not use 'all first options correct' as an assessment design.
6. **Assignment and rubric.** Give a deliverable, a worked example, performance criteria, and clear scoring/feedback rules. Make the difference between objectively auto-gradable knowledge and subjective movement quality explicit.
7. **Editorial and technical verification.** Check source URLs and metadata, remove generic duplicated prose, review accessibility and safety, validate JSON and page links, test the page in a real browser, and test account-based progress as a signed-in user where applicable.
8. **Only then mark FINAL.** A committed file, four section headings, a word count, or a generated lesson is not sufficient. Record verification evidence and outstanding limits.

## Student workflow for each lesson

**Learn:** Read the lesson and source-supported explanation.

**Observe:** Read the count-by-count example and identify the expected movement.

**Practice:** Warm up; rehearse a small-range version; observe foot pressure, timing, and balance; stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of control.

**Explain:** Describe the difference between intended and observed movement without treating a feeling as an objective measurement.

**Check:** Complete the knowledge quiz, review answer explanations, and retry incorrect concepts.

**Apply:** Transfer one idea into the original *Undercurrent* motif and record an annotated eight-count score.

**Reflect:** Write one concrete revision and the evidence for it.

**Progress:** Move to the next task only when you can reproduce the safe foundational action and explain its purpose; use qualified supervision for advanced or hazardous technique.

## Current material classification

The existing 80 structured plans and 80 narrated drafts are **DRAFT** until they meet the above checks. Research bibliographies in older drafts are leads, not a claim of verified comprehensive citations. Automated written checks cannot certify safe dance technique. The academy is an independent nonaccredited learning environment.

## Initial verified source corrections (checked against publisher / PubMed pages on October 29, 2026)

- Wilmerding, V., & Krasnow, D. (2011; revised 2025). *Turnout for Dancers: Hip Anatomy and Factors Affecting Turnout*. IADMS professional resource paper. https://iadms.org/media/rxngdhap/iadms-resource-paper-turnout-for-dancers-hip-anatomy-and-factors-affecting-turnout.pdf — supports anatomical variability and explanation of turnout, **not** a universal required angle.
- Kozai, A., & Surgenor, B. (2017; revised 2025). *The Importance of a Good Warm-Up: Are You Warm Enough to Start Dancing?* IADMS professional resource paper. https://iadms.org/media/glfba5xu/iadms-resource-paper-the_importance_of_a_good_warm-up.pdf — supports warm-up instruction and its rationale, **not** a guarantee against injury.
- Krasnow, D., Wilmerding, M. V., Stecyk, S., Wyon, M., & Koutedakis, Y. (2011). Biomechanical research in dance: A literature review. *Medical Problems of Performing Artists, 26*(1), 3–23. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21442132/ — a literature review of biomechanical studies; explicitly notes variation among dancers and differences between barre and center conditions.
- Wilson, M., & Kwon, Y.-H. (2008). The role of biomechanics in understanding dance movement: A review. *Journal of Dance Medicine & Science, 12*(3), 109–116. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19618586/ — reviews methods for analyzing dance movement, forces, muscle use, and motor control.
- Wyon, M. (2010). Preparing to perform: Periodization and dance. *Journal of Dance Medicine & Science, 14*(2), 67–72. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20507723/ — review of periodization concepts and their possible application to dance; not proof of a guaranteed injury-prevention protocol.

**Citation rule:** cite the specific work only where it actually supports the sentence. History, hip-hop lineage, accessibility, theater production, and aesthetics need their own discipline-specific scholarship. Do not cite a dance-medicine paper as evidence for an unrelated cultural or historical claim.
