# The Art of Movement — Hybrid University–Conservatory Model
**Decision:** Adopt a hybrid of university-style academic education, mastery-based conservatory technique, and an original choreography laboratory.
**Status:** Curriculum governance and first-term design approved for implementation; not an accredited university, degree, license, or professional credential.

## Three independent but connected learning strands
1. **Academic studies:** scheduled terms, structured reading, anatomy, dance history, culture, movement science, critical analysis, quizzes, essays and research. Academic advancement depends on academic achievement, not flexibility.
2. **Technical conservatory:** ballet, jazz, contemporary, hip-hop, floorwork, strength, active flexibility and supervised acro prerequisites. Physical advancement depends on observed technique, safety, recovery and repeatable control, not time served or academic term.
3. **Creative laboratory:** improvisation, choreography, composition, performance analysis, dramaturgy, original repertory and personal artistic voice. Revisit the same motif through progressively complex lessons.

## Levels
**Stage I — Undergraduate-style foundation (Terms 1–4):** dance history and cultural specificity; basic anatomy, joints, alignment, biomechanics, injury prevention; rhythm/meter/syncopation; ballet, jazz, contemporary, hip-hop and floorwork foundations; Laban Body/Effort/Shape/Space; composition, improvisation, basic dance notation; performance observation and criticism.

**Stage II — Graduate-style inquiry (Terms 5–6):** motor learning, movement analysis, choreographic devices, dance research methods, movement psychology, performance studies, critical aesthetics, narrative versus abstraction, chance and site-specific choreography; original capstone and research-informed artist statement.

**Stage III — Professional-style laboratory (Terms 7+):** repertory, rehearsal methods, stagecraft, performance presence, endurance, artistic direction, audition simulations, interdisciplinary collaboration, portfolio and public-facing work when chosen. Supervised advanced acro/contortion is a separate safety-gated pathway.

## Term format
Recommended **12-week terms**, with adjustable pacing and no penalty for physical progression taking longer.
- Weeks 1–2: baseline, terminology, introductory technique and movement motif.
- Weeks 3–5: applied technique, anatomy, musicality and guided composition.
- Week 6: academic midterm and technical feedback checkpoint (not an extreme flexibility test).
- Weeks 7–10: new readings, creative variations, practice and research.
- Week 11: rehearsal, peer/self-critique, revision.
- Week 12: academic final, practical demonstration at individual safe level, creative portfolio submission.
Academic classes can be completed outside gym hours. A single 20–30-minute dance block cannot contain a full 45–60-minute academic lesson.

## Every class uses one repeatable teaching template
Course ID, term, difficulty, prerequisites, essential question, central theme, learning outcomes, cultural/historical context with references, relevant anatomy and biomechanical explanation, warm-up, focused technique drill with sets/reps/quality cues, eight-count combination, adaptation of original choreography, creative improvisation task, written reflection, self-assessment rubric, homework, recall schedule, readiness gates, and modifications for discomfort or fatigue.

## Assessment: two separate scores plus creative portfolio
**Academic mastery (0–100):** readings/knowledge checks 20%, applied analyses 25%, research/writing 25%, final examination/project 30%.
**Technical mastery (rubric, not numerical degree):** alignment and control, musical timing, safe execution, consistency, retention, transitions, performance intent; ratings emerging / developing / consistent / mastered. No automatic graduation for logging practice.
**Creative portfolio:** movement score, motif revisions, recorded private drafts (only when secure media storage exists), reflection, final performance. Creativity is evaluated through clear artistic decisions and revision, not conformity to one aesthetic.

## First term: Movement Literacy and Original Choreography
- MOV 101: Anatomy, alignment and safe range of motion.
- DNC 101: Ballet/jazz foundations and active control.
- MUS 101: Rhythm, meter, musical phrasing and syncopation.
- CMP 101: Choreographic notation, motif and phrase design.
- HIS 101: Dance histories and cultural context (sources must be curated and credited).
- LAB 101: Original repertory **Undercurrent** — restraint becoming freedom; retain early versions and build complexity through subsequent terms.
- STG 101: Strength and movement quality: strict pull-up and 10 full push-ups; dance-specific lower-body strength, stamina, floor-to-standing transitions, extension control.

## First-term sample week (flexible)
Monday: MOV 101 reading (20–30m separate from gym), ballet/jazz technique (20–30m gym).
Tuesday: MUS 101 ear-training and phrase analysis (20m), technique/stamina (20–30m).
Wednesday: CMP 101 choreography lab (30–45m), contemporary/floorwork technique (20–30m).
Thursday: HIS 101 reading/reflection (20–30m), gentle mobility or rest.
Friday: LAB 101 Undercurrent rehearsal (20–30m), review recall and score.
Weekend: optional creative project, restorative movement and self-review.
The gym routine can retain the established BFR → treadmill → dynamic mobility → dance → flexibility sequence; total duration may exceed one hour. Do not stack exhaustive BFR and strenuous acro work without adequate recovery.

## Specific long-term milestones (not guaranteed)
- 180-degree unsupported leg extension: separate passive and active range, controlled lifts/holds and pain-free readiness; no forced turnout or ballistic ankle weights.
- Advanced head-to-foot backbend: assess spinal extension distribution, shoulders, hip extension, strength and recovery with a qualified specialist; no forced end range.
- Inversions: prerequisites and qualified in-person supervision for risky progressions.
- Strength: one strict controlled pull-up; ten strict full push-ups. Lower-body strength remains important for dance.

## Choreography retention system
Capture spontaneous imagery immediately with a short voice memo; then transcribe to a five-column score (count/time, movement, facing/pathway, musical cue, intention). Use four- and eight-count chunks, retrieval without reference, practice at varied slow tempos, and spaced checks after 24 hours, 3 days and 7 days. Measure accuracy and artistic continuity separately.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Publish this program model and an accessible student-facing overview.
2. Build course catalog with structured lesson data and detailed first-term classes.
3. Add separate academic progress, technical rubric and portfolio tracking.
4. Create source-grounded readings and original anatomy diagrams; review for cultural accuracy and biomechanical safety.
5. Implement secure, private media and consent controls before any student video upload.
6. Expand to graduate inquiry and performance laboratory only after foundation content is complete and tested.

## Safeguards
No false accreditation, professional certification, diagnostic assessment or promises of attaining contortion-level flexibility. No advanced unsupervised inversion/contortion instruction. User's past flexibility supports a historical baseline, not a present anatomical clearance. Respect copyright, cultural origin, and licensing for educational materials.
