# The Art of Movement — Flexibility, Strength and Acro curriculum map

**Status:** Teaching syllabus and assessment specification. Not a published video course. A module is only a video class after qualified instruction, rights clearance and playback testing.

## Curriculum architecture
The academy teaches dance from Level I, while flexibility, mobility and strength are **concurrent disciplines**. Acro has a separate safety-gated pathway. No student must earn splits before dancing, and passive flexibility does not by itself demonstrate safe active range.

### Level I: Movement foundations (suggested 12-week block, flexible pacing)
| Week | Flexibility / mobility focus | Strength and control focus | Applied dance skill | Evidence and progression |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline: comfortable hip, ankle, shoulder range; warm-up literacy | Breathing, bracing, controlled squat/hinge | Ballet alignment and jazz parallel/turnout | Repeatable baseline measurements; comfortable effort |
| 2 | Ankle dorsiflexion, foot articulation | Bilateral calf raise, supported single-leg balance | Relevé, weight transfers | Knee-to-wall and balance notes |
| 3 | Hip hinge, hamstring tolerance | Slow hinge, posterior chain | Jazz kicks below maximal range | Stable trunk and no forced depth |
| 4 | Hip flexor mobility, pelvic control | Supported lunge, controlled pelvic tilt | Ballet tendu/dégagé and belly-dance pelvic isolations | Distinguish pelvis from lumbar compensation |
| 5 | Adductor mobility, lateral weight transfer | Lateral squat regressions and adductor isometrics | Contemporary side travel and level changes | Comfortable transitions |
| 6 | Thoracic rotation and shoulder flexion | Scapular control and supported arm patterns | Port de bras, contemporary reach | Rib-cage and shoulder control |
| 7 | Active hip flexion, leg extension | Supported active leg lifts, single-leg stance | Jazz extension preparation | Active height, control, balance |
| 8 | Split preparation without forcing | Isometric control in comfortable lunge | Low-level traveling phrases | No pain or joint instability |
| 9 | Spine articulation, thoracic extension | Anti-extension trunk strength | Contemporary contraction/release | Controlled segmentation, no lumbar collapse |
| 10 | Dynamic mobility integrated with music | Tempo-controlled strength | Hip-hop grooves and ballet center patterns | Movement quality maintained with counts |
| 11 | Individual mobility limitations | Balanced strength and recovery | Chosen discipline's short combination | Identify needs without universal range targets |
| 12 | Retest and reflect | Repeat controlled baseline movements | Recorded dance phrase for formative review | Comparable camera and warm-up; no invented score |

## Level I flexibility video modules
1. Warm-up and mobility safety: active/passive range, sensation versus pain, safe self-regulation.
2. Feet and ankles for dancers: controlled articulation, dorsiflexion, relevé preparation.
3. Hip hinges and hamstring mobility: knee options, hinge control, active range.
4. Hip flexor mobility and pelvic organization: supported lunges and stable trunk.
5. Adductors and middle split preparation: lateral shifts, easy isometrics, no forced splits.
6. Shoulders and thoracic spine: arm lines, controlled rotation and scapular rhythm.
7. Active flexibility: supported leg lifts, controlled extensions and balance.
8. Integration and reassessment: dance phrase, matched baselines and training plan revision.

**Video production requirements for each:** 3–5-minute warm-up, 5–10-minute technique explanation, 10–20-minute follow-along, modifications, red-flag stop cues, captions, demonstration from useful camera angles, optional short self-check, credits and rights.

## Strength pathway
Level I: hinge, squat, push, pull, carry, trunk control, unilateral balance, load management, recovery. Resistance is scaled to ability. Avoid assuming a dancer is new to strength training. Progress by controlled execution, consistent effort and recovery, not a fixed weight target.

## Acro pathway: not unsupervised instruction
- **A0 — Readiness:** injury history and risk discussion, wrist/shoulder loading tolerance, landing surface, spotter/coach access, fall safety and emergency plan. No skill clearance from an AI image alone.
- **A1 — Ground-based foundations:** low-risk floor shapes, crawling patterns, supported weight transfers and basic rolls **only with appropriate instruction and safe space**.
- **A2 — Supported inversions:** instructor-supervised prerequisites, equipment, spotting and individualized clearance.
- **A3 — Tumbling/advanced acro:** qualified in-person coach, proper mats and progression. No AI-only certification, self-taught aerials, handsprings, walkovers or headstands from synthetic video.

## Assessments
- Baseline: front/side/back posture photos optional; active leg lift left/right, ankle knee-to-wall, comfortable fold/split gap, single-leg balance; record method and limitations.
- Process: self-reported discomfort, control, recovery and confidence, not simply centimeters gained.
- Follow-up: optional 4–6-week retest with matched angle and conditions.
- Instructor review: timestamped observable feedback, distinct from diagnosis or objective proficiency grading.
- Do not assign universal split deadlines or use generated videos as the only reference for body mechanics.

## Source/production status
No instructor-led flexibility or acro video has been published as an academy-owned class yet. This syllabus establishes the course sequence, safety gates and evidence standard for production.
