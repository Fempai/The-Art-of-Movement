# CMP502 · Lesson 01 — Retrograde: Reversing a Movement Sequence

**Advanced Composition | graduate-level creative inquiry | 50–70 minutes.** This is an authored learner-facing revision; bibliographic audit, expert review, and browser acceptance testing remain pending.

## Lecture: define the compositional device

**Learning goals:** Explain the featured device, construct a reproducible movement score, identify its difference from simple repetition, and justify a revision without claiming the resulting work has a universal emotional meaning.

Retrograde is a compositional transformation that presents selected movement events in reverse order. It is not necessarily a literal reverse-time video: a dancer must make physical choices about support, momentum, and transitions. If the source motif is step → gather → reach → recover, a conceptual retrograde is recover → reach → gather → step. Because 'recover' is a relation to a preceding action, it must be redefined as a visible movement event when performed first. Document what has been reversed (event order, pathway, or both) rather than claiming a perfect reversal. An original work can use retrograde without implying it reproduces any named choreographer's piece.

## Guided choreography laboratory

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Warm up with comfortable walking or seated shifts and gentle arm movements. Use an open nonslip area. No falls, forced ranges, or unsupported inversions are required.

**Build and rehearse (20 minutes):** Write the four actions of Undercurrent on separate cards: step right, gather hands, reach diagonally, return arm to center. Perform the original over eight counts: step 1–2, gather 3–4, reach 5–6, return 7–8. Now design a viable reverse event order: start with arms at center, extend on 1–2 (reverse of return), draw the arm inward on 3–4 (reverse of reach), open hands away from chest on 5–6 (reverse of gather), and shift weight left on 7–8 (reverse of step). Record initial positions and support changes explicitly. Perform each at half speed, then compare which movement properties were preserved and which were redesigned.

**Troubleshooting and critical editing:** If the retrograde requires a dangerous backward step or abrupt stop, choose a smaller weight shift and mark the redesign. If the beginning position makes an action impossible, alter the starting pose and state the adjustment rather than hiding it.

**Analytical comparison:** Record the original motif and its transformation in separate columns: event, count, pathway, support, and changed compositional rule. Explain which elements remain recognizable and why. If a video is unavailable, a detailed count score is sufficient.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is retrograde in this exercise?**

A. Performing the same events in the original order at a faster tempo

B. Reversing selected movement-event order

C. Mirroring the same movement sequence without reversing its event order

D. Repeating the original phrase with shorter holds but unchanged event order

**2. Why may literal reversal be impractical?**

A. Reversing the order automatically guarantees a stable support foot at every transition

B. Reversing the count order preserves every original momentum and support condition without adjustment

C. Support and momentum must remain physically manageable

D. A written retrograde rule removes the need to check whether the new sequence is physically manageable

**3. Which record makes the transformation transparent?**

A. A count sheet showing the original and reversed event order

B. A video of the reversed version without identifying which events changed order

C. A final pose photograph without a record of the reversed sequence

D. A statement that the reversal felt different without comparing event order

**4. What should happen if a reversed transition is unsafe?**

A. Preserve the literal reverse despite an unsupported weight transfer

B. Keep the unstable transition but shorten its duration

C. Replace the transition without noting the deviation from the rule

D. Redesign the transition and document it

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. B.** In this exercise retrograde reverses the selected order of movement events while keeping a record of what was transformed. Speeding up without reordering (A), mirroring the original sequence (C), or shortening holds without reversing events (D) is not the taught retrograde rule.

**2. C.** A literal event reversal may create a transition with insufficient support or unmanageable momentum, so physical feasibility must be checked. Neither reversal itself (A), preserved counts (B), nor a written rule (D) guarantees a stable transition.

**3. A.** Original and reversed count sheets allow a reader to compare the event order directly. A reversed video without an identified source sequence (B), a single final pose (C), or a feeling-only description (D) cannot show which event positions changed.

**4. D.** Redesign an unsafe support transition and record the deviation from the literal rule so the resulting score is both executable and transparent. Preserving instability (A), shortening an unsafe action (B), or changing it without disclosure (C) fails the safety or documentation criterion.

**Self-review:** For any missed item, correct the compositional rule and demonstrate the difference in a smaller four-count version. These questions are original instructional checks, not a validated professional assessment.

## Independent assignment

Submit a movement score, a performance or private rehearsal log, and a 250–350-word reflection explaining the transformation, an observable weakness, and a justified revision. **Lesson 04 capstone:** create a **2–4 minute original composition** integrating at least three devices (for example retrograde, accumulation, and fragmentation/chance), with count-level timing, section boundaries, and an artist statement. A private video is optional; the score must stand alone.

**Worked example:** “My source sequence is step–gather–reach–return. The retrograde score starts by extending the arm from center, then draws it inward. I replaced a backward weight shift with a small side shift because the literal reverse was unstable. I preserved the event-order reversal and documented the safety edit.”

**Rubric:** Device accuracy and transparent rules 30%; coherent, executable transitions 25%; originality and structural development 25%; critique, timing accuracy, and revision rationale 20%. The self-score is not a professional adjudication.
