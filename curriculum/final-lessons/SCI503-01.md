# SCI503 · Lesson 01 — Practice Variability: Stable Targets, Changing Conditions

**Motor Learning & Dance Science | graduate-level independent study | 45–60 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; source audit, expert review, and browser testing remain outstanding. Do not intentionally train to exhaustion or push through pain.

## Lecture: explain the learning mechanism

**Learning outcomes:** Distinguish observed performance from inferred learning, apply one defined practice method, collect repeatable movement observations, and identify what the data cannot prove.

Motor performance is what a learner does during a practice attempt; motor learning is a relatively lasting change in capability inferred from retention or transfer, not proven by one successful repetition. Practice variability changes a defined feature of the task or context while preserving a stable learning target. For the Undercurrent motif, the stable target can be accurate action order and count placement while facing direction or reach height changes. Constant practice may be useful when first learning a complex task; varied practice may support adaptability, but benefits depend on task, skill level, and study design. A small solo exercise cannot establish a general superiority of one schedule.

## Guided movement-science laboratory

**Prepare:** Warm up with gentle walking or seated weight shifts. Choose a safe, small-range version of the eight-count Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover motif.

**Protocol (15–20 minutes):** Choose the eight-count score: 1 step right, 2 settle, 3–4 gather, 5–6 reach, 7 return, 8 balance. Warm up, then complete six low-intensity trials. Trials 1–2: front-facing, shoulder-height reach. Trials 3–4: front-facing, lower reach. Trials 5–6: face a comfortable diagonal, shoulder-height reach. Speak the same counts in every trial. Record action-order errors, reach onset count, balance corrections, and the varied condition. **Keep a consistent comfortable rest interval between attempts (for example, 30–60 seconds) and record the actual duration. If using seated marking, sit in a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported; replace the step on 1 with a small rightward sitting-pressure shift and settle on 2, and use gaze orientation rather than a full chair turn for the diagonal condition. This is a seated adaptation, not a standing-balance comparison.** Finish with one front-facing trial after a short break and note whether the target score is retained.

**Troubleshooting and limitations:** If changing facing causes confusion, vary only arm height. If accuracy drops, return to the original condition for one clear repetition rather than adding more variables. Do not label a single successful last trial proof of lasting learning.

**Research integrity check:** Record the planned trial count, actual trial count, condition, prompts, and deviations. Do not change scoring definitions after seeing outcomes without documenting the change. A solo log can help direct practice but cannot establish general causal effects.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is kept stable in the taught variability exercise?**

A. The reach height, even though height is the condition being varied

B. The action order and count placement

C. The exact facing direction, even though orientation is deliberately varied

D. The learner's reported effort, even though effort may vary between trials

**2. What is the key distinction between performance and learning?**

A. A single accurate performance demonstrates the same retention as a delayed check

B. Learning is inferred from relatively lasting capability, not one practice attempt

C. The fastest trial establishes lasting learning without further testing

D. Repeating a successful trial immediately is sufficient to prove transfer to a new task

**3. Which is a controlled variation?**

A. Change the beat placement and reach height together, then attribute the result to height

B. Shift the reach onset to a different count while claiming the same score was retained

C. Alter reach height while keeping the score

D. Change the phrase order and movement amplitude simultaneously without documenting either

**4. What should you conclude from one good final trial?**

A. The successful attempt demonstrates the same accuracy will persist after a week

B. One performer's success predicts equal benefit for other dancers

C. The final success proves that fatigue played no role in earlier attempts

D. Only that this attempt met the observed target

### Answer key and explanation

**1. B.** The exercise preserves action order and beat placement so the learner can compare trials while varying a selected condition. Holding reach height constant (A) or facing fixed (C) contradicts the stated variations, while reported effort (D) is not the controlled score element.

**2. B.** Learning is inferred from relatively durable capability, typically examined through delayed retention or transfer, not a single good attempt. Immediate accuracy (A), speed (C), and instant repetition (D) do not demonstrate that performance persists or transfers.

**3. C.** Changing reach height while preserving the score lets the learner observe the effect of that condition more clearly. Changing timing and height together (A), moving the onset count (B), or altering order and amplitude without records (D) confounds the comparison.

**4. D.** One final attempt shows only that the observed performance met its target on that occasion. It cannot establish week-later retention (A), predict other dancers' results (B), or rule out fatigue as a contributor to earlier trials (C).

**Self-review:** Explain why any missed alternative confuses outcome with process, immediate performance with learning, or observation with causal inference. These are original teaching questions, not validated cognitive or clinical assessments.

## Independent assignment

Submit a dated six-trial practice-variability log for the eight-count Undercurrent score. Record the condition for each trial (1–2 front/shoulder-height, 3–4 front/lower reach, 5–6 diagonal/shoulder-height), action-order errors, reach onset, balance corrections, rest, and any deviations. Add the single front-facing post-break check separately; it is not a seventh trial in the six-trial condition comparison. Write a 250–350-word analysis identifying the stable target, what varied, a competing explanation, and one safe next practice adjustment. Do not claim short-term performance proves lasting learning.

**Worked example:** “My hypothetical log shows the reach starting on count 5 in both front-facing shoulder-height trials and on count 6 in one diagonal trial. The task score remained unchanged while facing varied. I cannot infer a general learning advantage from six trials or a short-break check; unfamiliar facing or observation error could explain the difference.”

**Rubric:** Clear constant target and controlled conditions 30%; complete and honest trial log 30%; performance-versus-learning inference and competing explanation 25%; safe justified adjustment 15%. No score rewards exhaustion or extreme range.
