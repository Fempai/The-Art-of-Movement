# EST504 · Lesson 02 — Evidence: Build a Defensible Performance Claim

**Dance Criticism & Aesthetics | graduate-level independent study | 50–70 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly citation audit, expert editorial review, and live-browser testing are outstanding.

## Critical framework

**Outcomes:** Distinguish description, interpretation, and evaluation; cite movement-specific evidence; recognize alternative readings; and critique the work without unsupported assumptions about a performer.

A performance claim should identify a specific formal choice and explain how evidence supports an evaluative judgment. Evidence can include a time-stamped video observation, a movement score, a program note, a documented interview, or scholarship. These sources answer different questions: a recording may show timing, a program note may report an artistic intention, and scholarship may supply a conceptual framework. A critic should not cite a generic publisher homepage as proof of a specific historical claim, or treat an abstract as if the full article were reviewed. A strong paragraph connects claim, evidence, reasoning, and a limitation.

## Guided performance-analysis studio

**Preparation:** Clear a safe area and mark a comfortable version of Undercurrent's step–gather–reach–recover motif. If performing is unsuitable, analyze an existing self-created score instead.

**Observe and compare (20–25 minutes):** Choose one claim: 'The longer reach in Undercurrent A′ makes the motif's return more recognizable.' Record a 32-count draft or use the written score. Note that A reaches on counts 5–6, while A′ sustains on 21–23. Create an evidence ledger with four columns: claim, observed feature and count, source or recording condition, and alternative explanation. Write a 180-word paragraph that compares the two phrases, states why the duration contrast matters to your reading, and acknowledges that viewer perception was not measured. Revise the claim if the footage does not clearly show the hands.

**Revision and ethical check:** If the claim is 'everyone sees hesitation,' narrow it to your interpretation and cite the specific timing cue. If your recording cuts off the arm, mark the evidence incomplete rather than inventing movement details.

**Evidence protocol:** Separate your notes into three headings: (1) what can be observed, with counts or timestamps; (2) what you infer and why; and (3) how you evaluate the choice under a stated criterion. A recording does not provide access to private emotions or universal audience reactions.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is the best evidence for a reach's duration?**

A. A program note describing the intended mood but not the movement counts

B. A counted movement score or time-stamped recording

C. A written description of the pose with no duration or timing information

D. A viewer's recollection of how long the reach felt without a timestamp

**2. What is a source-specific limitation?**

A. A program note establishes the same kinds of facts as a time-stamped performance video

B. A program note's stated intention establishes how every audience member responded

C. A program note reports intention, not universal reception

D. A performance recording directly reveals the dancer's unspoken intention

**3. Which paragraph structure is most defensible?**

A. Claim, evidence, reasoning, and limitation

B. A thesis restated in several ways without count-specific support

C. Several relevant citations without explaining how they support the claim

D. A detailed plot summary that omits the performed movement evidence

**4. What should happen when a crucial gesture is outside the frame?**

A. Describe the planned gesture from the score as if it were visible in the video

B. Infer the missing hand position from the performer's visible facial expression

C. Treat the missing footage as proof that the gesture was never performed

D. Mark the observation unavailable and adjust the evidence claim

### Answer key and rationale

**1. B.** Counts and visible timing support duration claims.

**2. C.** Different sources support different kinds of claims.

**3. A.** The structure ties evaluation to observable support.

**4. D.** The claim must reflect what the source can show.

**Self-correction:** For missed items, rewrite one unsupported assertion as a situated, evidence-supported claim. These questions are instructional, not a validated critical-writing examination.

## Independent critical assignment

Produce a 300–450-word analysis containing a thesis, at least four movement-specific observations, an alternative reading, and a source-limit statement. For Lesson 04, submit a **1,000–1,300-word critical essay** comparing narrative and abstract versions of the 32-count Undercurrent phrase, with six count-specific observations, a clear thesis, relevant verified scholarship, ethical attribution, and a conclusion identifying the limits of your interpretation.

**Worked example:** “The diagonal reach lasts two counts in A but three in A′. I read the added duration as a moment of uncertainty, although another viewer could interpret it as resolve. The timing contrast is observable; the emotion is my interpretation.”

**Rubric:** Accuracy and specificity of movement description 30%; reasoning and evidence 30%; engagement with alternatives and context 20%; ethical attribution, structure, and source transparency 20%. No credit for fabricated citations or presumed performer psychology.
