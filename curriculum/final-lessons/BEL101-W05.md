# BEL101 · Friday Week 05 — Beginner Movement Vocabulary

**Original 16-week belly-dance track | 35–50 minutes.** This is a self-study lesson built around the original Day 1 Friday video plan. The exact original Google Drive URL has not yet been recovered; do not substitute an unrelated video or claim playback was tested.

**Original planned video:** Top 10 Moves Every Belly Dancer Must Know

## Learning outcomes and concept

Compare three small movements already practiced—side slide, small circle, and alternating pulse—without assuming that any video list is a universal canon. Identify weight support and timing, demonstrate the eight-count version twice, and describe one correction and one evidence limit.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm-up (4–5 minutes):** March or shift weight comfortably; move ankles and shoulders gently. Clear a nonslip space, use a stable fixed support as needed, and avoid forced rotation or painful range. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of control.

**Count-by-count guided study (8–12 minutes):** 1–2 small slide right and return; 3–4 small slide left and return; 5–6 small comfortable circle; 7–8 two gentle alternating pulses.

**Repeat (5 minutes):** First say counts aloud at a slow tempo. Second focus on where your weight is supported. Third add a modest accent without increasing the movement range. Rest between attempts. When the original video link is restored, use it to compare the demonstrated vocabulary with this original drill, noting any difference rather than assuming equivalence.

**Modification:** Keep the circle as a hand-traced pathway while seated.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist or your lower back compresses, reduce range and return to comfortable parallel stance. If the pulse disappears, mark counts with claps before moving again. Do not treat pain as evidence of productive practice.

**Success check:** Can you name which movement changes direction continuously? **Worked observation:** The small circle; a slide travels laterally and a pulse repeats a rhythmic action. This checkpoint assesses self-observation, not medical safety or expert performance.

## Knowledge check — try before answers

**1. Which action belongs on counts 5–6 in the guided vocabulary phrase?**

A. A rapid turn

B. A small comfortable circle

C. A high kick

D. A held pose

**2. A learner confuses the slide and circle. What difference should they check?**

A. The slide must be faster

B. The circle must travel across the floor

C. The slide requires a backbend

D. The slide moves laterally; the circle continuously changes direction

**3. The circle causes discomfort but the learner can still count. What preserves the learning objective?**

A. Trace the circular pathway by hand while seated

B. Increase the circle's size

C. Lock the knees

D. Hold the breath through counts 5–6

**4. What does the title 'Top 10 Moves' establish by itself?**

A. A universal list of movements

B. Certification of the learner

C. A planned video topic, not a verified universal canon

D. The history of every regional style

### Answers and explanations

**1. B.** The sequence places right and left slides on counts 1–4, then a small circle on counts 5–6 before the pulses.

**2. D.** Lateral travel of the pelvis and a changing circular pathway are distinguishable movement descriptions in the lesson.

**3. A.** A hand-traced circle preserves directional vocabulary and counts without demanding uncomfortable pelvic motion.

**4. C.** A title can identify a proposed source topic, but it cannot prove a universal canon or authenticate a specific movement lineage.

**Retake rule:** For a missed question, revisit the relevant count or pathway, mark the movement slowly, and retry. These questions assess introductory understanding, not professional competence or cultural authority.

## Independent assignment

Record or write two eight-count attempts, identifying each count, supporting foot or seated surface, movement direction, and rhythmic accent. In 150–250 words compare an observable difference, one correction, one adaptation, and one uncertainty. Clearly distinguish your original practice from the named video technique, especially while the source URL remains unverified.

**Rubric:** movement pathway and counts 30%; self-correction 25%; modification and control 25%; careful cultural and source attribution 20%. Never grade maximal range or speed.
