## Learning outcomes and concept

Separate a figure-eight pathway from traveling steps. Learn a small horizontal figure-eight intention without forcing lumbar rotation, then add a simple weight shift only if stable. Identify weight support and timing, demonstrate the eight-count version twice, and describe one correction and one evidence limit.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm-up (4–5 minutes):** March or shift weight comfortably; move ankles and shoulders gently. Clear a nonslip space, use a stable fixed support as needed, and avoid forced rotation or painful range. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of control.

**Count-by-count guided study (8–12 minutes):** 1–4 trace a small right-loop then left-loop pelvic pathway while grounded; 5–6 step gently right and bring feet closer; 7–8 settle and mark the same pathway in a smaller range.

**Repeat (5 minutes):** First say counts aloud at a slow tempo. Second focus on where your weight is supported. Third add a modest accent without increasing the movement range. Rest between attempts. When the original video link is restored, use it to compare the demonstrated vocabulary with this original drill, noting any difference rather than assuming equivalence.

**Modification:** Draw a sideways figure eight with one hand while seated and shift pressure gently between sitting bones.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist or your lower back compresses, reduce range and return to comfortable parallel stance. If the pulse disappears, mark counts with claps before moving again. Do not treat pain as evidence of productive practice.

**Success check:** Can you explain whether the pelvis or the whole body traveled? **Worked observation:** The figure-eight describes a pathway; traveling means the body's location changes. This checkpoint assesses self-observation, not medical safety or expert performance.

## Knowledge check — try before answers

**1. What is the clearest difference between the first and second parts of the eight-count phrase?**

A. Counts 1–4 require a jump

B. Counts 5–6 repeat a stationary loop

C. Counts 1–4 travel farther

D. Counts 1–4 trace a grounded pathway; counts 5–6 include a step changing position

**2. A learner cannot comfortably trace the figure eight with the pelvis. What preserves the pathway study?**

A. Force a larger twist

B. Trace the loops with a hand while seated

C. Add a quick spin

D. Ignore the direction of the loops

**3. What would show that the traveling portion actually occurred?**

A. The learner's floor position changed after the step

B. The learner felt more confident

C. The movement looked culturally authentic

D. The hips moved through a larger range

**4. The learner loses timing at the change from looping to stepping. What is the next useful drill?**

A. Increase the movement range

B. Perform the full phrase faster

C. Practice counts 3–6 slowly and identify support during the step

D. Add a new arm movement

### Answers and explanations

**1. D.** The initial figure-eight intention is performed while grounded, whereas the later step changes the dancer's floor position.

**2. B.** A seated hand trace retains the two-loop directional idea without requiring pelvic rotation.

**3. A.** Floor position can be compared before and after the step; private feelings and cultural provenance are not established by the motion.

**4. C.** Slowing the transition between counts 3–6 isolates the connection between the pathway and the change in floor position.

**Retake rule:** For a missed question, revisit the relevant count or pathway, mark the movement slowly, and retry. These questions assess introductory understanding, not professional competence or cultural authority.
