## Learning outcomes and concept

Separate a figure-eight pathway from traveling steps. Learn a small horizontal figure-eight intention without forcing lumbar rotation, then add a simple weight shift only if stable. Identify weight support and timing, demonstrate the eight-count version twice, and describe one correction and one evidence limit.

**Optional source-observation study (3–5 minutes, if accessible):** Distinguish a figure-eight pathway from a traveling step in the scheduled video: record whether the dancer changes floor position, which foot supports the motion, and whether the figure-eight direction can be seen. Compare only matched observable components with the written loop-to-step drill. If the demonstration differs or is inaccessible, note **source not observed / movement match undetermined** rather than claiming the drill reproduces the video.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm-up (4–5 minutes):** March or shift weight comfortably; move ankles and shoulders gently. Clear a nonslip space, use a stable fixed support as needed, and avoid forced rotation or painful range. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of control.

**Count-by-count guided study (8–12 minutes):** First mark a sideways figure-eight with one hand: counts 1–2 trace a small right-side loop returning toward center, and 3–4 trace a matching left-side loop returning toward center. Then, if comfortable, repeat as a tiny pelvic intention with feet grounded, without forcing the back or twisting planted knees. For travel, **before count 5, begin in a comfortably supported parallel stance with enough weight on the left foot to free the right**. Count 5: take a small step to the right with the right foot and transfer weight onto it only as comfortable; 6: bring the left foot toward the right without crossing or forcing a pivot, then settle into comfortable support. **Use a fixed support if needed, and compare floor marks before and after counts 5–6 before calling the step 'travel'.** Counts 7–8: settle the stance and hand-trace a smaller right–left pathway while staying in the new floor position. Mark the loop and step separately before connecting counts 3–6. Traveling is optional if balance is uncertain; record whether the body's floor position actually changed.

**Repeat (5 minutes):** First say counts aloud at a slow tempo. Second focus on where your weight is supported. Third add a modest accent without increasing the movement range. Rest between attempts. When you can access the restored original video link, use it to compare the demonstrated vocabulary with this original drill, noting any difference rather than assuming equivalence.

**Modification:** While seated, trace the right and left figure-eight loops with one hand on counts 1–4. On counts 5–6, gesture gently to the right to represent the intended traveling step without claiming actual floor travel; on counts 7–8, settle and repeat a smaller hand-traced pathway. Keep the sitting surface stable and avoid uncomfortable pelvic rotation.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist or your lower back compresses, reduce range and return to comfortable parallel stance. If the transition from the second loop to the rightward step loses timing, isolate counts 3–6 slowly, check which foot supports you, and rehearse the step without forcing the pelvic pathway. Do not treat pain as evidence of productive practice.

**Success check:** On each of two eight-count attempts, mark **right loop 1–2 / left loop 3–4 / right step 5 / left gather 6 / settle and smaller hand pathway 7–8** as **on count / off count / unobservable**. Compare marked starting and ending floor positions to record **actual travel / no travel / unobservable** separately from pathway accuracy; a seated gesture is **symbolic travel only**. **Worked observation:** The figure-eight describes a pathway; traveling requires a change in the body's floor location. This checkpoint assesses self-observation, not medical safety or expert performance.

## Knowledge check — try before answers

**1. What is the clearest difference between the first and second parts of the eight-count phrase?**

A. Counts 1–4 trace a stationary pathway, while counts 5–6 repeat that pathway in place

B. Counts 1–4 change floor position, while counts 5–6 trace a stationary loop

C. Counts 1–4 and 5–6 both trace loops without a positional change

D. Counts 1–4 trace a grounded pathway; counts 5–6 introduce an optional step that can change floor position

**2. A learner cannot comfortably trace the figure eight with the pelvis. What preserves the pathway study?**

A. Replace the two loops with one comfortable side-to-side slide

B. Trace the loops with a hand while seated

C. Trace one loop with the hand but omit the reverse loop

D. Mark the same counts using alternating hand taps without tracing a pathway

**3. What would show that the traveling portion actually occurred?**

A. The learner's floor position changed after the step

B. The pelvis traced a wider figure eight while the feet stayed planted

C. The learner shifted weight from one foot to the other without changing floor location

D. The dancer completed both loops on the intended counts while remaining in place

**4. The learner loses timing at the change from looping to stepping. What is the next useful drill?**

A. Repeat counts 1–4 until the stationary loops look smoother, without rehearsing the step

B. Practice only counts 5–6 while omitting the preceding loop-to-step transition

C. Practice counts 3–6 slowly and identify support during the step

D. Count the complete phrase aloud without isolating the change in support

### Answers and explanations

**1. D.** Counts 1–4 trace a grounded figure-eight pathway, while 5–6 introduce an optional rightward step. That step counts as actual travel **only if floor marks confirm a position change**; the seated gesture is symbolic, not floor travel. A, B, and C misidentify the proposed stepping portion or treat both portions as stationary loops.

**2. B.** Tracing both loops with a hand while seated preserves the figure-eight pathway without uncomfortable pelvic movement. A substitutes a straight slide, C omits the reverse loop, and D marks rhythm without preserving the shape.

**3. A.** Comparing the dancer's floor position before and after the step provides evidence of travel. B and D keep the feet in place while changing the loop, and C shifts weight without necessarily traveling.

**4. C.** Slowly rehearsing counts 3–6 isolates the change from loop to step and lets the learner check support. A omits the step, B omits the preceding transition, and D repeats the whole phrase without isolating the error.

**Retake rule:** For a missed question, revisit the relevant count or pathway, mark the movement slowly, and retry. These questions assess introductory understanding, not professional competence or cultural authority.
