## Learning goal and explanation

Coordinate a comfortable arm pathway with a small torso wave, distinguishing introductory marking from advanced undulation technique. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** This phrase places arm opening on counts 3–4 and the optional gentle torso sequence on counts 5–6. These actions occur in sequence rather than simultaneously. A large backbend is unnecessary.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** Count 1: begin a gentle hand lift from waist level; 2: arrive at a comfortable chest-height position with relaxed shoulders. Count 3: begin opening the arms outward; 4: complete the soft opening without forcing height or reach. Count 5: optionally mark the upper/chest start of a tiny sequential torso intention; 6: mark its lower/pelvis continuation, or hand-trace that order instead. Count 7: begin lowering the arms; 8: settle at neutral with comfortable support. **These actions occur in sequence rather than simultaneously.** Practice the 3–4 to 5–6 boundary slowly; do not add an uncounted pause, and omit the torso motion entirely if uncomfortable. Hand tracing records pathway order, not demonstrated advanced undulation.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Stay seated and keep the hand lift on counts 1–2 and arm opening on 3–4; on 5–6 **keep the other arm resting comfortably** and trace the intended chest-to-pelvis sequence with one hand instead of performing the torso wave; on 7–8 lower the tracing hand and the other arm to neutral. **This substitutes a hand pathway for torso action; record the substitution rather than scoring a visible torso wave.**

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the eight-count phrase twice at a comfortable steady count. For each of two attempts, record **on time / off time / unobservable** for arm opening completed on 4, optional torso sequence **or labeled hand-traced substitute** begun on 5 without an extra beat, and return to neutral completed on 8 after lowering begins on 7. **If the optional action is omitted without substitution, label counts 5–6 'omitted by choice', not a completed torso sequence.** Identify one observed correction and one limitation; do not score a hand trace as performed torso isolation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Which counts introduce the optional small torso sequence?**

A. 1–2

B. 3–4

C. 5–6

D. 7–8

**2. The learner can open the arms but feels uncomfortable during the wave. What should they do?**

A. Omit the wave and trace its pathway with the hands, seated if useful

B. Continue the torso wave at a smaller range even though it remains uncomfortable

C. Replace the wave with a large standing pelvic circle that also causes discomfort

D. Omit the wave but also skip the planned count structure

**3. Why rehearse the arms and torso sequence separately?**

A. To make each component faster before combining them

B. To practice the arm opening alone without checking how it links to the wave

C. To preserve the final pose while changing the planned order of the components

D. To identify timing and pathway before coordinating the components

**4. Which conclusion is supported after repeating the eight-count drill?**

A. The dancer can repeat the arm pathway but has not attempted the optional torso wave

B. The dancer can describe and repeat this adapted original sequence

C. The dancer repeated a low-range wave once, which establishes readiness for every larger version

D. The written phrase matches the original source video's exact technique without checking the video

### Answer key and explanations

**1. C.** The optional small torso sequence is assigned to counts 5–6 after the earlier hand lift and arm opening. A and B identify earlier preparation counts, while D marks the closing portion.

**2. A.** Omit the uncomfortable wave and trace its order with the hands, seated if useful. B continues discomfort, C replaces it with another painful large motion, and D drops the count structure unnecessarily.

**3. D.** Practicing the arm and torso sequences separately identifies their timing and pathways before recombination. A emphasizes speed, B avoids checking the link, and C changes the intended order.

**4. B.** The learner can describe and repeat the adapted original sequence under observed conditions. A describes only the arms rather than the full adapted score, C infers readiness for larger variants, and D asserts unverified source-video matching.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
