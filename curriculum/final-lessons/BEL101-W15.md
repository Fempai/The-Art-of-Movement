## Learning goal and explanation

Coordinate a comfortable arm pathway with a small torso wave, distinguishing introductory marking from advanced undulation technique. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** Arm styling and undulation can be practiced separately before combining them; a large backbend is unnecessary.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** Counts 1–2 lift hands from waist to chest height with relaxed shoulders; 3–4 open arms softly; 5–6 mark a tiny chest-to-pelvis sequential wave only if comfortable; 7–8 lower arms and return to neutral.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Stay seated and omit the torso wave, using the hands to trace its intended pathway.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the adapted phrase twice at a steady count, identify one observable change, and explain one limitation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

### Answer key and explanations

**1. A.** The eight-count drill combines gentle arm placement with an optional small sequential torso wave; it does not demonstrate advanced undulation mastery.

**2. B.** A slow tempo lets the learner distinguish the arm opening on counts 3–4 from the optional torso sequence on counts 5–6.

**3. C.** Tracing the wave with the hands preserves the sequencing concept without requiring spinal motion or standing balance.

**4. D.** Separating the arm and torso actions allows controlled coordination; neither a large backbend nor a particular named technique is required for this original drill.
