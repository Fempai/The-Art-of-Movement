## Learning goal and explanation

Coordinate a comfortable arm pathway with a small torso wave, distinguishing introductory marking from advanced undulation technique. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** Arm styling and undulation can be practiced separately before combining them; a large backbend is unnecessary.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** Counts 1–2 lift hands from waist to chest height with relaxed shoulders; 3–4 open arms softly; 5–6 mark a tiny chest-to-pelvis sequential wave only if comfortable; 7–8 lower arms and return to neutral.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Stay seated and omit the torso wave, using the hands to trace its intended pathway.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the adapted phrase twice at a steady count, identify one observable change, and explain one limitation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Which counts introduce the optional small torso sequence?**

A. 1–2

B. 3–4

C. 5–6

D. 7–8

**2. The learner can open the arms but feels uncomfortable during the wave. What should they do?**

A. Omit the wave and trace its pathway with the hands, seated if useful

B. Continue the torso wave at a smaller range even though it remains uncomfortable

C. Replace the wave with a large standing pelvic circle that also causes discomfort

D. Omit the wave but also skip the planned count structure

**3. Why rehearse the arms and torso sequence separately?**

A. To make each component faster before combining them

B. To practice the arm opening alone without checking how it links to the wave

C. To preserve the final pose while changing the planned order of the components

D. To identify timing and pathway before coordinating the components

**4. Which conclusion is supported after repeating the eight-count drill?**

A. The dancer can repeat the arm pathway but has not attempted the optional torso wave

B. The dancer can describe and repeat this adapted original sequence

C. The dancer repeated a low-range wave once, which establishes readiness for every larger version

D. The written phrase matches the original source video's exact technique without checking the video

### Answer key and explanations

**1. C.** Counts 5–6 are designated for the optional chest-to-pelvis wave, following the hand lift and arm opening.

**2. A.** The hand trace retains the concept of sequential motion without requiring an uncomfortable torso movement.

**3. D.** Separate marking makes each pathway and count identifiable before the learner combines movements at a comfortable tempo.

**4. B.** Repeatability and description are appropriate beginner outcomes; advanced skill, universal safety, and cultural lineage are not demonstrated.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
