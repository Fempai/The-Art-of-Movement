# STG101 · Lesson 02 — Pushing Strength: Elevated Push-Up Mechanics

**Independent dance conditioning | 35–50 minutes.** This lesson is an authored learner-facing revision. Final citation, professional safety, and live-browser audits remain outstanding. Beginners should choose comfortable loads and stop for pain, dizziness, chest discomfort, or unusual shortness of breath. Seek qualified medical advice when symptoms warrant it.

## Learn: movement and training principle

A push-up challenges the arms and trunk while the body moves relative to the hands. Elevating the hands on a stable, immovable surface usually reduces the relative loading compared with a floor push-up, although the exact amount depends on geometry and individual technique. Wrist, elbow, and shoulder comfort determine the suitable version. 'Core engagement' should not mean breath-holding or rigidly flattening every natural spinal curve. A controlled push-up is more informative than an arbitrary high repetition count.

**Training principle:** Repetition quality and repeatable conditions matter more than a dramatic single attempt. A self-directed website cannot assess individual injury risk, prescribe rehabilitation, or certify technique from a self-reported score.

## Guided conditioning lab

**Warm-up and practice:** Use a fixed wall first. Place palms against it slightly wider than shoulders, step back until the position is comfortable, and keep feet planted. Bend elbows to bring chest toward the wall over two counts; push away over two counts. Keep head, ribs, and pelvis moving together without forcing a perfectly straight line. Perform 6–10 controlled repetitions, rest, and repeat only if comfortable. For progression, use a stable, nonmoving gym bench with appropriate height and no risk of sliding; do not use a rolling chair. Exhale naturally during the push, without straining.

**Observe and troubleshoot:** Check whether elbows flare abruptly, shoulders shrug excessively, or the trunk collapses. If wrists hurt, adjust hand height or stop rather than pushing through. Record surface height and reps so future comparisons use similar conditions.

**Transfer to dance:** Perform one eight-count Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover motif in a comfortable range. Ask whether your breathing, posture, and transitions remain controllable. Conditioning supports the phrase; the phrase is not a test of maximal strength.

**Ready for progression?** You can complete the chosen version without pain, loss of control, or compensatory rushing, and can explain exactly which training variable you would change. Do not progress solely because a numerical repetition target was met.

## Knowledge check

**1. What usually makes a wall push-up easier than a floor push-up?**

A. More jumping

B. Lower relative body loading

C. A different musical meter

D. No arm involvement

**2. What is a useful movement-quality observation?**

A. The performer looked determined

B. The song sounded dramatic

C. The trunk and pelvis moved together

D. The exercise proves professional strength

**3. What should you do if wrist pain persists?**

A. Increase repetitions

B. Ignore it

C. Move faster

D. Stop or change to a comfortable alternative

**4. Which is an appropriate beginner progression?**

A. Move from a wall to a secure lower support when ready

B. Use a rolling chair

C. Add a jump after each rep

D. Hold the breath throughout

### Answer key with explanations

**1. B.** Hand elevation generally reduces the proportion of body weight resisted.

**2. C.** Body alignment during the repetition is observable.

**3. D.** Pain is a reason to modify or stop, not to progress load.

**4. A.** Progression should preserve control and stable support.

**Self-review:** Correct answers test the stated training distinctions. For a missed question, explain why the chosen distractor is incorrect and repeat the relevant demonstration with a comfortable modification. These questions are not a validated health or fitness test.

## Independent assignment

Complete a personal baseline record: date, exercise variation, equipment/support, repetitions or work/rest duration, perceived effort (0–10), one visible quality cue, one modification, and one next-step decision. Write 200–300 words distinguishing observations from interpretations. For Lesson 04, submit a combined **personal baseline log** covering pulling, pushing, trunk control, and easy dance intervals, including any exercises not attempted and why.

**Worked example:** “I performed eight wall push-ups at an easy perceived effort of 4/10. My elbows moved comfortably, and I completed each repetition without stopping. Next time I will keep the wall distance unchanged and compare controlled repetitions; this record does not prove I can perform a floor push-up.”
