## Learning goal and explanation

Refine the contrast between a small figure-eight pelvis pathway and actual travel across the floor. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** The figure eight describes the pathway; traveling describes a change in floor location.

**Optional source-observation study (3–5 minutes, if accessible):** Watch the scheduled figure-eight/travel source for a clearly observable loop direction and whether the dancer changes position on the floor. Record what is visible, then compare the loop and travel components separately with this original exercise. Do not infer hidden weight transfers or a named regional lineage from the video title. If inaccessible or obscured, record **source not observed / technique match undetermined**.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** First hand-trace a sideways figure eight: counts 1–2 make a small right loop back toward center; counts 3–4 make a small left loop back toward center. If comfortable, repeat the same tiny intention through the pelvis with feet supported, avoiding forced rotation of planted knees. **Before count 5**, prepare a comfortable stance with enough support on the left to free the right foot. Count 5: take one small step right with the right foot and transfer weight only as comfortable; count 6: bring the left foot toward the right without crossing or twisting the planted knee, then settle into a stable stance. Use a fixed support if needed. Counts 7–8: settle in parallel at the **ending** floor position without deliberately stepping back. **If a safe rightward step actually changed your floor marks, this is the new position; if it did not, record no travel rather than claiming displacement.** Mark a starting and ending floor reference if safe, so you can distinguish actual travel from an in-place weight shift. Practice the loop and step separately before linking counts 3–6.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Sit in a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported. Hand-trace the **right loop on counts 1–2** and the **left loop on counts 3–4**, returning toward center after each loop. On **5**, gesture rightward with one hand to indicate the *intended* direction of a step; on **6**, bring the other hand toward it to symbolize the following foot. On **7–8**, settle the hands comfortably while staying in the same seat. Label counts 5–6 **symbolic travel only**: moving a hand sideways does not change the body's floor location. Do not claim a seated gesture demonstrates a standing weight transfer or actual travel.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** For each of two attempts, score the **right loop over counts 1–2**, **left loop over 3–4**, **rightward step beginning on 5**, **following-foot arrival on 6**, and **stable new position on 7–8** as **on count / off count / unobservable / intentionally omitted**. Separately compare the body's marked start and end floor positions: report **travel observed** only when a standing attempt visibly ends at a different floor position, **no travel observed** when the position is visibly unchanged, or **unobservable** when the floor positions cannot be judged. For seated work report **symbolic travel only**, not observed floor travel. Do not infer travel from a lateral pelvis pathway or hand gesture. Compare attempts only where support conditions and task match; explain one limitation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. A learner traces a figure eight with planted feet. What changes when they travel on counts 5–6?**

A. The same two loops become larger, but floor position remains unchanged

B. A step is introduced; actual travel is confirmed only if their floor position changes

C. The pelvis changes direction while the feet remain planted

D. The loops are repeated without a change of support or floor position

**2. The learner's knees twist during the stationary pathway. What should they try?**

A. Keep the same range and move faster to reduce time spent in each direction

B. Transfer weight farther sideways while maintaining the same twisting pathway

C. Reduce range and return to comfortable knee alignment

D. Repeat the same pathway with the knees pointing inward

**3. Which adaptation retains the directional study without requiring standing travel?**

A. Trace the pathway with one hand while seated

B. Tap alternating feet while seated without tracing the loops

C. Trace a straight horizontal line with one hand while seated

D. Mark only one loop of the eight while omitting the return

**4. What can an observer conclude if a rightward step on counts 5–6 leaves the dancer at a new marked floor position?**

A. The dancer completed the optional step and therefore maintained the figure-eight pathway during counts 1–4

B. The dancer changed floor location and therefore kept the same support conditions as the stationary version

C. The dancer's displacement confirms the written step occurred, so it also confirms a match to the planned video's movement

D. The dancer changed floor location during that count

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Counts 5–6 introduce a step, but **actual travel is established only when the starting and ending floor marks confirm displacement**. A step in place alone would not establish travel, unlike a confirmed change of floor position; the first four counts study a stationary figure-eight pathway. A, C, and D all keep the feet or floor position stationary, so they do not demonstrate travel.

**2. C.** Reduce the loop size and restore comfortable knee alignment if twisting occurs. A speeds up the same problem, B increases the sideways shift, and D repeats the inward knee position. Stop if discomfort continues.

**3. A.** A seated hand trace preserves both loops of the directional figure-eight study. B practices only alternating rhythm, C replaces loops with a straight line, and D omits the returning loop.

**4. D.** Comparing starting and ending floor marks establishes a change of floor location during the step; it does not establish other properties of the phrase. A infers the quality of the earlier stationary loops from later displacement, B assumes unchanged support despite a position-changing step, and C treats evidence of travel as evidence of correspondence with an unverified source video.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
