# LAB101 · Lesson 01 — Establish the Undercurrent Motif

**Original repertory | 40–55 minutes | independent learner.** This is an authored lesson revision, not a certified professional performance evaluation. Source auditing and browser testing remain pending.

## Objective and explanation

Perform and describe the lesson's movement material, identify at least one specific timing or support error, correct it using a written score, and explain an artistic choice without treating intention as universally observable.

A repertory study is not just a sequence to memorize. It combines a repeatable movement score, transitions, musical choices, and interpretive decisions. *Undercurrent* is an original teaching repertory, not a reconstruction of a named community tradition. Its first motif is **step → gather → reach → recover**. Each action must have an observable start, duration, and endpoint. The motif can suggest different meanings to different viewers, so record intended meaning separately from movement facts. The first technical goal is not extreme range: it is reliable weight support and a controlled diagonal reach.

## Follow-along studio

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area. Warm up with gentle weight shifts, comfortable shoulder circles, and small arm reaches. Keep the movement within your own pain-free range. Use seated marking when balance or standing is uncomfortable; do not attempt falls, inversions, or unsupported acrobatics.

**Guided rehearsal (15 minutes):** Face front with both feet comfortably grounded. Count 1: step right, transferring weight gently to the right foot. Count 2: bring left foot toward right without forcing contact, leaving enough support for balance. Counts 3–4: draw both hands toward the sternum while keeping shoulders comfortable. Counts 5–6: reach the right arm diagonally forward and upward without lifting the shoulder toward the ear. Count 7: return the arm toward center. Count 8: settle in a balanced stance. Repeat four times; on the final repetition use the left arm instead. Seated option: shift torso pressure gently right on 1–2, gather hands 3–4, reach 5–6, recover 7–8.

**Solo feedback (5 minutes):** If count 5 starts before the gather finishes, rehearse counts 3–6 alone. If the reach destabilizes you, reduce its height and keep both feet grounded. If you cannot repeat the phrase twice with the same counts, slow your spoken count rather than adding difficulty.

**Memory and expressive check:** First perform while reading the score, then from memory, then compare with the score. Write two factual observations (for example, 'I reached on count 6 rather than 5') and one artistic intention (for example, 'I wanted the delay to suggest anticipation'). Do not confuse the two.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is the original Undercurrent motif?**

A. Step–turn–reach–recover

B. Step–gather–reach–recover

C. Step–gather–turn–recover

D. Step–gather–reach–travel

**2. On which counts does the initial diagonal reach occur?**

A. 1–2, immediately after the first step

B. 3–4, during the hand-gathering action

C. 5–6

D. 7–8, during the recovery and finish

**3. What is the first measure of reliable execution?**

A. Repeatable counts and controlled support

B. Repeatable counts with a larger reach but unstable support

C. A single expressive performance without a repeatable count sequence

D. A successful final pose even if the preceding weight transfers vary

**4. Which statement describes the work accurately?**

A. A reconstruction of a traditional dance because the phrase uses eight counts

B. A historical ballet variation because it includes a diagonal reach

C. An original teaching repertory

D. A standardized training protocol because it includes a written rubric

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. B.** The study begins with four clearly defined actions.

**2. C.** The reach follows the gather on counts 3–4.

**3. A.** Repetition with stable support precedes stylistic complexity.

**4. C.** Undercurrent is original course choreography, not an authenticated historical dance.

**Self-correction:** Explain any missed answer in your own words and repeat the associated movement chunk at a slower count. These questions check conceptual understanding, not externally validated technique.

## Repertory assignment

Submit a written count sheet and, if you choose, a private practice recording. Document starting and ending facing, supporting foot or seated support, the movement on each count, and one deliberate revision. For Lesson 04, submit the **complete 32-count performance draft** and a 300–400-word reflection on memory, timing, expressive intent, and remaining limitations.

**Worked example:** “I placed my right foot on count 1, gathered my hands over 3–4, and reached over 5–6. On the first attempt I began the reach on 4. I isolated counts 3–6 and corrected the overlap. My intention was anticipation, but the recording can only establish timing and visible focus.”

**Rubric:** Reproducible counts 30%; safe, controlled transitions 25%; legible compositional choices 25%; critical self-observation and revision 20%. Self-scoring does not certify readiness for advanced physical skills.
