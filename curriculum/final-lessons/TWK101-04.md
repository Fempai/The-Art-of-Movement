# TWK101 · Lesson 04 — Lateral Weight Shifts and Pelvic Direction

**Standalone twerk foundations | learner-facing written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** This is not a verified instructor-led video class.

## Objective
Shift support side-to-side without confusing weight transfer with a full hip circle.

## Cultural and technique context
Modern twerking is strongly associated with Black social dance and New Orleans bounce culture. Similar-looking actions across traditions should not be labeled identical. This is an original teaching exercise, not a named historical step. Historical sourcing and practitioner review are still pending.

## Follow-along practice
Warm up with gentle side steps and small pulses. Counts 1–2: shift weight slightly right and return center; 3–4: shift left and return; 5–6: repeat right with a small comfortable pelvic orientation change; 7–8: repeat left. Keep both feet available for support and do not twist the planted knee. Practice four rounds, pause, then alternate one shift-only phrase and one shift-plus-orientation phrase for 10–15 minutes.

## Accessible adaptation
Use a sturdy chair and mark side-to-side sitting pressure; the lateral timing is comparable, but standing load transfer and pelvic orientation are not mechanically identical.

## Self-correction and safety
Twisting knee: point knee along toes and decrease rotation. Losing balance: use counter support. Turning it into a circle: return to center between each side. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness or repeated loss of balance.

## Apply and reflect
Write two eight-count scores distinguishing support shift from pelvic orientation.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Which action changes the supporting side without requiring a full hip circle?**

A. Alternating small lateral weight shifts

B. Maximal spinal extension

C. A shoulder roll only

D. A deep jump

**Key: A.** A shift transfers support laterally.

**2. Why return to center between shifts?**

A. To create continuous circling

B. To distinguish discrete side-to-side shifts

C. To increase twisting

D. To stop listening to rhythm

**Key: B.** The return clarifies each directional action.

**3. How should the knee behave during a lateral shift?**

A. Twist against the planted foot

B. Lock in extension

C. Track comfortably with the foot direction

D. Rotate independently of the foot

**Key: C.** Avoid twisting through a planted knee.

**4. What is different in a seated adaptation?**

A. No rhythmic timing remains

B. The move becomes a jump

C. It is identical to standing

D. Sitting pressure replaces standing load transfer

**Key: D.** Seated support changes the mechanics.

## Completion checkpoint
Perform or notate two comfortable eight-count phrases; identify the movement initiator, one correction, and an adaptation. A written response does not prove physical proficiency.
