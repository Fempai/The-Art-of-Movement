# LMA201 · Lesson 02 — Effort: Weight, Time, Space, and Flow

**Laban Movement Analysis | independent learner | 40–55 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; passage-level source checks and browser acceptance are outstanding. These lessons introduce descriptive tools; they do not confer LMA certification.

## Learn the analytical distinction

After this lesson, you should identify the featured category, describe a visible movement cue, contrast two performances of the same phrase, and avoid inferring internal psychological states from movement alone.

In LMA, **Effort** addresses dynamic qualities of movement, often described through four factors: **Weight** (light/strong), **Time** (sustained/sudden), **Space** (indirect/direct), and **Flow** (free/bound). These terms are analytic qualities, not measurements of kilograms, seconds, or the literal dimensions of a room. A movement may appear light without requiring low muscle activity; 'strong' is not an instruction to force a joint. A direct spatial intention can be maintained through a curved body pathway, depending on the analysis, so beginner descriptions should focus on what is observable and avoid overclaiming. Flow describes the degree of ongoing versus contained movement, not the absence of muscular control.

## Guided movement-observation lab

**Warm-up (5 minutes):** Gently shift weight, mobilize shoulders within a comfortable range, and establish a clear front-facing direction. A seated version is acceptable for all arm and torso comparisons.

**Follow along (15 minutes):** Use the same comfortable eight-count step–gather–reach–recover motif. Trial 1: counts 5–6 extend the arm with a sustained, light quality. Trial 2: begin the reach suddenly on count 5, then hold through 6 without jerking. Trial 3: keep a steady count but make the reach more direct toward one chosen point. Trial 4: let the arm travel in a gentle exploratory pathway before arriving. After each trial, write one dynamic term and one visible cue. For bound/free exploration, compare a reach that deliberately stops at count 6 with a reach that continues softly into recovery; avoid uncontrolled flinging.

**Observe and correct (5 minutes):** Do not treat sudden as maximally fast or violent. Do not confuse 'Weight' with actual dumbbell resistance. If a variation causes pain or loss of control, reduce amplitude and practice with hands only.

**Transfer to Undercurrent:** Perform the original step–gather–reach–recover motif on counts 1–8. Change only the category featured in this lesson. Write the before/after score and describe what remains constant. A viewer's emotional interpretation is a hypothesis, not an observation.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is an Effort factor?**

A. Shape, because the body becomes wider during the phrase

B. Weight

C. Facing, because the dancer turns toward a diagonal

D. Travel, because the dancer crosses the floor

**2. In Effort language, what does sustained describe?**

A. A timing quality

B. A phrase that must occupy at least eight counts

C. The amount of physical load lifted by the dancer

D. A movement that necessarily travels through a straight spatial path

**3. What does bound/free describe?**

A. The suddenness or gradualness of the movement's timing

B. The amount of muscular force required by the movement

C. A quality of Flow

D. The size of the space reached around the body

**4. Which is an unsafe interpretation of strong Effort?**

A. A dynamic quality of intent

B. A description of movement

C. A quality to analyze

D. An instruction to use maximal physical resistance regardless of control

### Correct answers and reasoning

**1. B.** Weight is one of four Effort factors.

**2. A.** Sustained contrasts with sudden in the Time factor.

**3. C.** Bound and free are Flow qualities.

**4. D.** Strong Effort does not require unsafe exertion.

**Self-review:** If you miss an item, state which category the distractor actually describes. Repeat the relevant movement with a smaller range if needed. The questions are original conceptual checks, not a standardized LMA competency examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit a written score with count, body initiation, dynamic quality, shape change, and spatial destination where relevant. Provide two factual observations, one artistic intention, and a 200–300-word explanation of how you distinguished them. For Lesson 04, submit a **four-variation score** using Body, Effort, Shape, and Space separately, plus a 300-word comparative reflection.

**Worked example:** “On count 5 the fingertips initiated the reach while the torso remained nearly stationary (Body). In the next version, the reach began suddenly (Effort/Time). Both arrived at the same front-right diagonal (Space). I intended urgency, but cannot infer that every observer perceived urgency.”

**Rubric:** Correct category identification 30%; observable, reproducible movement description 30%; controlled contrast and safe execution 20%; reflective distinction between observation and interpretation 20%. Do not score on emotional conformity or extreme range.
