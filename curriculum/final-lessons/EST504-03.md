# EST504 · Lesson 03 — Ethics: Attribution, Cultural Context, and Critical Power

**Dance Criticism & Aesthetics | graduate-level independent study | 50–70 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly citation audit, expert editorial review, and live-browser testing are outstanding.

## Critical framework

**Outcomes:** Distinguish description, interpretation, and evaluation; cite movement-specific evidence; recognize alternative readings; and critique the work without unsupported assumptions about a performer.

Dance criticism involves ethical decisions about whose work is named, whose context is represented, and how judgments are framed. A critic can analyze a performance without assuming that a movement belongs exclusively to one person or that a community's dance has a single fixed meaning. Distinguish documented lineage from resemblance, and distinguish a critique of a compositional choice from an unsupported judgment about a performer's body, identity, or motives. When writing about dance traditions outside your own experience, identify the limits of your knowledge and seek accounts by practitioners and scholars from those traditions. Attribution is not satisfied by a vague statement that a dance is 'inspired by culture.'

## Guided performance-analysis studio

**Preparation:** Clear a safe area and mark a comfortable version of Undercurrent's step–gather–reach–recover motif. If performing is unsuitable, analyze an existing self-created score instead.

**Observe and compare (20–25 minutes):** Draft a 250-word review of a self-created Undercurrent study, identifying the original choreographic material and any external sources used. Write two versions of a sentence: one unhelpful ('the dancer is lazy because the reach is small') and one evidence-based ('the reach's small amplitude reduced its visibility from the recorded camera position'). Add a provenance table with columns for movement source, what you can verify, what remains uncertain, and attribution language. If discussing a specific cultural practice, do not invent origins; mark research gaps and identify practitioner-led sources to consult.

**Revision and ethical check:** Avoid inferring physical ability, identity, or character from appearance. Avoid presenting your own movement as an authenticated example of a cultural tradition without research. When evidence is limited, say so rather than filling gaps.

**Evidence protocol:** Separate your notes into three headings: (1) what can be observed, with counts or timestamps; (2) what you infer and why; and (3) how you evaluate the choice under a stated criterion. A recording does not provide access to private emotions or universal audience reactions.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which critique is ethically stronger?**

A. The performer lacks expressive ability because the reach is obscured by the camera

B. The recorded reach is hard to see from this camera angle

C. The dancer's physical appearance makes the phrase unsuitable for the genre

D. The lowered gaze establishes that the performer privately felt ashamed

**2. How should uncertain cultural lineage be handled?**

A. Assign the phrase to the most familiar tradition based on visual resemblance

B. Treat a shared movement shape as proof of one common cultural origin

C. Mark uncertainty and seek practitioner-informed evidence

D. Describe the phrase as historically authentic without citing a practitioner or source

**3. What is an appropriate object of critique?**

A. A visible compositional choice and its effects

B. A dancer's private motivation inferred from one facial expression

C. A performer's identity treated as sufficient evidence that the staging failed

D. A medical diagnosis inferred from the visible quality of a reach

**4. Which is insufficient attribution?**

A. Naming a well-known dancer without showing a link to the borrowed phrase

B. Citing a general interview that does not discuss the movement or tradition being claimed

C. Referring to an unspecified traditional source without a traceable reference

D. Saying only 'inspired by culture' without identifying the tradition or source

### Answer key and rationale

**1. B.** It addresses an observable staging effect, not personal worth.

**2. C.** Transparency and relevant sources are necessary.

**3. A.** Criticism should be grounded in the work and evidence.

**4. D.** Vague attribution obscures actual lineage.

**Self-correction:** For missed items, rewrite one unsupported assertion as a situated, evidence-supported claim. These questions are instructional, not a validated critical-writing examination.

## Independent critical assignment

Produce a 300–450-word analysis containing a thesis, at least four movement-specific observations, an alternative reading, and a source-limit statement. For Lesson 04, submit a **1,000–1,300-word critical essay** comparing narrative and abstract versions of the 32-count Undercurrent phrase, with six count-specific observations, a clear thesis, relevant verified scholarship, ethical attribution, and a conclusion identifying the limits of your interpretation.

**Worked example:** “The diagonal reach lasts two counts in A but three in A′. I read the added duration as a moment of uncertainty, although another viewer could interpret it as resolve. The timing contrast is observable; the emotion is my interpretation.”

**Rubric:** Accuracy and specificity of movement description 30%; reasoning and evidence 30%; engagement with alternatives and context 20%; ethical attribution, structure, and source transparency 20%. No credit for fabricated citations or presumed performer psychology.
