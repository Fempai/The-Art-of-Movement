# MUS101 · Lesson 02 — Subdivision: Eighth Notes and the 'And' Counts

**Self-study course | 35–50 minutes | learner-facing revision, final source/link/browser acceptance pending.** No musical instrument, teacher, or ballet barre is required. You may clap or tap while seated.

## Learning objectives

Define the key concept accurately, demonstrate it using a steady count, analyze an original eight-count phrase, distinguish direct timing observations from interpretations, and explain your answers to four knowledge questions.

## Lesson: read and reason

A **subdivision** divides a beat into smaller timing units. In a simple equal division of a quarter-note beat, two eighth notes each last half a beat. Dancers often speak '1-and-2-and-3-and-4-and' to locate eighth-note subdivisions in 4/4. The word **and** is a verbal marker for the midpoint between consecutive beats in this exercise. It is not the only way music subdivides: triplets divide a beat into three, and swung rhythms may use unequal durations. Do not assume every piece of music can be captured accurately by straight 'and' counts. The key skill is hearing whether a movement lands on a beat or between beats.

**Worked calculation and example:** At 80 beats per minute, a quarter-note beat lasts 60/80 = 0.75 seconds. An evenly divided eighth-note subdivision lasts 0.375 seconds. The beat rate stays at 80 even though there are two taps per beat. Doubling the number of events is not the same as doubling the underlying tempo.

**Common misunderstanding:** A common counting error is to say '1 and 2 and' at twice the original beat rate without preserving the steady main pulse. Keep one hand tapping the quarter-note beats while the other adds the offbeats.

## Follow-along rhythm laboratory

**Preparation:** Find a quiet space, sit or stand comfortably, and tap a steady beat for 30 seconds. You can use your own spoken count; no copyrighted recording is required. Do not force breathing or physical range. If stepping causes discomfort, remain seated and tap hands or feet gently.

**Guided practice (10–15 minutes):** Start with a steady slow pulse, clap on '1 2 3 4'. Without speeding up the main beats, say '1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and' and tap your thigh on each 'and'. Then count eight beats while alternating small right and left steps on numbered beats 1–8; clap on each intervening 'and' after counts 1–7. After count 8, either finish the phrase or mark the 'and' after 8 as a pickup into the next cycle. Keep the numbered beat steady. If stepping and clapping together is confusing, remain seated and use separate hands for beats and offbeats. **Seated count-for-count version:** Sit on a stable nonrolling chair with feet supported. Tap numbered beats 1–8 with the right hand at a constant pulse and tap the left hand halfway between numbered beats on 1-and through 7-and. Mark 8-and only if immediately continuing into the next eight-count cycle; otherwise stop after beat 8. Slow the pulse without changing the two-equal-parts relationship. Hands can be reversed for comfort.

**Troubleshooting:** If you lose the pulse, stop adding accents and tap only the main beat. If the subdivisions become confusing, count aloud with one hand marking beats and the other marking 'ands'. If a movement transition is unstable, keep both feet on the ground and mark the phrase with your hands. Correct timing is not a reason to ignore pain or dizziness.

**Transfer to Undercurrent:** Keep the step–gather–reach–recover motif, then change exactly one rhythmic feature: where the accent lands, how many beats the reach occupies, or where an offbeat gesture appears. Write the original and revised counts side by side. Do not assume that a musical count dictates an emotional meaning.

## Knowledge check — answer before looking below

**1. In a straight eighth-note division, how many equal parts are in one quarter-note beat?**

A. Three equal parts, as in a triplet subdivision

B. Two

C. Four equal parts, as in sixteenth-note subdivisions

D. One part, because the eighth note has the same duration as a quarter note

**2. At 80 BPM, how long is one evenly divided eighth note?**

A. 0.75 s, the duration of the full quarter-note beat

B. 1.5 s, doubling rather than halving the beat duration

C. 0.375 s

D. 0.125 s, dividing the beat into six equal parts

**3. What does 'and' mark in this introductory counting system?**

A. The next numbered beat after the current beat

B. The final subdivision of a four-beat measure

C. The midpoint between consecutive beats

D. A note that must be accented regardless of the music

**4. Which statement is accurate about swung rhythms?**

A. They preserve the straight eighth-note midpoint in every performance

B. They may use unequal subdivision timing

C. They require the dancer to move at twice the written tempo

D. They replace the recurring pulse with a completely unmetered pattern

### Correct answers with reasoning

**1. B.** In straight eighth-note subdivision, one quarter-note beat divides into two equal eighth notes. A describes three triplet parts, C describes four sixteenth-note parts, and D incorrectly treats an eighth note as a full quarter-note beat.

**2. C.** At 80 quarter-note BPM, one beat lasts 60/80 = 0.75 seconds; a straight eighth note lasts half, or 0.375 seconds. A is the full beat, B doubles it, and D incorrectly divides it into six parts.

**3. C.** In this straight-eighth counting exercise, 'and' names the midpoint between adjacent numbered beats. A is the next numbered beat, B wrongly confines the subdivision to a measure's end, and D assumes every 'and' must be accented.

**4. B.** Swung eighths can be performed with unequal timing rather than the exact straight midpoint. A insists on equal division, C invents a required tempo doubling, and D incorrectly removes the recurring pulse. Actual swing ratios can vary by tempo and style.

**Self-assessment:** Four correct demonstrates comprehension of the concepts sampled here. If you miss an item, work through the arithmetic or clapping example again and retry. These questions are instructor-authored and not psychometrically validated.

## Independent assignment

**Deliverable:** Create a 32-count score in four eight-count phrases with an unchanged quarter-note pulse. Label numbered beats 1–32 and the intervening straight eighth-note 'ands'. Include **8-and, 16-and, and 24-and only when a subdivision actually leads into the next phrase**; do not insert an automatic extra event at each boundary. **32-and is outside the required 32-beat score** unless you explicitly notate a pickup into a subsequent cycle. Place two or more hand accents on 'ands', keeping the numbered foot or seated-hand pulse steady. Score one phrase without subdivisions and a comparable phrase with subdivisions; state the chosen BPM and the calculated duration of one half-beat. Include 250–350 words explaining the rhythmic revision, a verifiable timing observation, and an interpretation.

**Example:** “At 80 quarter-note beats per minute, each beat lasts 0.75 seconds and the straight 'and' occurs 0.375 seconds after its numbered beat. I added a hand tap on the 'and' after count 2 while keeping my numbered-beat foot taps unchanged. The added event does not make the tempo 160 BPM.”
