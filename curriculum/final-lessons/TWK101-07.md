# TWK101 · Lesson 07 — Four-Pattern Transitions

**Standalone twerk foundations | learner-facing written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** This is not a verified instructor-led video class.

## Objective
Connect pulse, tilt, shift and accent changes without losing the eight-count framework.

## Cultural and technique context
Modern twerking is strongly associated with Black social dance and New Orleans bounce culture. Similar-looking actions across traditions should not be labeled identical. This is an original teaching exercise, not a named historical step. Historical sourcing and practitioner review are still pending.

## Follow-along practice
Warm up with walking, small pelvic rocks, and supported side shifts. Phrase A: counts 1–2 small leg-led pulse; 3–4 small pelvic tilt and return; 5–6 shift right and center; 7–8 shift left and center. Repeat twice. Phrase B uses the same pathway but places an intentional musical accent on count 5. Practice each phrase twice, then alternate A–B–A–B with pauses as needed for 10–15 minutes.

## Accessible adaptation
Use seated pelvic rocking and pressure shifts, omitting standing weight transfer. Mark the accents with hands if pelvic movement is uncomfortable; describe the substitutions.

## Self-correction and safety
Forgetting sequence: rehearse two counts at a time. Losing alignment: reduce range. Forcing pelvic movement: stop and return to comfortable neutral. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness or repeated loss of balance.

## Apply and reflect
Record or notate A–B–A–B and name the four distinct action types.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Which action occupies counts 3–4 in Phrase A?**

A. Small pelvic tilt and return

B. A floor drop

C. A spin

D. A shoulder-only clap

**Key: A.** The second two-count unit is a pelvic tilt.

**2. How does Phrase B differ from Phrase A?**

A. It changes every action

B. It emphasizes count 5 while retaining the pathway

C. It removes the lateral shifts

D. It doubles the speed

**Key: B.** Phrase B changes the accent.

**3. Which strategy helps if the sequence is forgotten?**

A. Perform at maximum speed

B. Add a fifth pattern

C. Rehearse two counts at a time

D. Skip the return positions

**Key: C.** Chunking the phrase reduces complexity.

**4. What must the learner distinguish in the reflection?**

A. Only song preference

B. Only repetition count

C. Only hand position

D. Pulse, tilt, shift and accent

**Key: D.** The four action categories form the phrase.

## Completion checkpoint
Perform or notate two comfortable eight-count phrases; identify the movement initiator, one correction, and an adaptation. A written response does not prove physical proficiency.
