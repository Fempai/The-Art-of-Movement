## What you will learn

Define the featured movement; identify the supporting leg, working leg, and relevant joint actions; reproduce a small-range phrase with counts; identify and correct common beginner errors; explain how musical timing changes the effect.

## Lesson: read before practicing

Weight transfer is the deliberate movement of support from one foot to another. Développé means 'developed': the working leg moves through a bent position and unfolds into an extension. In ballet, the exact pathway depends on the exercise and position; here we use a small, supported forward version. Unlike a tendu, the working foot leaves the floor. Unlike a high kick, the movement is gradual and controlled. A supported développé does not require a barre if a fixed support is available, and a seated or very small-range marking alternative can teach the sequence when standing balance is limited.

**Why this matters:** In a self-taught setting, a name alone is not instruction. You need to know which foot supports your weight, what pathway the working limb takes, and what would count as a repeatable action. A shape that resembles a photograph is not proof that you used the intended movement. A slower tempo often helps you observe transitions, but it is not automatically the correct performance tempo for every style.

**Preparation:** Clear a nonslip space and use a stable fixed support for one hand if needed. Start in parallel or comfortable natural turnout. Do not use a chair that rolls or slides. Keep the extension low enough to avoid pain or trunk strain. Begin with 3–5 minutes of comfortable marching, ankle circles, and small knee bends. Do not force range, and stop if you experience pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

## Follow-along movement instruction

- **Count 1: Shift weight to the left foot.**
- **Count 2: Touch the right toes lightly beside the left foot.**
- **Count 3: Lift the right knee a few inches, keeping the standing knee comfortable.**
- **Count 4: Pause and check balance.**
- **Count 5: Extend the right lower leg forward only a small distance.**
- **Count 6: Bend the right knee again.**
- **Count 7: Lower the right foot gently to the floor.**
- **Count 8: Return weight to both feet and settle.**

**Seated eight-count adaptation:** Sit on a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported. Count 1 shift sitting pressure slightly left; 2 lightly draw the right toes beside the left foot; 3 lift the right knee a small comfortable amount or mark the lift with a hand; 4 pause; 5 unfold the right lower leg a small distance if comfortable (or trace the unfolding with a hand); 6 re-bend; 7 return the foot to the floor; 8 settle with even seated support. This preserves the bend–unfold–bend–return order but **does not demonstrate standing weight transfer or ballet développé technique**. For the assignment's second phrase, mark 9–11 with a short right-foot slide and return, 12–14 with a comfortable seated knee-bend intention or hand-marked rhythm, and 15–16 with a small seated heel lift and return; record these as substitutes rather than standing plié or relevé.

**Repeat the phrase:** Perform it three times at a comfortable pace. On the first repetition, say the counts. On the second, attend to foot contact and support. On the third, make the phrasing intentional without increasing the range.

**Correct-versus-misleading comparison:** A développé is not defined by maximum leg height. If the pelvis tips dramatically and the knee locks during the unfolding, reduce the extension. The learning goal is a coherent bend–unfold–bend–return sequence with an intentional weight transfer.

**Transfer to Undercurrent:** Use the original eight-count step–gather–reach–recover motif. Replace the gather or transition with a small version of today's movement, then explain whether the change affects timing, support, or the audience's visual focus. You are composing an original study, not copying choreography from a source.

## Troubleshooting when practicing alone

| What you notice | What might be happening | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| You begin lifting before weight is on the standing foot | The support transfer may be incomplete | Rehearse counts 1–2 without lifting |
| Your lower back arches during extension | The range may exceed current active control | Keep the knee bent or extend only a few inches |
| You lose balance on count 5 | The combination may be too complex | Use fixed support and practice the unfold while seated |

**Success check:** You can identify the left supporting foot before the right leg lifts, demonstrate the bent-knee lift, small unfolding, re-bend, and return twice at the same counts, and explain why a controlled pathway matters more than height. This does not certify injury safety or advanced technique.

## Knowledge check — try before reading the answers

**1. What distinguishes a développé from a tendu in this lesson?**

A. The working foot slides along the floor and returns without lifting

B. The working leg lifts and unfolds after a bent position

C. The working leg extends directly from standing without first folding

D. The working knee stays bent throughout the entire movement

**2. What should happen before the right leg lifts?**

A. The right working foot takes more weight before it lifts

B. The pelvis tips backward to raise the working knee

C. Weight shifts to the left supporting foot

D. The right foot slides farther forward before the left leg supports the weight

**3. Which is the most useful modification for a beginner?**

A. A small supported extension

B. A higher leg extension with the same loss of balance

C. A supported tendu that omits the bend–unfold sequence

D. A bent-knee hold without attempting the controlled unfolding

**4. What is the best evidence of learning?**

A. A higher leg lift that changes the sequence each repetition

B. A repeatable final pose without evidence of the unfolding pathway

C. A smooth extension that begins before weight reaches the support foot

D. A repeatable bend–unfold–return sequence with intentional weight transfer

### Answers and explanations

**1. B.** The taught développé lifts and unfolds the working leg after a bent position. A describes a floor slide, C omits the preparatory bend, and D never unfolds the knee.

**2. C.** Transferring weight to the left supporting foot frees the right leg to lift. A loads the working foot, B substitutes pelvic tipping for support preparation, and D delays the necessary transfer while extending the working foot.

**3. A.** A small supported extension retains the bend–unfold–return sequence without requiring high elevation. B preserves loss of balance, C substitutes a tendu without unfolding, and D stops before the defining extension. Use comfortable support and range.

**4. D.** A repeatable bend–unfold–return sequence with deliberate weight transfer demonstrates the taught coordination in these attempts. A changes the sequence for height, B judges only an endpoint, and C lifts before support is established. This alone does not prove long-term learning; delayed practice would provide stronger evidence.

## Assignment and worked example

**Task:** Compose a 16-count barre-free phrase. Counts 1–8 use the taught weight-transfer and supported développé (shift left 1, right-toe touch 2, small knee lift 3, pause 4, unfold 5, refold 6, lower 7, settle 8). For counts 9–16, use a comfortable supported synthesis: small right tendu 9–10, return 11, shallow demi-plié 12–13, straighten 14, low supported relevé 15, lower and settle 16. Maintain stable support and adapt the phrase to a smaller range if necessary. Annotate counts, support, direction, and modifications. Write 250–350 words explaining your movement choices, what you observed, what you could not verify, and one source-supported principle relevant to the exercise.

**Worked example:** “I transferred my weight to the left foot before lifting the right knee. On count five I gently unfolded the right lower leg, then bent it again before returning the foot to the floor. In my second eight-count phrase I added a small tendu, demi-plié, and supported relevé. I reduced the extension when I noticed my trunk leaning backward.”

**Rubric:** Terminology and accuracy 25%; counts and movement description 25%; analysis and revision 25%; appropriate source use and limitations 25%. The rubric evaluates academic reasoning separately from technical skill.
