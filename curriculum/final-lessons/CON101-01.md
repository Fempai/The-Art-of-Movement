# CON101 · Lesson 01 — Breath and Phrasing

**Self-directed contemporary dance | 35–50 minutes including movement, retrieval, and reflection.** No instructor or barre required for the foundational version. Advanced floorwork, inversions, falls, and painful movements require qualified instruction. Live browser acceptance and claim-level source auditing are still pending.

## Learning goal and core explanation

After this lesson, explain the concept, demonstrate a controlled low-risk version, compare two intentional choices, and document a movement phrase.

Breath can be used as an expressive timing cue: a dancer may coordinate an inhale with expansion or an exhale with a gathering movement. That is a compositional choice, not a universal law that inhalation must always accompany rising. Breathing and movement interact, but one breathing pattern does not automatically make a phrase safer or more expressive. Contemporary dance is not a single technique; different teachers and lineages emphasize different relationships among breath, gravity, and initiation.

**Think like a choreographer:** What do you actually observe—timing, direction, weight, contact points—and what are you inferring, such as the dancer's emotion or intention? A strong interpretation states its evidence and acknowledges alternatives.

## Follow-along studio instruction

**Warm up for 3–5 minutes:** Walk or march gently, move shoulders and ankles through comfortable ranges, and practice an easy weight shift. Clear the floor, avoid slippery socks or moving furniture, and keep a fixed support available if needed. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided eight-count study:** Seated upright on a stable surface or standing with both feet grounded, inhale comfortably over counts 1–2 while allowing the arms to open; exhale naturally over counts 3–4 while gathering the arms; on counts 5–6 repeat the reach with a different direction; on 7–8 recover and rest. Repeat once with a slower arm opening while maintaining easy, unforced breathing. Do not hold your breath or try to synchronize every inhale to a fixed number of seconds.

**Independent practice:** Perform the sequence once while speaking counts, once while observing the initiation or support pathway, and once with a deliberate change of timing. If a version becomes unstable, reduce the range or use the seated alternative.

**Undercurrent application:** In Undercurrent, the reach on counts 5–6 can feel suspended if the arms travel gradually, but the audience cannot know the dancer's breathing pattern from appearance alone. Use the original step–gather–reach–recover motif, then change only one variable associated with this lesson. Write down the eight counts and describe the difference without claiming a universal audience reaction.

## Troubleshooting without a teacher

| What happens | Possible explanation, not diagnosis | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| You feel lightheaded | Breathing may be forced or excessively controlled | Stop and resume ordinary breathing; rest before practicing again |
| You lose the counts while concentrating on breath | Two simultaneous tasks may exceed your current attention | Mark the arm phrase first, then let breathing remain comfortable |
| The movement feels mechanical | You may be copying an inhale–reach rule too literally | Change the phrasing and allow breath to remain natural |

**Ready to move on when:** You can repeat the comfortable version twice at a steady count, name the supporting surface or foot, and explain one deliberate choreographic change. This is a learning checkpoint, not medical clearance.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is the strongest statement about breath and phrasing?**

A. One breathing pattern is required for all dance styles

B. Breath can be used as a compositional timing cue

C. Every exhale proves relaxation

D. Breath always determines the audience's emotion

**2. What should you do if breath counting makes you dizzy?**

A. Keep going until the phrase ends

B. Hold the breath longer

C. Stop and return to ordinary breathing

D. Increase movement speed

**3. Which is directly observable in a video?**

A. A change in arm timing

B. The dancer's precise lung volume

C. The dancer's intended emotion

D. The dancer's fitness diagnosis

**4. What is a valid modification?**

A. Perform the arm phrase seated with comfortable breathing

B. Force the deepest inhale possible

C. Hold breath through eight counts

D. Increase range until breath becomes difficult

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Breath can inform phrasing, but its expressive use varies by dancer and choreography.

**2. C.** Dizziness is a reason to stop and recover, not a challenge to push through.

**3. A.** Arm timing is visible; the other conclusions cannot be established from the image alone.

**4. A.** Seated marking preserves timing and expressive choices without requiring standing balance.

**Self-grading:** Four correct indicates understanding of the introductory distinctions tested here. For a missed answer, revisit its explanation and demonstrate the related idea at a smaller range before retrying. These original questions are not externally validated examinations.

## Independent assignment

Create two eight-count versions of the same phrase. Record the count, initiating segment, direction, support, and intended effect. Write 250–350 words comparing the versions, identifying one observable difference, one interpretation, and one limitation. Include at least one relevant reference and explain what that source can and cannot support.

**Worked example:** “In version A, I reached for two counts and returned immediately. In version B, I held the reach for one additional count. The duration changed, which I can verify by counting; whether that pause suggests hesitation is an interpretation.”

**Rubric:** Concept and terminology 25%; accurate movement score 25%; comparison and critical reasoning 25%; source use and revision 25%. Written grades do not establish the safety or quality of unsupervised advanced technique.
