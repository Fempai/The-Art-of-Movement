# MUS101 · Lesson 04 — Tempo, Phrase, and a 32-Count Dance Score

**Self-study course | 35–50 minutes | learner-facing revision, final source/link/browser acceptance pending.** No musical instrument, teacher, or ballet barre is required. You may clap or tap while seated.

## Learning objectives

Define the key concept accurately, demonstrate it using a steady count, analyze an original eight-count phrase, distinguish direct timing observations from interpretations, and explain your answers to four knowledge questions.

## Lesson: read and reason

**Tempo** is the rate of the beat, commonly expressed in beats per minute (BPM). **Phrase** describes a meaningful grouping of musical or movement events; a phrase may align with eight counts, but it need not. A choreographer can vary speed, accent, and movement density without changing the underlying tempo. For example, performing a reach over four beats rather than two changes its duration, not necessarily the musical BPM. A **movement score** is a written record of what happens and when. For self-study, a precise score should identify counts, weight transfers, facings, levels, and accents so that you can reproduce the sequence rather than rely on memory.

**Worked calculation and example:** At 96 BPM, 32 beats last 32×60/96 = 20 seconds. If you perform the same score at 120 BPM, it lasts 16 seconds, assuming one count per beat. If you instead hold one reach for four beats rather than two while keeping 96 BPM, you have altered choreography within the tempo, not the tempo itself.

**Common misunderstanding:** Do not call every 32-count phrase four musical measures. In 4/4 it is eight measures if each dance count is one quarter-note beat. Also avoid assuming that a 32-count score proves musical expression; the score must be practiced against a pulse and revised based on observation.

## Follow-along rhythm laboratory

**Preparation:** Find a quiet space, sit or stand comfortably, and tap a steady beat for 30 seconds. You can use your own spoken count; no copyrighted recording is required. Do not force breathing or physical range. If stepping causes discomfort, remain seated and tap hands or feet gently.

**Guided practice (10–15 minutes):** Warm up with comfortable walking and gentle arm movements. Use a steady beat at a self-chosen comfortable pace. Phrase A (counts 1–8): 1 step right, 2 settle, 3 gather arms, 4 pause, 5 reach up, 6 sustain, 7 return, 8 hold. Phrase B (9–16): 9 step left, 10 settle, 11–12 slow arm circle, 13 turn a small comfortable diagonal, 14 pause, 15–16 recover. Phrase A-prime (17–24): repeat A but place a small hand accent on the 'and' after 20; maintain the main beat. Phrase C (25–32): 25–26 shift weight right, 27–28 shift left, 29 reach forward, 30 gather, 31 lower arms, 32 finish. Do not turn beyond a comfortable range. If standing is uncomfortable, perform all counts seated with torso and arm gestures.

**Troubleshooting:** If you lose the pulse, stop adding accents and tap only the main beat. If the subdivisions become confusing, count aloud with one hand marking beats and the other marking 'ands'. If a movement transition is unstable, keep both feet on the ground and mark the phrase with your hands. Correct timing is not a reason to ignore pain or dizziness.

**Transfer to Undercurrent:** Keep the step–gather–reach–recover motif, then change exactly one rhythmic feature: where the accent lands, how many beats the reach occupies, or where an offbeat gesture appears. Write the original and revised counts side by side. Do not assume that a musical count dictates an emotional meaning.

## Knowledge check — answer before looking below

**1. At 96 BPM, how long do 32 quarter-note beats last?**

A. 32 seconds, assuming one second per beat

B. 16 seconds, treating 96 BPM as 120 BPM

C. 20 seconds

D. 24 seconds, treating the beat duration as 0.75 seconds

**2. In 4/4, 32 quarter-note beats span how many measures?**

A. Four measures, treating each measure as eight beats

B. Eight

C. Two measures, treating each measure as sixteen beats

D. Sixteen measures, treating each measure as two beats

**3. If a reach lasts four beats instead of two at unchanged BPM, what changed?**

A. The movement's duration

B. The tempo, even though the BPM is unchanged

C. The count of beats per measure, even though the meter is unchanged

D. The direction of the reach, even when its pathway stays identical

**4. Which information makes a movement score more reproducible?**

A. A tempo marking without specifying which foot supports the movement

B. A final pose and a mood word without the transitions

C. Counts, support foot, facing, and accents

D. Counts and music title without the dancer's facing or weight shifts

### Correct answers with reasoning

**1. C.** At 96 quarter-note beats per minute, each beat lasts 60/96 = 0.625 seconds; 32 beats therefore take 32 × 0.625 = 20 seconds. The other choices assume different beat lengths or tempos.

**2. B.** Four beats per measure means 32/4 = eight measures.

**3. A.** The movement's duration changed while the underlying tempo may remain fixed.

**4. C.** A detailed count-based score communicates sequence and spatial choices.

**Self-assessment:** Four correct demonstrates comprehension of the concepts sampled here. If you miss an item, work through the arithmetic or clapping example again and retry. These questions are instructor-authored and not psychometrically validated.

## Independent assignment

**Deliverable:** Create a 32-count movement-and-rhythm score. Mark beats, any subdivisions, weight shifts or seated equivalents, and at least two intentional accents. Include a 250–350-word explanation of one rhythmic revision and one claim you can verify from counts versus one artistic interpretation.

**Example:** “At 120 BPM, my eight-count phrase takes four seconds. I moved the hand accent from beat three to the 'and' after beat two without changing the beat rate. The new emphasis is observable in the score; whether it sounds surprising depends on the musical context.”

**Rubric:** Accurate terminology 25%; correct counts/calculations 25%; reproducible movement score 25%; critical comparison and source awareness 25%. Written accuracy and movement execution are separate criteria. A website cannot certify performance quality from a self-report.
