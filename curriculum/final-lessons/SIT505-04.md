# SIT505 · Lesson 04 — Cultural Context, Permissions, and a Site-Specific Proposal

**Site-Specific & Cultural Performance | graduate-level independent study | 45–65 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Source verification, site-specific safety review, and browser testing remain outstanding. No public-space access or public performance is required.

## Lecture: site-responsive reasoning

**Learning goals:** Describe a site constraint, transform a movement choice in response, document access and permission limits, and distinguish verified context from artistic speculation.

A site carries social histories and relationships beyond its geometry. A memorial, place of worship, neighborhood gathering space, or culturally significant location may require particular consultation and permission even when an artist can physically enter it. Site-responsive work should not treat a community as a visual backdrop without considering representation, consent, and attribution. Distinguish documented history from a choreographer's interpretation; do not invent a site's cultural meaning. A rigorous proposal includes location rationale, site map, access and safety checks, permission status, artistic score, and a plan to revise in response to stakeholder feedback.

## Guided site-design laboratory

**Prepare:** Use only a private or explicitly permitted, level, unobstructed practice area. Never block exits, rehearse in traffic, or test uninspected elevated surfaces.

**Investigate and choreograph (20–25 minutes):** Draft a proposal for a private, permitted room or another site whose access you can verify. Include a two-paragraph site rationale and a scaled or clearly labeled schematic showing performance zone, audience positions, entry/exit, and obstacles. Create a 32-count Undercurrent score with A (1–8) oriented to the entrance, B (9–16) toward a side boundary, A′ (17–24) with an altered reach direction, and C (25–32) returning focus to the entry. Add an accessibility checklist, a permission log (granted/pending/not applicable), a contextual source ledger, and a contingency plan for noise, glare, or obstruction. No public performance is required.

**Troubleshoot:** If historical context is uncertain, identify sources to verify rather than making a claim. If permission is pending, present the design as a proposal only. If any route is obstructed, revise the layout before rehearsing.

**Undercurrent transfer:** Write a count-level step–gather–reach–recover score with the site's relevant orientation and a safer seated or reduced-travel alternative. Document which design choices respond to the site and which remain artistic preferences.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is a key ethical question about a culturally significant site?**

A. Whether the site's visual appearance supports the costume palette, without examining its history

B. Whose history and permissions are relevant to the work

C. Whether the floor allows a leap, without asking who controls or uses the space

D. Whether a reflective wall can amplify the movement, without checking site permissions

**2. What should be included in a site-specific proposal?**

A. A compelling concept statement without access or permission information

B. A movement score and soundtrack without a map or site-use status

C. Map, access plan, permission status, and movement score

D. A promotional image and projected audience reaction without logistics

**3. What is the correct treatment of unverified site history?**

A. Mark it uncertain and identify sources for verification

B. Choose the most frequently repeated local story and state it as verified

C. Infer the site's cultural significance from its architecture alone

D. Attribute the site to a tradition based on visual resemblance without sources

**4. What does pending permission allow?**

A. A scheduled rehearsal on site while the authorization request is pending

B. Entering the site for filming because a proposal has been drafted

C. Announcing the performance as approved because the request was submitted

D. Proposal development without assuming performance access

### Correct answers and explanations

**1. B.** Planning at a culturally significant site requires examining whose histories, permissions, and relationships the work engages. Costume aesthetics alone (A), floor suitability alone (C), and reflective staging alone (D) ignore those ethical and access considerations.

**2. C.** A site-specific proposal needs a map, access plan, current permission status, and executable movement score. A concept statement without logistics (A), a score without site-use information (B), or promotional imagery without practical planning (D) is incomplete.

**3. A.** Label uncertain site history and identify the records or informed people needed to verify it. Repeating an unverified story (B), inferring history from architecture (C), or assigning a tradition from resemblance (D) would present speculation as evidence.

**4. D.** A pending request permits continued proposal development, not entry or performance under an ungranted authorization. Rehearsing while approval is pending (A), filming because a draft exists (B), and advertising approval before it is granted (C) misrepresent permission status.

**Feedback cycle:** For each missed question, distinguish a verified condition, a design hypothesis, and an unsupported assumption. These are original concept checks, not a formal accessibility or permitting assessment.

## Independent assignment

Submit a labeled site sketch, a movement score, a constraints-and-decisions table, and a 250–350-word reflection. Identify at least two uncertainties and how you would verify them. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a **site-specific proposal** including artistic rationale, 32-count score, site and audience map, accessibility and safety plan, permission status, cultural-context source ledger, and contingency plan.

**Worked example:** “The room's side window creates glare toward the front-facing performer. I rotated the reach toward the interior wall on counts 5–6 and kept the gather unchanged. This is a design response to observed light, not proof that every spectator will prefer it.”

**Rubric:** Site-responsive movement design 25%; safe and legible spatial plan 25%; accessibility and permissions transparency 30%; cultural-context research and reflective justification 20%. No points for unverified claims of legal compliance.
