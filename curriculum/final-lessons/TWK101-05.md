# TWK101 · Lesson 05 — Supported Positions and Accessible Variations

**Standalone twerk foundations | learner-facing written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** This is not a verified instructor-led video class.

## Objective
Choose a stance or support that maintains control while explaining differences among variations.

## Cultural and technique context
Modern twerking is strongly associated with Black social dance and New Orleans bounce culture. Similar-looking actions across traditions should not be labeled identical. This is an original teaching exercise, not a named historical step. Historical sourcing and practitioner review are still pending.

## Follow-along practice
Warm up four minutes with walking and comfortable hip hinges. Practice eight counts in upright stance: four shallow pulses and four side shifts. Repeat in a small hip hinge with hands lightly supported on thighs, keeping the back comfortable and knees tracking over feet. Return upright between rounds. Practice 10–15 minutes with breaks. Never require deep squat or prolonged wrist loading.

## Accessible adaptation
Use a counter for light hand support or a sturdy chair for seated pulse marking. Seated pressure shifts retain rhythm but cannot reproduce standing squat or hip-hinge forces.

## Self-correction and safety
Back strain: reduce hinge or stand upright. Hands bearing body weight: raise torso and use counter support. Breath held: pause and exhale. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness or repeated loss of balance.

## Apply and reflect
Select two versions and explain which movements and loading demands change.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. What is the purpose of moving from upright to a small supported hinge?**

A. Compare control and loading demands

B. Force the deepest squat

C. Test wrist endurance

D. Maximize spinal extension

**Key: A.** The two variations offer different support demands.

**2. Which position should be avoided if it strains the back?**

A. Comfortable upright stance

B. A forced deep hip hinge

C. A small supported pulse

D. A rested neutral position

**Key: B.** A forced hinge is not required.

**3. What should you do if hands bear excessive weight on the thighs?**

A. Push harder

B. Hold the breath

C. Raise the torso and use lighter support

D. Move to an unstable chair

**Key: C.** Reduce hand loading and use stable support.

**4. Which reflection demonstrates understanding?**

A. Claim all variations are identical

B. Measure only repetitions

C. Ignore discomfort

D. Describe how support and loading change

**Key: D.** The assignment compares mechanical differences.

## Completion checkpoint
Perform or notate two comfortable eight-count phrases; identify the movement initiator, one correction, and an adaptation. A written response does not prove physical proficiency.
