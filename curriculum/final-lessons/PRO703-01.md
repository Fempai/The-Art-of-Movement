# PRO703 · Lesson 01 — Revision Dossier: From First Draft to Defensible Choices

**Performance Portfolio & Capstone | independent final-year study | 60–90 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly audit, expert review, and live-browser acceptance testing remain outstanding. This is a nonaccredited self-study program.

## Capstone seminar

**Learning outcomes:** Produce a traceable creative decision, connect an artistic claim to movement evidence, document ethical attribution, and evaluate growth without inventing external validation.

A capstone portfolio should show how artistic choices changed, not just display a polished outcome. A revision dossier preserves a baseline score, identifies an observable problem, records a specific intervention, and evaluates the result against the original criterion. This is different from rewriting a retrospective narrative that makes every choice appear inevitable. Revision can be compositional (reordering a motif), technical (stabilizing a transition), or interpretive (changing focus). Each category requires different evidence. An independent dancer can evaluate visible changes but should not claim professional external validation without obtaining it.

## Guided portfolio studio

**Prepare:** Use a clear, level, permitted space and a comfortable movement range. No dangerous inversions, lifts, or forced stretching are required.

**Create and document (30–40 minutes):** Use the 32-count Undercurrent A–B–A′–C score. Define four review criteria: phrase order, reach onset, transition control, and legible contrast between A and A′. Mark or perform the piece once at comfortable intensity, recording errors or uncertainty by count. Choose the weakest criterion. Example: if A′ resembles A too closely, sustain the reach on counts 21–23 and adjust recovery on 24, documenting the change. Rehearse the altered eight counts twice, then perform the full 32-count score again. Preserve both versions and label the date and evidence type.

**Troubleshooting and integrity:** If a revision makes a transition unstable, reduce travel or restore the original. If footage omits a body part, score that criterion as unobservable. Never erase an unsuccessful trial from the process record.

**Evidence discipline:** Label every artifact as planned, rehearsed, recorded, or performed; identify dates and criteria. Distinguish an observed improvement from an interpretation or a claim about long-term learning.

## Knowledge check

**1. What belongs in a revision dossier?**

A. Baseline, criterion, intervention, and observed result

B. The final recording without a dated baseline or a stated success criterion

C. The planned intervention without a record of what happened after applying it

D. The baseline and result without identifying the movement variable changed

**2. Which is a compositional revision?**

A. Moving the camera to a new angle while keeping the phrase's movement order unchanged

B. Reordering or extending a motif under a stated rule

C. Increasing the rehearsal tempo without specifying a structural change to the motif

D. Repeating the original sequence with a different costume but no movement revision

**3. How should unobservable movement be scored?**

A. Use the written score to mark the unseen action as executed correctly

B. Assign a technical error even when the camera does not show the relevant limb

C. As unobservable with a note

D. Infer improvement from the performer's stated confidence despite missing footage

**4. What should happen to an unsuccessful trial?**

A. Keep only the successful take so the process record shows continuous improvement

B. Change the recorded success criterion after seeing the unsuccessful attempt

C. Relabel the failed trial as an external adjudication without an actual reviewer

D. Keep it in the dated process record

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. A.** The dossier makes the creative process auditable.

**2. B.** Composition concerns how movement material is organized.

**3. C.** Evidence limits must remain explicit.

**4. D.** Honest revision includes attempts that did not work.

**Self-review:** Correct any missed concept in a sentence that names the evidence and its limitation. These are original knowledge checks, not an external examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit the lesson's specified artifact, a 250–350-word explanation of one artistic decision, and a dated revision record. **Lesson 04 final capstone:** a **2–4 minute original work** with a complete count score, dated draft and final versions, rehearsal journal, artist statement, staging plan, critical reflection, permissions and attribution notes, and either a private performance recording or a clearly labeled unperformed score.

**Worked example:** “My first draft returned to center on count 8, obscuring the planned stillness. In the final score I moved the return to 7 and kept 8 still. Two recorded trials show the revised count placement, though they do not establish universal audience response.”

**Rubric:** Original choreographic structure and executable score 25%; documented revision and rehearsal process 25%; evidence-based critique and academic integrity 25%; safe presentation, attribution, and professional communication 25%. The score is a self-assessment, not accreditation.
