# BEL101 · Friday Week 04 — Snake Arms and Chest Circles

**Original planned video:** Upper-body Snake Arms / Chest Circles. The exact user-supplied private Google Drive URL is not recovered; no video demonstration or playback has been verified. This is an original beginner preparation, not a transcription or authentication of that video.

**Duration:** 35–45 minutes. **Prerequisites:** Comfortable upright or seated posture, easy breathing, and the ability to move shoulders without pain. Do not force chest or spinal range.

## Learning outcomes

Describe the difference between a sequential arm wave and a small upper-torso circle. Mark each movement for eight counts, explain which segments initiate and follow, and document one safe adaptation. Distinguish observable motion from an unsupported claim about the cultural origin of a technique.

## Guided follow-along practice

**Warm-up (5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, then make small comfortable shoulder rolls and open and close the arms at chest height. Keep the neck relaxed, breathe normally, and use a chair if balance is uncertain.

**Snake-arm preparation (8 minutes):** Begin with hands low and shoulders relaxed. Counts 1–2: initiate a small lift through the right elbow, allowing the wrist and hand to follow rather than raising the shoulder toward the ear. Counts 3–4: lower the right elbow, wrist, and hand sequentially. Counts 5–8: repeat with the left arm. Practice slowly before attempting a continuous alternating arm wave. Observe whether the hand follows the elbow; if it moves first, slow down and restart. This is a simplified coordination drill, not a definitive description of all snake-arm techniques.

**Chest-circle preparation (8 minutes):** Keep feet comfortably parallel or sit upright. Imagine the breastbone tracing a tiny clock face while the pelvis stays comparatively quiet: counts 1–2 move the upper torso gently right, 3–4 slightly forward, 5–6 left, 7–8 return to neutral through a small backward intention without forcing a backbend. Repeat in the opposite direction. The pathway can be a very small oval. Do not hold the breath, thrust the ribs, or compress the lower back.

**Combine (5 minutes):** Counts 1–4 mark one slow right-arm wave; 5–8 trace a small upper-torso circle. Repeat with left arm and reverse circle. Pause between components before trying a continuous phrase.

## Modifications and self-correction

- **Seated version:** Sit with both feet supported; trace the elbow-to-hand wave and a tiny breastbone oval without leaning far beyond the chair's support.
- **Shoulder discomfort:** Keep the elbows below shoulder height, reduce the pathway, or omit the arm wave.
- **Back discomfort:** Omit the backward component and mark the oval with the hands instead.
- **Coordination breakdown:** Practice the arm and torso separately for four counts each; count aloud and recombine only when repeatable.
- **Stop:** Pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of balance warrants stopping the action. No exercise is guaranteed safe for everyone.

**Success check:** Repeat an adapted eight-count phrase twice and identify which segment starts each movement. Do not use range, speed, or an elaborate performance as the criterion.

**3. Which modification is appropriate if the back feels compressed?**

A. Increase the backward range

B. Hold the breath

**4. Which claim is justified by this lesson alone?**

A. Every regional dance tradition teaches identical snake arms

B. The original video has been verified

C. The exercise certifies technique mastery

D. The student can observe whether the hand follows the elbow

### Answers and explanations

**1. A.** A small elbow lift followed by wrist and hand — the sequential relationship is the target of this original coordination drill.

**2. B.** A small upper-torso oval within a comfortable range — the exercise emphasizes pathway awareness, not extreme mobility.

**3. C.** Omit the backward motion and trace the oval with the hands — the pathway can be studied without spinal extension.

**4. D.** The student can observe whether the hand follows the elbow — observation does not establish lineage, source verification, or expert competence.
