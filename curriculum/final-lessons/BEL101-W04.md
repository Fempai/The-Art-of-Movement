# BEL101 · Friday Week 04 — Snake Arms and Chest Circles

**Original planned video:** Upper-body Snake Arms / Chest Circles. Video playback and technique matching have not been independently verified. This is an original beginner preparation, not a transcription or authentication of that video.

**Duration:** 35–45 minutes. **Prerequisites:** Comfortable upright or seated posture, easy breathing, and the ability to move shoulders without pain. Do not force chest or spinal range.

## Learning outcomes

Describe the difference between a sequential arm wave and a small upper-torso circle. Mark each movement for eight counts, explain which segments initiate and follow, and document one safe adaptation. Distinguish observable motion from an unsupported claim about the cultural origin of a technique.

## Guided follow-along practice

**Warm-up (5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, then make small comfortable shoulder rolls and open and close the arms at chest height. Keep the neck relaxed, breathe normally, and use a chair if balance is uncertain.

**Snake-arm preparation (8 minutes):** Begin with hands low and shoulders relaxed. Counts 1–2: initiate a small lift through the right elbow, allowing the wrist and hand to follow rather than raising the shoulder toward the ear. Counts 3–4: lower the right elbow, wrist, and hand sequentially. Counts 5–6: lift the **left elbow first**, allowing its wrist and hand to follow; counts 7–8: lower the left elbow, wrist, and hand in sequence. The left side uses the same two-count rise and two-count lowering as the right; **do not add an extra preparation count between arms**. Practice slowly before attempting a continuous alternating arm wave. Observe whether the hand follows the elbow; if it moves first, slow down and restart. This is a simplified coordination drill, not a definitive description of all snake-arm techniques.

**Chest-circle preparation (8 minutes):** Keep feet comfortably parallel or sit upright. Imagine the breastbone tracing a tiny clock face while the pelvis stays comparatively quiet: counts 1–2 move the upper torso gently right, 3–4 slightly forward, 5–6 left, 7–8 return to neutral through a small backward intention without forcing a backbend. For a **left-start mirrored pathway** (not a strict reversal of the original traversal), use counts 1–2 gently left, 3–4 slightly forward, 5–6 right, and 7–8 return toward neutral without forcing a backward bend. **To rehearse the actual reverse order of the original four directional marks, trace neutral/backward intention → left → forward → right slowly with a hand first; omit any uncomfortable torso component.** Name the directions from your own body's perspective, not a viewer's. Start with four stationary directional marks before attempting to link them into a tiny oval; omit any segment that causes discomfort. The pathway can be a very small oval. Do not hold the breath, thrust the ribs, or compress the lower back.

**Combine (5 minutes):** This is a **new eight-count combination**, not the two full eight-count studies performed simultaneously. On counts **1–4**, mark the right-arm wave (elbow-led rise on 1–2, sequential lowering on 3–4). On counts **5–8**, use **one count per torso directional mark**: right on 5, gently forward on 6, left on 7, and return toward neutral on 8 without forcing a backward bend. This **compresses the separately rehearsed eight-count torso study into four counts**; rehearse its four directions slowly before attempting the combination, and keep the full eight-count version separate if the faster mapping is not comfortable. Repeat with a left-arm wave on 1–4 and the **left-start mirrored** torso pathway (left 5, forward 6, right 7, neutral 8); label it mirrored rather than time-reversed. A seated hand-traced torso pathway is a coordination adaptation, not evidence of chest isolation. Pause between components during rehearsal only; for a connected attempt, do not insert an extra beat at 4→5.

## Modifications and self-correction

- **Seated version:** Sit in a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported. For the arm phrase, mark right elbow-led rise on 1–2, right lowering on 3–4, left rise on 5–6, and left lowering on 7–8. **As a separate eight-count trial**, mark a tiny breastbone oval right 1–2, forward 3–4, left 5–6, and neutral 7–8; hand-trace any uncomfortable chest component. Keep the torso within the chair's stable support. A hand-traced substitute records directional knowledge, not demonstrated chest isolation.
- **Shoulder discomfort:** Keep the elbows below shoulder height, reduce the pathway, or omit the arm wave.
- **Back discomfort:** Omit the backward component and mark the oval with the hands instead.
- **Coordination breakdown:** Practice the arm and torso separately for four counts each; count aloud and recombine only when repeatable.
- **Stop:** Pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of balance warrants stopping the action. No exercise is guaranteed safe for everyone.

**Success check:** Repeat the eight-count arm phrase twice: right elbow-led rise on 1–2, right sequential lowering on 3–4, left elbow-led rise on 5–6, and left lowering on 7–8. Record each segment as **on count / off count / unobservable** and note any seated or reduced-range adaptation. Separately assess the eight-count chest-circle phrase; **do not treat both distinct eight-count studies as one eight-count performance**. Identify which segment initiates each action. Do not use range, speed, or an elaborate performance as the criterion.

## Knowledge check — try before answers

**1. In the snake-arm drill, which sequence best matches the intended wave?**

A. Wrist lifts first, followed by the elbow and hand

B. Elbow and hand rise together as one rigid unit

C. Elbow lifts gently, followed by wrist and hand

D. Hand lifts first, then the wrist and elbow follow

**2. The chest oval causes uncomfortable back compression. What is an appropriate adjustment?**

A. Omit the backward portion and trace the oval with the hands

B. Keep the same uncomfortable oval but trace it more slowly

C. Make the oval narrower but retain the same uncomfortable backward segment

D. Repeat the chest oval at the same range with the hands held still

**3. A learner's hand moves before the elbow. What should they practice?**

A. Practice the hand pathway first and allow the elbow to follow afterward

B. Repeat the whole arm wave at the same speed without separating the segments

C. Keep the elbow fixed and repeat only wrist-and-hand motion

D. Slow elbow-first sequencing with a relaxed wrist

**4. What can be concluded after a learner repeats the wave accurately?**

A. They repeated the sequence, which confirms the source video's precise choreography

B. They reproduced this original coordination sequence

C. They performed a small arm wave, so the same movement order applies to all named regional styles

D. They reproduced one wave at a chosen tempo, which establishes proficiency at every tempo

### Answers and explanations

**1. C.** The intended sequence begins with a gentle elbow lift, followed by wrist and hand. A and D initiate with the wrist or hand, reversing the taught order, while B moves the elbow and hand together rather than sequentially. This is an original introductory drill, not a universal regional technique.

**2. A.** Omitting the backward torso portion and tracing the oval with the hands retains the directional study without continuing uncomfortable back compression. B and C preserve the painful component, while D repeats the same range without addressing discomfort. Stop if discomfort persists.

**3. D.** Slow elbow-first sequencing with a relaxed wrist targets the observed hand-first error. A repeats the incorrect order, B avoids isolating the error, and C fixes the elbow rather than practicing its initiating role.

**4. B.** Repeating the original coordination sequence demonstrates that specific practiced action. A claims unverified video correspondence, C generalizes across diverse named regional styles, and D assumes control at every tempo from one practiced speed.

**Retake rule:** For a missed question, revisit the relevant count or pathway, mark the movement slowly, and retry. These questions assess introductory understanding, not professional competence or cultural authority.

## Independent assignment

Write an eight-count score for the arm wave and a separate eight-count score for the chest circle. **Then score the connected eight-count combination**: arm wave on 1–4, compressed four-direction torso pathway on 5–8, with no added beat at 4→5. If you use the left-start mirrored version, identify its changed directional order; if you cannot safely compress the torso pathway, submit the two separate studies and mark the connected combination as **not yet attempted**, not completed. In 150–250 words compare their initiation points, direction, rhythm, and support. Describe one correction and one modification. Explain why your original drill cannot be presented as a verified reproduction of the planned video or a historically authenticated movement.
