# PER201 · Lesson 02 — Intention: Three Readings of One Phrase

**Performance & Storytelling | 40–55 minutes | independent learner.** This is an authored lesson revision, not a professionally adjudicated performance assessment. Bibliographic and live-browser audits remain outstanding.

## Learn: the performance principle

By the end, identify a visible expressive variable, perform two contrasting versions of a phrase, and critique a recording without confusing observable facts with presumed audience emotions.

Intention is the performer or choreographer's chosen purpose for a movement. It is not an objective emotional label attached to a particular step. To explore intention rigorously, keep the choreography stable while changing one or two expressive variables. For example, a reach might be approached as an invitation, a question, or a boundary; those are prompts for decisions about timing, distance, and recovery, not universal gesture dictionaries. You can document whether a hand moved slowly or stopped sharply. You cannot infer that all viewers perceived the intended story.

## Guided performance studio

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area and choose a safe front-facing reference point. Warm up with easy steps, gentle arm reaches, and comfortable head movements. Seated or small-range marking is acceptable; no leaps or forced movements are required.

**Perform (15 minutes):** Use the same eight-count step–gather–reach–recover motif for three trials. **Invitation:** 1–2 step gently, 3–4 gather, 5–6 extend the hand palm open at comfortable height, 7–8 soften the return. **Question:** keep the counts but pause fractionally at the start of 5 before reaching; arrive by 6 and hold gaze toward the hand. **Boundary:** keep the footwork, reach to a shorter distance on 5, pause on 6, and return decisively on 7–8. Speak counts steadily; any hesitation must fit within the beat rather than adding an unscored count. **Supported seated option:** On a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported, substitute a small rightward seated-pressure shift on 1 and settle on 2; keep the gather on 3–4 and the three arm/gaze variations on 5–8 exactly as scored. A seated pressure shift is not equivalent to a standing step. **Comparison control:** Keep the same starting facing, overall eight-count duration, and comfortable arm-height limit; record any additional changed variables rather than attributing all visible differences to intention. Record the movement variables, then separately name the intended readings.

**Observe and revise (5 minutes):** If the versions feel identical, alter only the duration or stopping point and compare. If a performance prompt feels emotionally uncomfortable, choose neutral intentions such as approach, investigate, and withdraw. You do not need to relive personal distress to perform expressively.

**Undercurrent application:** Record the count, facing, gaze, pathway, and expressive intention separately. Ask what actually changed between versions. If using video, preserve the same camera position and note what falls outside the frame.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is intention in this lesson?**

A. A fixed emotion that every viewer must perceive in the gesture

B. A chosen purpose guiding movement decisions

C. A psychological state proven by the movement's final pose

D. The measurable distance reached by the arm, independent of artistic purpose

**2. Which variable changes in the 'boundary' trial?**

A. The duration of the full phrase, even though the same count structure is retained

B. The direction of travel, even though the phrase stays in place

C. Reach distance and recovery quality

D. The movement's genre, even though the same original motif is used

**3. Which is an observable note?**

A. The arm stopped at a shorter distance on count 6

B. The shorter reach made every viewer feel rejected

C. The short reach proves the performer has a guarded personality

D. The movement's ending pose establishes one meaning in every culture

**4. What should you do if an emotional prompt feels uncomfortable?**

A. Continue using the distressing memory but reduce only the movement size

B. Keep the same uncomfortable emotional prompt and shorten the phrase

C. Repeat the prompt until the discomfort disappears before modifying it

D. Use a neutral action-based intention

### Answers and rationales

**1. B.** Intention is a selected purpose that guides how the performer shapes the action. It does not require every viewer to perceive one emotion (A), prove a private mental state from a pose (C), or reduce purpose to measurable reach distance (D).

**2. C.** The boundary trial changes reach distance and recovery quality while retaining the original motif and counts. Full-phrase duration (A), travel direction (B), and genre (D) are not the deliberately changed variables in this comparison.

**3. A.** An arm stopping at a shorter distance on count 6 is an observable movement description. Universal audience rejection (B), a personality inference (C), and a claim of identical meaning across cultures (D) exceed what the movement alone demonstrates.

**4. D.** A neutral action-based intention preserves the expressive task without requiring uncomfortable personal recall. Continuing the same distressing prompt with smaller movement (A), a shorter phrase (B), or repeated exposure until discomfort fades (C) does not respect the learner's choice.

**Self-review:** For any missed question, label the alternatives as observation, interpretation, or unsupported generalization. This knowledge check is original and not a validated audience-response measure.

## Independent assignment

Perform and score the same eight-count motif three ways: invitation (open-palm reach 5–6, softened return 7–8), question (brief hesitation within count 5, arrival by 6, gaze toward the hand), and boundary (shorter reach on 5, pause 6, decisive return 7–8). Keep the footwork and overall eight-beat duration consistent. Record at least three observable contrasts in reach distance, timing, gaze, or recovery; label intended readings separately from viewer evidence. Revise one expressive variable and submit a 250–350-word critique; do not add an unscored beat.

**Worked example:** “I kept the same eight-count footwork. For invitation I opened the palm during the reach on 5–6 and softened the return on 7–8. For question I hesitated briefly within count 5 but still arrived by 6. For boundary I shortened the reach on 5, paused on 6, and returned decisively on 7–8. These actions are visible; the three emotional readings are my intentions, not proven audience responses.”

**Rubric:** Observable specificity 30%; reproducible performance choices 25%; clear distinction between intention and interpretation 25%; justified revision and reflection 20%. A self-score does not establish professional stage readiness.
