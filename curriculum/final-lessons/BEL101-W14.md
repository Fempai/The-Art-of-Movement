## Learning goal and explanation

Compose two connected eight-count phrases using familiar foundation actions and a deliberate transition. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** A good transition preserves support and timing while making the change of movement legible.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** **Phrase A:** 1 slide right, 2 return center, 3 slide left, 4 return center; 5–6 mark a small comfortable circle (hand-trace first if needed); 7–8 settle in a stable stance. **Phrase B:** 1 right gentle knee pulse, 2 left, 3 right, 4 left; 5–6 open the arms gradually at a comfortable height while the feet remain supported; 7–8 lower the arms and return to neutral. First practice A and B separately, pausing between them **only during rehearsal**. For the connected 16-count attempt, finish A's count 8 (**global count 8**) and start B's count 1 (**global count 9**) on the very next beat **without an extra pause or skipped count**. **Map B counts 1–8 to global counts 9–16; specifically, the boundary A7–A8–B1–B2 equals global counts 7–10.** If that transition is uncertain, rehearse A7–A8–B1–B2 slowly before attempting all 16 counts. Seated marking is an acceptable adaptation, but label it as such.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Sit in a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported. For **Phrase A (global 1–8)**, mark right–center–left–center with gentle seated pressure shifts or hand tracing on 1–4, trace the small circle with one hand on 5–6, and settle on 7–8. For **Phrase B (global 9–16)**, alternate gentle right–left foot-pressure or hand-tap pulses on 9–12, open the arms on 13–14, and lower them on 15–16. Maintain the A8→B1 boundary on consecutive beats **global 8→9**. Seated marking preserves sequencing but does not demonstrate standing hip or knee mechanics. If necessary, rehearse each phrase separately and then test the connection slowly before considering the 16-count composition complete.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Complete two connected 16-count attempts at a comfortable steady tempo. For each, check **A7 (global 7): settle begun; A8 (global 8): stable finish; B1 (global 9): right pulse starts on the next beat; B2 (global 10): left pulse follows**. Record each boundary count as **on time / off time / unobservable**, and separately label any attempt that paused between phrases as **segmented rehearsal, not a connected 16-count performance**. State one observed correction and one limitation; a seated adaptation may demonstrate sequencing without proving standing knee mechanics. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Phrase A ends by settling on counts 7–8. What is the most useful preparation for Phrase B?**

A. Extend the final settle into the first count of B without recording the timing change

B. Confirm support and start the next eight-count phrase on time

C. Start Phrase B on its second count to make the transition feel quicker

D. Restart Phrase A instead of connecting to Phrase B

**2. The two phrases are individually accurate, but the link is inconsistent. What should be practiced?**

A. Rehearse Phrase A alone, focusing on range rather than its final counts

B. Rehearse Phrase B alone without examining its entry from A

C. The final counts of A and first counts of B at a slower tempo

D. Practice the two phrases separately at the original speed, omitting the boundary

**3. A learner cannot maintain the 16-count sequence standing. Which change preserves the composition task?**

A. Mark both eight-count phrases seated and practice the boundary between them slowly before completing the connected sequence

B. Perform only the first eight-count phrase and treat it as the full 16-count composition

C. Rehearse the phrases in reverse order without documenting the change

D. Rehearse both phrases separately and assume the connection is complete without testing it

**4. Two recorded 16-count attempts show A8 on global count 8 and B1 on global count 9, with no extra pause and stable support. What conclusion is justified?**

A. The two phrases were individually rehearsed, so their connection needs no separate evidence

B. The connection will remain accurate at every tempo, including untested tempos

C. The linked video authenticates the learner's original phrase and transition

D. The learner repeated this original 8-to-9 boundary under the observed conditions

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Confirming stable support at A's settle and entering B on time protects the phrase boundary. A silently extends the settle, C skips B's first count, and D avoids connecting the phrases.

**2. C.** Slowly practicing A's final counts and B's first counts isolates the unreliable connection. A and B rehearse only one side, while D omits the boundary altogether.

**3. A.** Seated marking of both phrases plus their boundary preserves the full 16-count task without standing balance. B omits half the composition, C reverses the order without documenting it, and D assumes a connection that has not been tested.

**4. D.** Two observed connected attempts support a narrow claim of repeatable boundary timing with stable support at the practiced tempo. A treats separate rehearsal as proof of a connection, B generalizes to untested tempos, and C asserts unverified video authenticity. The observation does not certify cultural provenance or safety.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
