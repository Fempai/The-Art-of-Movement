## Learning goal and explanation

Compose two connected eight-count phrases using familiar foundation actions and a deliberate transition. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** A good transition preserves support and timing while making the change of movement legible.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** Phrase A: 1–2 slide right and center; 3–4 slide left and center; 5–6 small circle; 7–8 settle. Phrase B: 1–4 repeat a tiny alternating pulse; 5–6 open arms gently; 7–8 return to neutral. Pause between phrases before linking them.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Mark the entire 16-count sequence seated or perform each phrase separately.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the adapted phrase twice at a steady count, identify one observable change, and explain one limitation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Phrase A ends by settling on counts 7–8. What is the most useful preparation for Phrase B?**

A. Extend the final settle into the first count of B without recording the timing change

B. Confirm support and start the next eight-count phrase on time

C. Start Phrase B on its second count to make the transition feel quicker

D. Restart Phrase A instead of connecting to Phrase B

**2. The two phrases are individually accurate, but the link is inconsistent. What should be practiced?**

A. Rehearse Phrase A alone, focusing on range rather than its final counts

B. Rehearse Phrase B alone without examining its entry from A

C. The final counts of A and first counts of B at a slower tempo

D. Practice the two phrases separately at the original speed, omitting the boundary

**3. A learner cannot maintain the 16-count sequence standing. Which change preserves the composition task?**

A. Mark seated or rehearse the two eight-count phrases separately

B. Perform only the first eight-count phrase and treat it as the full 16-count composition

C. Rehearse the phrases in reverse order without documenting the change

D. Rehearse both phrases separately and assume the connection is complete without testing it

**4. What does a clear original transition demonstrate?**

A. Accurate execution of each phrase separately, even if the connecting counts are lost

B. A fluent transition at one tempo, which establishes the same result at any tempo

C. A smooth-looking connection without evidence of count accuracy or support

D. A repeatable change of movement with legible timing and support

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** A deliberate settled ending creates a clear, supported transition into Phrase B's opening pulses without sacrificing the count.

**2. C.** Rehearsing the boundary between phrases targets the actual transition error rather than replacing the existing movement vocabulary.

**3. A.** Seated or segmented practice preserves phrase order and makes the connection easier to examine without requiring continuous standing balance.

**4. D.** The observable result is a controlled connection between original phrases; cultural provenance and safety guarantees require separate evidence.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
