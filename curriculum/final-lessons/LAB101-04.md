# LAB101 · Lesson 04 — Perform a Complete 32-Count Undercurrent Draft

**Original repertory | 40–55 minutes | independent learner.** This is an authored lesson revision, not a certified professional performance evaluation. Source auditing and browser testing remain pending.

## Objective and explanation

Perform a complete, repeatable 32-count A–B–A′–C draft, inspect all three phrase-boundary transitions, and explain a documented timing or support revision and an expressive choice in a 300–400-word reflection.

A first repertory performance draft integrates recognizable motif, contrast, return, and resolution. The compositional form here is A–B–A′–C: A introduces the reach-and-gather idea, B changes facing and pathway, A′ returns to the motif with a longer reach, and C settles the sequence. A performance draft should be reproducible and intentional, not perfect or extreme. Grade the accuracy of counts and transitions separately from the clarity of artistic intention. A solo video may help identify visible timing errors, but it cannot prove how an outside audience would interpret the piece.

## Follow-along studio

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area. Warm up with gentle weight shifts, comfortable shoulder circles, and small arm reaches. Keep the movement within your own pain-free range. Use seated marking when balance or standing is uncomfortable; do not attempt falls, inversions, or unsupported acrobatics.

**Guided rehearsal (15 minutes):** Use a comfortable steady count. **A (1–8):** 1 step right, 2 settle, 3–4 gather hands, 5–6 reach diagonally right, 7 return arm, 8 recover. **B (9–16):** 9 step left, 10 settle, 11–12 sweep both arms across the body, 13–14 turn a comfortable small diagonal, 15 lower arms, 16 return facing front. **A′ (17–24):** 17 step right, 18 settle, 19–20 gather, 21–23 reach right with one extra count of sustain, 24 recover. **C (25–32):** 25 shift left, 26 settle, 27 shift right, 28 settle, 29 reach forward, 30 gather, 31 lower arms, 32 finish balanced. Repeat in four eight-count chunks, then connect. **Seated alternative (stable nonrolling chair, both feet supported):** **A (1–8):** 1 small rightward seated-pressure shift, 2 settle, 3–4 gather hands, 5–6 reach right arm diagonally, 7 return arm, 8 recover. **B (9–16):** 9 small leftward seated-pressure shift, 10 settle, 11–12 sweep arms across body, 13–14 turn gaze toward a comfortable diagonal while torso remains facing front, 15 lower arms, 16 gaze front. **A′ (17–24):** 17 shift seated pressure right, 18 settle, 19–20 gather, 21–23 sustain right-arm diagonal reach, 24 recover. **C (25–32):** 25 shift pressure left, 26 settle, 27 shift pressure right, 28 settle, 29 reach forward, 30 gather, 31 lower, 32 finish upright. The seated gaze change is **not** a standing turn or torso-facing change. **Boundary supports:** 8 ends neutral and supported before the leftward shift on 9; 16 ends neutral before the rightward shift on 17; 24 ends recovered before the leftward shift on 25. Keep the same 32 numbered counts without extra transition beats.

**Solo feedback (5 minutes):** Check transitions 8→9, 16→17, and 24→25 separately. If a transition needs an unscored step, revise the count sheet or pathway. If your performance is inconsistent, slow the pulse and practice the least reliable eight-count before returning to the whole work. Stop if you experience pain or dizziness.

**Memory and expressive check:** First perform while reading the score, then from memory, then compare with the score. Write two factual observations (for example, 'I reached on count 6 rather than 5') and one artistic intention (for example, 'I wanted the delay to suggest anticipation'). Do not confuse the two.

## Knowledge check

**1. How many eight-count phrases make the 32-count draft?**

A. Two, counting each eight-count phrase as sixteen beats

B. Three, omitting the final eight-count phrase

C. Four

D. Eight, counting each four-count half-phrase separately

**2. What does A′ represent?**

A. An exact repetition of A with no changed movement feature

B. A varied return of A

C. A new contrasting phrase that does not refer to A

D. A transition that replaces A rather than returning to its motif

**3. Which transition should be checked explicitly?**

A. Only count 32, because earlier phrase boundaries cannot affect support

B. Only counts 1→2, because the first step establishes all later transitions

C. Counts 8→9, 16→17, and 24→25

D. Only the first count of each phrase, without inspecting the preceding exit

**4. What does a solo recording establish most reliably?**

A. That the performer intended exactly the emotion a viewer reports

B. Visible timing and pathway details

C. That a successful recording proves the dancer can repeat every transition live

D. That the visible gesture establishes its historical cultural origin

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. C.** Four eight-count phrases total 4 × 8 = 32 counts. A and D double-count phrases or half-phrases, while B omits one eight-count phrase.

**2. B.** A′ is a recognizable return of A with a deliberate change. A describes exact repetition, C an unrelated contrast, and D a replacement transition rather than a return.

**3. C.** Checking 8→9, 16→17, and 24→25 tests the exits and entries at each eight-count boundary. A and B inspect only one location, while D ignores the preceding exit state.

**4. B.** A solo recording can show timing and visible pathways. A infers private intent, C assumes one take proves repeatability, and D assigns cultural lineage without historical evidence.

**Self-correction:** Explain any missed answer in your own words and repeat the associated movement chunk at a slower count. These questions check conceptual understanding, not externally validated technique.

## Repertory assignment

Submit a written count sheet and, if you choose, a private practice recording. Document starting and ending facing, supporting foot or seated support, the movement on each count, and one deliberate revision. For Lesson 04, submit the **complete 32-count A–B–A′–C performance draft** and a 300–400-word reflection on memory, timing, expressive intent, the three phrase-boundary transitions, and remaining limitations.

**Worked example (excerpt, not a substitute for the required reflection):** “I performed A on counts 1–8 and B on 9–16, returning to front on 16 before stepping right into A′ on 17. I sustained the varied reach through 21–23, recovered on 24, then completed C on 25–32. My first run inserted an extra step between counts 24 and 25; I reduced the travel in A′, rehearsed 23–26, and repeated the whole draft without the extra step. The longer A′ reach was intended to convey hesitation, but the recording documents timing and pathway, not a viewer's emotional interpretation. I still need to check consistency across additional attempts.”

**Rubric:** Complete and repeatable A–B–A′–C 32-count score 30%; safe support and accurately connected 8→9, 16→17, and 24→25 transitions 25%; legible A′ variation and expressive choices distinguished from observable facts 25%; 300–400-word reflection documenting a correction, memory/timing observations, and remaining limitations 20%. Self-scoring does not certify readiness for advanced physical skills.
