# LAB101 · Lesson 04 — Perform a Complete 32-Count Undercurrent Draft

**Original repertory | 40–55 minutes | independent learner.** This is an authored lesson revision, not a certified professional performance evaluation. Source auditing and browser testing remain pending.

## Objective and explanation

Perform and describe the lesson's movement material, identify at least one specific timing or support error, correct it using a written score, and explain an artistic choice without treating intention as universally observable.

A first repertory performance draft integrates recognizable motif, contrast, return, and resolution. The compositional form here is A–B–A′–C: A introduces the reach-and-gather idea, B changes facing and pathway, A′ returns to the motif with a longer reach, and C settles the sequence. A performance draft should be reproducible and intentional, not perfect or extreme. Grade the accuracy of counts and transitions separately from the clarity of artistic intention. A solo video may help identify visible timing errors, but it cannot prove how an outside audience would interpret the piece.

## Follow-along studio

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area. Warm up with gentle weight shifts, comfortable shoulder circles, and small arm reaches. Keep the movement within your own pain-free range. Use seated marking when balance or standing is uncomfortable; do not attempt falls, inversions, or unsupported acrobatics.

**Guided rehearsal (15 minutes):** Use a comfortable steady count. **A (1–8):** 1 step right, 2 settle, 3–4 gather hands, 5–6 reach diagonally right, 7 return arm, 8 recover. **B (9–16):** 9 step left, 10 settle, 11–12 sweep both arms across the body, 13–14 turn a comfortable small diagonal, 15 lower arms, 16 return facing front. **A′ (17–24):** 17 step right, 18 settle, 19–20 gather, 21–23 reach right with one extra count of sustain, 24 recover. **C (25–32):** 25 shift left, 26 settle, 27 shift right, 28 settle, 29 reach forward, 30 gather, 31 lower arms, 32 finish balanced. Repeat in four eight-count chunks, then connect. Seated alternative: replace steps with small torso shifts while retaining all arm counts and facing cues.

**Solo feedback (5 minutes):** Check transitions 8→9, 16→17, and 24→25 separately. If a transition needs an unscored step, revise the count sheet or pathway. If your performance is inconsistent, slow the pulse and practice the least reliable eight-count before returning to the whole work. Stop if you experience pain or dizziness.

**Memory and expressive check:** First perform while reading the score, then from memory, then compare with the score. Write two factual observations (for example, 'I reached on count 6 rather than 5') and one artistic intention (for example, 'I wanted the delay to suggest anticipation'). Do not confuse the two.

## Knowledge check

**1. How many eight-count phrases make the 32-count draft?**

A. Two

B. Three

C. Four

D. Eight

**2. What does A′ represent?**

A. A complete deletion of A

B. A varied return of A

C. An unrelated historical tradition

D. A required leap

**3. Which transition should be checked explicitly?**

A. Only the final pose

B. The moment before rehearsal starts

C. Counts 8→9, 16→17, and 24→25

D. Only the musical title

**4. What does a solo recording establish most reliably?**

A. That every viewer feels the same emotion

B. Visible timing and pathway details

C. Professional accreditation

D. The dancer's internal state

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. C.** Four eight-count phrases total 32 counts.

**2. B.** A-prime retains recognizable A material with a deliberate variation.

**3. C.** Phrase boundaries are common sites of support and timing errors.

**4. B.** A recording supports observable movement analysis, not universal audience interpretation.

**Self-correction:** Explain any missed answer in your own words and repeat the associated movement chunk at a slower count. These questions check conceptual understanding, not externally validated technique.

## Repertory assignment

Submit a written count sheet and, if you choose, a private practice recording. Document starting and ending facing, supporting foot or seated support, the movement on each count, and one deliberate revision. For Lesson 04, submit the **complete 32-count performance draft** and a 300–400-word reflection on memory, timing, expressive intent, and remaining limitations.

**Worked example:** “I placed my right foot on count 1, gathered my hands over 3–4, and reached over 5–6. On the first attempt I began the reach on 4. I isolated counts 3–6 and corrected the overlap. My intention was anticipation, but the recording can only establish timing and visible focus.”

**Rubric:** Reproducible counts 30%; safe, controlled transitions 25%; legible compositional choices 25%; critical self-observation and revision 20%. Self-scoring does not certify readiness for advanced physical skills.
