## Learning outcomes and concept

Revisit the original foundation topics and compare current controlled execution with an earlier record **only where the movement task matches**. The Week 1 right–center–left–center pressure-shift drill is not identical to this midterm's slide–circle–settle phrase. This is a formative midterm, not a pass/fail demand for extreme range or a claim of certified technique. Identify weight support and timing, demonstrate the eight-count version twice, and describe one correction and one evidence limit.

**Optional source-observation study (3–5 minutes, if accessible):** Compare one observable hip-work action from the scheduled hip-up video with one slide from the scheduled hip-slide video. Record their support, direction, and tempo separately; do not treat either demonstration as automatically equivalent to the midterm's combined slide–circle–settle phrase. If you have a Week 2 record of the same slide task, compare only genuinely matched conditions. Otherwise record **source not observed / no matched baseline** and assess the current written study without inventing improvement.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm-up (4–5 minutes):** March or shift weight comfortably; move ankles and shoulders gently. Clear a nonslip space, use a stable fixed support as needed, and avoid forced rotation or painful range. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of control.

**Count-by-count guided study (8–12 minutes):** Count 1: slide gently right; 2: return to center; 3: slide left; 4: return to center. Counts 5–6: trace a small, comfortable circular pathway, first marking the loop slowly with a hand if needed. Count 7: settle into comfortable support; 8: hold or mark the beat while noticing the final position. Do not attempt to verbalize a reflection while balance is uncertain; record your observation after the phrase. Practice the same eight counts twice at a recorded, comfortable tempo.

**Repeat (5 minutes):** First say counts aloud at a slow tempo. Second focus on where your weight is supported. Third add a modest accent without increasing the movement range. Rest between attempts. When you can access the restored original video link, use it to compare the demonstrated vocabulary with this original drill, noting any difference rather than assuming equivalence.

**Modification:** Sit in a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported. On **1** mark a small rightward seated-pressure shift (or hand trace), **2** return center, **3** mark leftward pressure (or hand trace), and **4** return center. On **5–6** trace a small comfortable loop with one hand, **7** settle with both feet supported, and **8** hold neutral or hand-mark the beat. Record whether each action used pressure or hand tracing; neither substitutes for demonstrated standing hip-slide or pelvic-circle mechanics. Alternatively, use a slower comfortable count while retaining all eight numbered actions. For the two scored attempts, keep tempo and adaptation consistent where feasible and disclose any differences.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist or your lower back compresses, reduce range and return to comfortable parallel stance. If the circle rushes on counts 5–6, rehearse those counts slowly and check the controlled return to neutral on 7–8 before repeating the whole phrase. Do not treat pain as evidence of productive practice.

**Success check:** Complete **two separate eight-count Week 8 attempts** at a recorded comfortable tempo. For each attempt, mark the planned **right slide 1 / center return 2 / left slide 3 / center return 4 / circle begins 5 and continues 6 / settle 7 / hold or beat mark 8** as **on count / off count / unobservable / intentionally omitted**; label any seated or hand-traced substitution rather than counting it as demonstrated standing hip technique. Report **on-count actions out of observable, attempted actions**; exclude both unobservable and intentionally omitted actions from the denominator. If no attempted action can be observed, write **not assessable**, not zero or perfect. Score an adapted seated timing action only as that documented adaptation, never as evidence of standing hip technique. Describe one count-specific correction and whether the second attempt provides observable evidence of a difference, or state that it does not. **Worked observation:** An on-count return to center on 2 and 4 is a timing observation, not proof of equivalent Week 1 hip mechanics. Compare Week 1 and Week 8 directional **order** descriptively, not as equivalent timing or mechanics; for progress evidence, repeat the original Week 1 drill under matched conditions. Assess the Week 8 circle and settle only across Week 8 trials. This checkpoint assesses self-observation, not medical safety or expert performance.

## Midterm observation protocol (formative, not a proficiency certification)

1. **Baseline:** Locate an actual Week 1 record, if available. Record its date, standing/seated support, tempo or approximate count speed, and whether the pathway was performed or hand-marked. **The right–center–left–center order is shared, but Week 1 uses two counts per direction and Week 8 uses one; neither timing nor mechanics is a like-for-like baseline. Repeat the original Week 1 pressure-shift drill separately under matched conditions if you want a defensible progress comparison. The Week 8 circle/settle has no Week 1 equivalent.** If the record is missing, write “no recorded baseline” and compare only the two Week 8 attempts.
2. **Two Week 8 trials:** Use the same comfortable tempo and support conditions for both trials when feasible. After each, mark each count **on count / off count / unobservable / intentionally omitted**, then report on-count actions out of **observable, attempted actions** (not automatically out of eight); if none is observable, write **not assessable**. Also note (a) whether you returned to center on 2 and 4, (b) whether the small loop stayed comfortable on 5–6, and (c) whether you settled without losing balance on 7–8. Keep intentionally omitted movements and hand-traced substitutions visible in the record rather than scoring them as demonstrated standing technique. Record any modification and stop if symptoms arise.
3. **Interpretation:** Name one specific observation supported by your records and one correction to rehearse. If Week 1 and Week 8 differ in support, tempo, or movement type, describe those differences rather than claiming a direct improvement in range or skill.
4. **Evidence boundary:** Written self-observation can show more consistent counts or clearer self-correction under the recorded conditions. It cannot independently establish professional technique, injury prevention, or correspondence with either planned video.

## Knowledge check — try before answers

**1. What is the best basis for comparing the Week 8 midterm with an earlier attempt?**

A. Comparing the largest circle in each attempt, even if the tempo differs

B. Matching the camera angle and costume without recording support or timing

C. Count consistency, support, and repeatable movement choices

D. Completing more repetitions without documenting count accuracy

**2. The learner uses a seated version at midterm but stood previously. What should the reflection include?**

A. The adaptation and the effect it has on the comparison

B. Comparing range directly without recording that the support conditions changed

C. Comparing only standing and seated movement size

D. Comparing the two performances as if the support conditions were identical

**3. A learner rushes the small circle on counts 5–6. What is a useful correction?**

A. Repeat the full phrase at the same tempo without isolating counts 5–6

B. Mark the circle on counts 4–6 to allow extra preparation time

C. Skip the final settle to finish the circle more comfortably

D. Mark counts 5–6 slowly and check the return to the final settle

**4. A learner has a Week 1 record of right–center–left–center pressure shifts and two Week 8 slide–circle–settle trials. What comparison is justified?**

A. A direct Week 1–Week 8 comparison of the entire slide–circle–settle phrase, treating the Week 1 pressure-shift record as if it contained the circle

B. A descriptive comparison of the shared right–center–left–center order, explicitly noting different count durations and mechanics; compare progress only using a separately repeated original Week 1 drill, and assess the Week 8 circle/settle within Week 8

C. A comparison of Week 1 pressure-shift timing with Week 8 circle range, treating the two different measures as one improvement score

D. A Week 1–Week 8 comparison of the circle and settle after estimating those unrecorded Week 1 movements from the Week 8 attempts

### Answers and explanations

**1. C.** Count consistency, support, and repeatable movement choices make the midterm comparison interpretable. A compares circle size at different tempos, B omits support and timing despite matching camera setup, and D counts repetitions without checking accuracy.

**2. A.** Note that one attempt was seated and explain how changed support affects what can be compared. B, C, and D ignore or reduce the comparison to movement size while failing to account for the different support conditions.

**3. D.** Isolating counts 5–6 slowly and checking the final settle addresses the rushed circle. A repeats the same full phrase without isolating it, B shifts the circle onto count 4, and C skips the planned settle.

**4. B.** Week 1 pressure shifts take two counts per direction; Week 8 slides take one count per direction. The shared directional order may be described, but the timing and mechanics are **not** matched. Progress against Week 1 requires a separate repeat of the original Week 1 task under comparable conditions. The Week 8 circle and settle have no Week 1 equivalent and can be compared only between the two Week 8 trials. A treats an unrecorded circle as if it were in the baseline, C combines timing and range into a falsely comparable measure, and D imputes absent Week 1 observations from later performances. Do not invent missing baseline data.

**Retake rule:** For a missed question, revisit the relevant count or pathway, mark the movement slowly, and retry. These questions assess introductory understanding, not professional competence or cultural authority.

## Independent assignment

Record or write the Week 8 eight-count phrase twice, identifying each count, support surface, movement direction, and rhythmic accent. Week 1's pressure-shift study allocates **two counts per direction** (right 1–2, center 3–4, left 5–6, center 7–8), whereas this midterm's slide-and-return study allocates **one count per direction** (right 1, center 2, left 3, center 4). The shared right–center–left–center order is **not** a matched timing task, and a standing weight-pressure shift is not automatically the same movement as a pelvic slide. For an actual Week 1 progress comparison, separately repeat the **original Week 1 pressure-shift drill** under comparable tempo, support, and scoring criteria and compare it with an existing Week 1 record. Otherwise compare only the named directional order descriptively and state **no matched baseline**. The Week 8 circle/settle is a different task; do not compare its counts 5–8 with Week 1. In 150–250 words identify one observable difference, one correction, one adaptation, and one uncertainty. If no Week 1 record exists, explicitly state that a baseline comparison is unavailable rather than reconstructing one from memory. Clearly distinguish your original practice from the named video technique, while playback and the source-to-lesson match remain unverified.

**Rubric:** movement pathway and counts 30%; self-correction 25%; modification and control 25%; careful cultural and source attribution 20%. **Scoring interpretation:** The on-count/observable-attempted fraction measures timing only for attempted, observable actions; it is **not** the midterm grade or proof that all eight planned actions were completed. Report the eight-count coverage (attempted, omitted, unobservable, or adapted) separately from the timing numerator and denominator for each trial. A perfect timing fraction on a partial phrase must **not** be presented as full-phrase completion. Judge the 30% pathway/count criterion using both documented count accuracy and the extent of the attempted sequence; assess modification and control only from observable, comfortable actions. A seated hand trace can evidence adapted timing but **not** standing pelvic technique. Safety-motivated omissions are documented rather than treated as failed technique; where standing technique was not observed, label it **not demonstrated**. Never grade maximal range or speed.
