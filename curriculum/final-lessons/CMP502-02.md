# CMP502 · Lesson 02 — Canon: Staggered Entries in a Solo Study

**Advanced Composition | graduate-level creative inquiry | 50–70 minutes.** This is an authored learner-facing revision; bibliographic audit, expert review, and browser acceptance testing remain pending.

## Lecture: define the compositional device

**Learning goals:** Explain the featured device, construct a reproducible movement score, identify its difference from simple repetition, and justify a revision without claiming the resulting work has a universal emotional meaning.

Canon occurs when a movement phrase is introduced by one performer or part and then repeated after a delay by another. In group dance, dancers may overlap while performing the same phrase at offset times. A solo learner cannot physically perform two full-body phrases simultaneously; however, canon can be studied using a recorded first layer, a second pass, or a smaller body-part echo. A delayed echo is not the same as simple repetition when the first phrase has ended. Specify the entry offset and whether phrases overlap. Use only your own recordings or appropriately licensed material.

## Guided choreography laboratory

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Warm up with comfortable walking or seated shifts and gentle arm movements. Use an open nonslip area. No falls, forced ranges, or unsupported inversions are required.

**Build and rehearse (20 minutes):** Compose a four-count arm phrase: 1 gather, 2 open right arm, 3 open left arm, 4 recover. Record yourself performing it twice without traveling, on counts 1–4 and 5–8. On a second pass, begin the same four-count arm phrase on count 3, repeating on 7; align the two recordings if you have a simple video editor. The entries differ by two counts, so the phrases overlap on counts 3–4. If editing is unavailable, draw a two-row timeline: Layer A occupies 1–4; Layer B occupies 3–6. Then perform a bodily echo: right arm begins on 1, left arm begins a related gesture on 3, using a smaller range to avoid awkward crossing.

**Troubleshooting and critical editing:** If the layers are just consecutive, revise the start count to create overlap. If you cannot overlay recordings, the written timeline is a valid analysis artifact, but do not claim you performed a full multi-dancer canon.

**Analytical comparison:** Record the original motif and its transformation in separate columns: event, count, pathway, support, and changed compositional rule. Explain which elements remain recognizable and why. If a video is unavailable, a detailed count score is sufficient.

## Knowledge check

**1. What defines a canon?**

A. Two dancers perform the same phrase starting on the same count

B. A phrase is repeated with a delayed entry

C. One dancer repeats the phrase alone after completing the first version

D. A second layer performs the phrase backward with no delayed entry

**2. When does Layer B enter in the taught example?**

A. Count 1, simultaneous with Layer A

B. Count 2, one count after Layer A

C. Count 3

D. Count 8, seven counts after Layer A

**3. Which is an honest solo alternative?**

A. Use a two-row timeline or layered self-recording

B. Record the two layers separately but present the result as an unedited live duet

C. Draw two timelines without indicating the count at which Layer B begins

D. Perform Layer A fully before Layer B and label the nonoverlapping sequence a canon

**4. Which feature is absent when the two phrases are performed consecutively without overlap?**

A. A shared phrase performed in the same movement vocabulary

B. A documented starting count for each separate phrase

C. A clearly recorded order for the two phrases

D. Overlapping activity between the two layers

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. B.** A canon repeats related phrase material with a delayed entry, producing a temporal relationship between layers. Simultaneous unison (A), a completed solo repetition without overlap (C), and an unrelated backward layer without the delayed entry (D) do not demonstrate this taught canon.

**2. C.** In the lesson's timeline Layer A enters on count 1 and Layer B enters on count 3, a two-count offset. Entering together on count 1 (A), one count later on count 2 (B), or seven counts later on count 8 (D) would change the specified compositional rule.

**3. A.** A two-row timeline or honestly labeled layered self-recording can show simultaneous activity without pretending two dancers were present. Passing separate takes off as a live duet (B), omitting the entry count (C), or labeling a nonoverlapping repetition a canon (D) misrepresents the evidence or the taught structure.

**4. D.** The consecutive phrases lack overlapping activity, which is the defining timing feature of this particular two-layer canon. They can still share movement vocabulary (A), documented start counts (B), and an explicit order (C); none of those features requires simultaneous activity.

**Self-review:** For any missed item, correct the compositional rule and demonstrate the difference in a smaller four-count version. These questions are original instructional checks, not a validated professional assessment.

## Independent assignment

Submit a two-layer eight-count canon timeline using the four-count arm phrase (gather 1, right arm 2, left arm 3, recover 4). Show Layer A on global counts 1–4 and 5–8, and Layer B beginning on global count 3 (thus 3–6 and 7–10 if repeated). Explicitly mark overlapping counts 3–4 and 7–8, and do not truncate the second B phrase at count 8 without saying so. Provide a layered self-recording if available, or a clearly labeled written simulation and one safe bodily-echo experiment. Log a timing correction and write a 250–350-word reflection distinguishing overlapping delayed entries from consecutive repetition. A video is optional.

**Worked example:** “My first layer performs gather/open-right/open-left/recover on global counts 1–4, then again on 5–8. The second layer enters with gather on global count 3, opens right on 4, left on 5, and recovers on 6; its next entry on 7 continues through 10. Both layers are active on 3–4 and 7–8. I can score that overlap without claiming to dance two complete bodies simultaneously.”

**Rubric:** Accurate entry offset and overlap 35%; complete count-level two-layer timeline 30%; honest solo simulation and safe execution 15%; critique and justified revision 20%. The self-score is not a professional adjudication.
