# CMP101 · Lesson 01 — Motif: Build, Identify, and Transform a Movement Idea

**Independent composition study | 40–55 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; source metadata, live links, and browser acceptance remain pending. The site serves as your teacher for these foundational tasks, but no self-score guarantees how an outside viewer will interpret a work.

## Concept and learning outcomes

After this lesson you should be able to define the key compositional idea, apply it to an original phrase, record it precisely enough to repeat, and explain what the notation does and does not show.

A movement motif is a recognizable movement idea that can recur and be developed. It need not be a single pose or an eight-count sequence. Its identity may depend on a pathway, gesture, rhythmic accent, relationship between body parts, or several of these together. A useful test is whether you can change one feature while retaining enough of the idea for a viewer—or your own movement score—to recognize the relationship. Distinguish *repetition* (the idea returns with little change) from *variation* (the idea returns with a deliberate alteration). Neither automatically makes choreography meaningful: you must state what you intend and then observe whether the pattern is legible.

**Observation versus intention:** You can verify that an arm reaches on count five. You cannot prove that every viewer reads that reach as longing. Write the intended effect separately from the count and pathway.

## Guided studio and writing lab

**Preparation (3–5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip space, warm up with gentle steps or seated torso shifts, and decide which direction is the front of the room. Use a small comfortable range; avoid painful or unstable movement.

**Follow this score (10–15 minutes):** Create motif M: 1 step right, 2 settle, 3–4 draw both hands toward the sternum, 5–6 reach the right arm diagonally, 7 lower arm, 8 recover. Perform M twice. For M-prime, keep counts 3–4 and the reach pathway but change the reach to the left and sustain through count 7. Write which two features stayed constant and which changed.

**Compare and revise:** If you replace the gather, diagonal reach, timing, and direction simultaneously, you may have created a new idea rather than a legible variation. Preserve at least one identifying feature for this experiment.

**Undercurrent connection:** Use the existing step–gather–reach–recover motif as material. Identify what you kept, changed, and why. Record two takes or write two complete count sheets. A single photograph of the endpoint is insufficient to document timing or support.

**Self-check:** Can you perform the phrase twice with the same counts and orientation? Does your score identify the support foot and the pathway? If you cannot reconstruct it, revise the notation before increasing complexity.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is a movement motif?**

A. A recognizable movement idea that can recur and develop

B. A single movement performed once with no identifiable feature recurring

C. A complete phrase whose length must remain fixed at 32 counts

D. A final position with no relationship to the preceding movement

**2. Which is an example of variation?**

A. Repeat the diagonal reach with identical direction, duration, and dynamics

B. Preserve the diagonal reach but change its timing

C. Replace the reach with unrelated footwork and call it the same motif without any shared feature

D. Change only the costume while retaining identical movement timing and pathway

**3. What best tests motif legibility?**

A. The choreographer's statement that the motif is recognizable, without comparing performances

B. A repeatable score showing which features recur

C. Whether the final pose looks impressive without checking repeated features

D. Whether the audience can see the costume detail, even if the motif changes

**4. What is a limitation of a self-evaluation?**

A. The performer may know their intention even when the change is unclear to others

B. The performer's familiarity with the motif proves that its variation is obvious to a new viewer

C. A detailed movement score guarantees the same emotional response across audiences

D. Repeating a motif twice guarantees the audience will identify its intended meaning

### Answer key and rationale

**1. A.** A motif is a recurring movement idea, not necessarily a fixed length.

**2. B.** Changing one feature while retaining a recognizable relationship is a variation.

**3. B.** A score lets you compare the motif's identifying features across versions.

**4. A.** Authorial intention and audience recognition are different questions.

**Retry:** Revisit any missed concept, annotate the movement example, and answer again without looking. These are instructor-authored conceptual checks, not externally validated tests of choreographic talent.

## Independent assignment and rubric

Create an annotated composition score. Include count numbers, action verbs, support foot or seated support, facing, pathway, at least one intentional change, and a 250–350-word critical reflection. For Lesson 04, submit a complete **32-count A–B–A′–C score**.

**Worked example:** “Counts 3–4: both hands gather toward the sternum; weight remains on the right foot. Counts 5–6: right arm reaches northeast. In A′, the reach lasts through count 7. The duration changed; my intended meaning is an interpretation, not an objective property of the score.”

**Rubric:** Structural clarity 25%; precise, reproducible notation 25%; intentional variation or contrast 25%; critical reflection and source awareness 25%. A self-assessment should identify a concrete revision, not merely give a high score.
