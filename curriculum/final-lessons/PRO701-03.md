# PRO701 · Lesson 03 — Interpretation Under Constraint: One Score, Three Readings

**Professional Repertory Laboratory | independent performance study | 50–70 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; scholarly verification, expert adjudication, and live browser testing remain pending.

## Performance principle

**Objectives:** Define a reproducible performance criterion, apply a deliberate rehearsal method, compare observed execution with intention, and revise safely.

Interpretation in repertory requires keeping the agreed movement structure recognizable while varying expressive decisions. A dancer can alter gaze, attack, duration, or recovery quality to support an intention; the audience's interpretation remains open. The performer should be able to explain which physical variables changed rather than relying on private emotion alone. This is particularly important in a solo self-taught academy: no teacher is present to infer intentions or validate audience effects. Documentation should distinguish the choreographic score, the performer's chosen reading, and the observable execution.

## Guided repertory laboratory

**Prepare (5–8 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area. Warm up with easy steps, gentle reaches, and comfortable changes of direction. No unsupervised aerials, lifts, or extreme ranges are required.

**Rehearse (20–30 minutes):** Perform the same 16-count Undercurrent section twice: A (1–8) step–gather–reach–recover and B (9–16) redirect left, sweep, turn slightly, return front. Version 1, 'invitation': gaze follows the open hand and recovery softens. Version 2, 'hesitation': reach begins on the scored count but travels slowly, ending by 6; hold stillness on 8. Version 3, 'resolve': reach completes decisively on 6 and gaze returns front on 7. Keep the footwork and count placements stable. Record each version twice, then make a matrix of gaze destination, reach duration, recovery quality, and observed count accuracy.

**Troubleshooting:** If the versions become indistinguishable, choose one variable to exaggerate within a comfortable range. If changing expression disrupts timing, practice at half speed. Do not use distressing autobiographical recall as a requirement for expressive work.

**Independent observation:** Keep an annotated score, recording the planned count, what actually occurred, the viewing or recording limitations, and a single correction. When using video, preserve the camera position for comparisons.

## Knowledge check

**1. What must remain recognizable across these interpretations?**

A. The agreed movement structure and counts

B. The exact gaze direction

C. A fixed emotional meaning

D. A guaranteed audience response

**2. Which is an observable expressive variable?**

A. A dancer's private memory

B. Gaze destination on count 7

C. Every viewer's feelings

D. An assumed personality trait

**3. What should a dancer do if expressive variation disrupts the counts?**

A. Ignore timing

B. Add unscripted steps

C. Slow down and stabilize the score

D. Increase intensity

**4. What cannot the rehearsal video establish?**

A. Visible timing

B. Hand pathway

C. Head orientation

D. A universal interpretation of the phrase

### Answers and explanations

**1. A.** Interpretation changes expression while retaining the score.

**2. B.** Gaze orientation can be documented.

**3. C.** Secure the structure before adding expressive complexity.

**4. D.** Audience meaning cannot be inferred universally from footage.

**Self-correction:** For missed answers, demonstrate the corrected concept in a four- or eight-count example. This knowledge check is not a validated measure of professional performance ability.

## Assignment and assessment

Submit an annotated rehearsal score, an error-and-revision log, and a 250–350-word reflection with at least two observable observations. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a **2–3 minute repertory showcase** with a timed five-section score, one continuous performance or a documented non-video equivalent, and a critique of technical consistency, transitions, expressive choices, and recovery.

**Worked example:** “The reach began on count 4 in my first trial instead of count 5. I isolated gather 3–4 and reach 5–6, then repeated the full phrase. My second trial met the timing target; I have not established long-term retention.”

**Rubric:** Score accuracy and movement control 30%; rehearsal and correction process 25%; expressive clarity with evidence 25%; safety, recovery, and reflection 20%. Self-assessment does not imply certification.
