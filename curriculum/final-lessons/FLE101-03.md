# FLE101 · Lesson 03 — Hamstring Flexibility Versus Active Leg Control

**Active Flexibility Foundations | 35–45 minutes | independent study.** This is a learner-facing revision, not a medical evaluation or a certified stretching prescription. Stop for sharp pain, tingling, numbness, dizziness, or unusual symptoms. Do not use forced splits, partner pressure, or aggressive stretching.

## Learning goals and anatomical reasoning

Explain the relevant movement concept, demonstrate a comfortable version, distinguish active control from passive range, and document observable progress without claiming that a particular range is necessary for dance.

The hamstrings cross the back of the hip and knee, with the short head of biceps femoris crossing only the knee. Their perceived stretch changes with hip and knee position. A straight-leg forward fold is not the only way to examine hamstring mobility; it can also be influenced by spinal motion and neural sensitivity. An active leg lift tests coordination and strength in addition to available range. This lesson does not treat a particular toe-touch distance as a universal standard, nor does it prescribe aggressive nerve stretching.

## Guided mobility studio

**Follow along (10–15 minutes):** After three minutes of easy walking, sit on a stable chair. Extend the right knee only as far as comfortable while keeping the torso upright and the heel near the floor; hold for two counts, lower for two. Repeat four times per side. Then, seated with one foot grounded, gently hinge forward from the hips with a comfortable knee bend until you feel mild tension, not pain; return after two easy breaths. Do not round forcibly or pull on the foot. For a standing active option, hold a stable rail and lift the knee a few inches rather than attempting a high straight-leg kick.

**Troubleshooting and safety:** If you feel tingling, burning, or electric sensations, stop the stretch; these symptoms are not a target. If the knee cannot straighten comfortably, maintain a bend. If the torso rocks to lift the leg, reduce the range and focus on control.

**Apply to Undercurrent:** Perform a comfortable step–gather–reach–recover phrase for eight counts. Note whether you can move and recover without compensatory back arching, unstable support, or forced range. Keep your musical intention separate from the physical observation.

**Independent readiness check:** Can you enter and exit the selected range twice smoothly, breathe comfortably, and describe a specific modification? If not, reduce the demand. A self-report cannot rule out an underlying injury.

## Knowledge check

**1. What distinguishes an active leg lift from a passive assisted stretch?**

A. It requires muscular control to move or hold the leg

B. It reaches the same range as an assisted stretch without requiring muscular effort

C. It is defined by the use of an external strap to lift the leg

D. It establishes the person's maximum passive range from one held position

**2. Which hamstring statement is anatomically accurate?**

A. Every muscle commonly grouped with the hamstrings crosses both the hip and knee

B. The short head of biceps femoris crosses the hip but not the knee

C. The short head of biceps femoris crosses only the knee

D. The long head of biceps femoris crosses only the knee

**3. What should you do if stretching produces tingling or electric sensations?**

A. Maintain the same tingling position but reduce only the hold time

B. Stop rather than forcing the position

C. Increase the stretch gradually until the sensation fades

D. Continue the same position if the muscle itself does not feel sore

**4. Which is the best measure for comparing your own practice?**

A. Your current range compared with a demonstrator using a different setup

B. The deepest passive position achieved once without a control check

C. A higher leg lift performed with different support each session

D. A consistent comfortable active range and control note

### Answer key and explanations

**1. A.** An active leg lift requires the learner's muscles to move or maintain the position. B wrongly removes muscular effort, C defines the action by external assistance, and D infers maximal passive range from one held position.

**2. C.** The short head of biceps femoris crosses the knee but not the hip, unlike the commonly described biarticular hamstrings. A incorrectly generalizes to every hamstring component, B reverses the joints crossed by the short head, and D incorrectly describes the long head as knee-only.

**3. B.** Stop the stretch if tingling or electric sensations occur; do not force the position. A retains the provoking position, C increases the stretch, and D ignores concerning sensations because the muscle itself is not sore. Seek medical advice if symptoms persist or recur.

**4. D.** Logging comfortable active range and control under consistent conditions makes practice comparisons more meaningful. A compares different people and setups, B prioritizes a single passive extreme, and C changes support between sessions.

**Review protocol:** For a missed item, explain why the distractor is wrong, then demonstrate the relevant idea in a smaller range. These are original conceptual questions, not validated clinical assessments.

## Independent assignment: range and quality

Submit a dated hamstring-range and active-leg-control comparison. Record seated knee-extension repetitions on each side, comfortable end range, chair/foot support, and whether the torso stayed upright; then document the gentle supported hip hinge, any sensations requiring a stop, and one adjustment. Include a 200–300-word reflection distinguishing active knee extension from passive stretch sensation without claiming a clinical flexibility measurement.

**Worked example:** “After walking, I completed four seated knee extensions on each side with my torso upright. I kept my right knee slightly bent because full extension was uncomfortable. During the gentle hip hinge I felt mild tension but no pain, and I returned without pulling on the leg. I recorded both tasks separately rather than treating stretch sensation as proof of greater active range.”

**Rubric:** Active-versus-passive movement distinction 25%; comfort and safe adaptation 25%; reproducible bilateral observations 30%; evidence-limited interpretation and next-step decision 20%. Do not award points for extreme range or pain tolerance.
