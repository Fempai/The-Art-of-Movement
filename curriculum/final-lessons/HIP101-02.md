# HIP101 · Lesson 02 — Bounce: Downbeat, Rebound, and Dynamic Choice

**Learner-facing draft | 40–55 minutes.** The website is the instructor for this introductory lesson. This is not a certification of technique or an endorsement of unsupported cultural attributions. Scholarly metadata and deep links still require independent verification.

## Learn the idea

A bounce can be explored as a recurring lowering and rising action that connects the body to a pulse. It may involve small coordinated motions at the ankles, knees, and hips, not just isolated knee flexion. The word 'bounce' names a movement quality across many settings; this classroom drill does not claim to define all hip-hop bounce practices. A down accent emphasizes the lowering phase; an up accent emphasizes the return. Moving slowly can help reveal where the change of direction happens, but a slow exercise is not a substitute for learning the music and cultural context.

**Vocabulary in practice:** A *count* is a numbered timing reference, a *pulse* is a recurring temporal reference, an *accent* is a point of emphasis, and a *weight shift* is a change in how your body is supported. These are working descriptions for the exercises below, not universal definitions of a dance tradition.

## Follow-along practice

**Prepare (4 minutes):** Clear a nonslip space. March gently, shift weight between feet, and move shoulders in a comfortable range. Keep knees soft; avoid forced twists, maximal knee bends, and unstable furniture. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Practice (10–15 minutes):** Begin in a comfortable parallel stance. On 1 bend the knees slightly; 2 return toward standing; 3 lower; 4 rise; 5 lower while shifting weight right; 6 rise; 7 lower while shifting left; 8 rise. Repeat with counts 1–4 emphasizing the down motion and 5–8 emphasizing the return. Keep heels grounded if comfortable and avoid locked knees. Mark seated if standing pulses are uncomfortable.

**What to notice:** Two dancers may bend their knees the same distance yet appear rhythmically different because one initiates before the beat and one lands directly on it. You can document this by counting or recording, not by guessing what each dancer intended.

**Apply to Undercurrent (5–8 minutes):** Begin with the original step–gather–reach–recover phrase. Keep the eight-count framework, but replace one accent or weight transfer with today's concept. Perform your new version twice. Write the counts and explain what you intentionally changed. An original exercise is not a historical claim.

## Fix common difficulties

| Observation | Possible explanation | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| You feel pressure in the knees | You may be using more depth than necessary | Reduce the bend or switch to seated pulse |
| The rebound is a sudden jerk | You may be forcing the return | Use a smaller, smoother action |
| You cannot hear where the accent falls | You may be attending only to your legs | Clap the beat first, then add the movement |

**Progression checkpoint:** You can maintain an eight-count pulse, reproduce the phrase twice in a comfortable range, explain the difference between observation and interpretation, and avoid presenting a personal invention as an attributed historical step. A written score cannot verify expert performance quality.

## Knowledge check

**1. What distinguishes a down-accented bounce?**

A. An upward rebound receiving the strongest musical emphasis

B. Emphasis on the lowering phase

C. Equal emphasis on both lowering and rising

D. A delayed accent after the knees have already risen

**2. Which statement about slowing a bounce is most accurate?**

A. It preserves the exact rhythmic task at the original tempo

B. It changes only the movement range, never the timing relationship

C. It can help observation but changes the timing task

D. It guarantees the accent will remain on the same musical subdivision

**3. What is a sensible response to knee discomfort?**

A. Reduce depth or choose seated marking

B. Maintain the same uncomfortable depth but slow the rebound

C. Reduce arm movement while keeping the uncomfortable knee depth

D. Practice a faster shallow bounce before checking comfort

**4. What can a recording help you assess?**

A. Whether the dancer intended a downward accent, regardless of the visible timing

B. Whether the movement is safe for every learner with similar knee motion

C. Whether the bounce matches a specific community tradition without source evidence

D. Whether the lowering aligns with your intended count

### Answers and explanations

**1. B.** The accent is placed on the downward movement.

**2. C.** Slowing can clarify coordination while altering the musical demand.

**3. A.** A smaller, tolerable range preserves the rhythmic concept.

**4. D.** Video and sound can support timing observations but not all inferences.

**Important assessment limitation:** The questions test introductory conceptual understanding and have not been psychometrically validated. All answers happen to be placed as written here; a future interactive question bank should randomize options and explain incorrect choices. Retake any missed item after reviewing its explanation.

## Independent assignment

Produce a two-phrase, sixteen-count original groove study. Provide (1) a count-by-count movement score, (2) 250–350 words explaining your rhythmic choices, (3) one precise cultural or historical claim supported by an appropriate source, and (4) one claim you deliberately avoid making because the evidence is insufficient.

**Worked example:** “In phrase A, I lowered slightly on counts 1, 3, 5, and 7 and returned upward on the following counts. In phrase B, I used the same small range but made the upward return more noticeable. I can describe where I placed the accents without claiming this original drill represents every hip-hop bounce style.”

**Rubric:** Rhythmic description 25%; clear and repeatable movement design 25%; cultural precision and attribution 25%; evidence, limitations, and reflection 25%. Do not grade a learner's cultural authenticity from their body or appearance.
