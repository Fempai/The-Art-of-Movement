# TWK201 · Lesson 01 — Rebound Mechanics

**Standalone twerk technique | written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** TWK101 comfortable eight-count pulse and supported stance are recommended before this lesson. Not a verified instructor-led video.

## Objective
Distinguish a controlled rebound from an uncontrolled drop.

## Context
These original exercises explore coordination and musical choices, not a claim to represent a single historically universal twerk technique. Black social dance and New Orleans bounce context requires verified sourcing and practitioner review before release.

## Guided follow-along
Warm up five minutes with ankle articulation, small knee bends, and relaxed weight shifts. Over eight counts, use shallow leg-led down/up pulses on 1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–8. Compare a soft controlled return with a deliberately paused return at the same safe depth; repeat twice and rest. Work 10–15 minutes with recovery breaks. Do not force impact or speed.

## Modification
Use counter support and smaller depth; seated pressure pulses preserve timing but not standing loading.

## Self-correction
If rhythm breaks, reduce complexity and count aloud. If joints pinch or balance deteriorates, stop and choose a comfortable supported option. Do not equate visible motion with proof of muscle activation.

## Assignment
Notate two eight-count phrases, explain the chosen accents, and identify one technique correction and one adaptation.

## Four-question knowledge check
**1. Which is the controlled rebound?**

A. An uncontrolled drop

B. A forced back arch

C. A small lowering followed by a managed return

D. A locked-knee landing

**Key: C.** A small lowering followed by a managed return.

**2. What is the safest response to pain during practice?**

A. Stop and reduce range or choose a comfortable alternative

B. Continue at higher speed

C. Add resistance immediately

D. Hold the position

**Key: A.** Stop and reduce range or choose a comfortable alternative.

**3. What can the seated option preserve?**

A. Exactly the same joint loading

B. Proven isolated glute activation

C. Full standing travel

D. Some timing and directional intention, not all standing mechanics

**Key: D.** Some timing and directional intention, not all standing mechanics.

**4. Which checkpoint best supports intentional practice?**

A. Only watch the page

B. Repeat two eight-count phrases and describe a movement choice

C. Claim perfect form without observation

D. Increase range regardless of comfort

**Key: B.** Repeat two eight-count phrases and describe a movement choice.

## Checkpoint
Reproduce or notate two comfortable phrases and accurately distinguish the movement mechanics. Completion of this written lesson does not certify movement proficiency.
