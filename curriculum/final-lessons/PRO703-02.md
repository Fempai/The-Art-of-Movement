# PRO703 · Lesson 02 — Artist Statement: Intention, Form, and Attribution

**Performance Portfolio & Capstone | independent final-year study | 60–90 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly audit, expert review, and live-browser acceptance testing remain outstanding. This is a nonaccredited self-study program.

## Capstone seminar

**Learning outcomes:** Produce a traceable creative decision, connect an artistic claim to movement evidence, document ethical attribution, and evaluate growth without inventing external validation.

An artist statement communicates a work's questions, formal decisions, and context without dictating what every viewer must feel. It should be specific enough that a reader can connect language to visible movement: a repeated gather, delayed reach, or changed facing. The statement can discuss personal motivation, but disclosure of private experience is optional. If the work draws on a named dance tradition, artist, recording, or text, attribution must distinguish documented influence from a broad stylistic resemblance. A statement is not a substitute for movement evidence or permission to use third-party media.

## Guided portfolio studio

**Prepare:** Use a clear, level, permitted space and a comfortable movement range. No dangerous inversions, lifts, or forced stretching are required.

**Create and document (30–40 minutes):** Draft a 200–300-word artist statement for Undercurrent using five prompts: (1) What question drives the work? (2) Which movement motif carries it? (3) Which two formal transformations develop the motif? (4) What choices shape the audience's viewing experience? (5) What sources or collaborators need attribution? Underline every sentence that names a specific count, gesture, or structural choice. Rewrite vague claims such as 'the dance explores everything about emotion' into testable specifics. Pair the statement with a four-row A–B–A′–C movement score and check whether the described choices are actually present.

**Troubleshooting and integrity:** If a claim says the audience will definitely feel grief, change it to an intention or possible reading. If you used music without confirmed rights, note that the portfolio version must use licensed, original, or rights-cleared audio. Do not invent an artistic lineage.

**Evidence discipline:** Label every artifact as planned, rehearsed, recorded, or performed; identify dates and criteria. Distinguish an observed improvement from an interpretation or a claim about long-term learning.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is the strongest artist-statement sentence?**

A. Every viewer will feel longing when the arm extends in the final phrase

B. The reach on counts 5–6 recurs with a delayed recovery in A′

C. The movement is expressive because the performer intends it to be expressive

D. The reach is important, but its timing and structural role need not be specified

**2. How should audience meaning be described?**

A. As an emotion necessarily experienced by every person who sees the gesture

B. As something proven solely by the choreographer's private intention

C. As an intended or possible interpretation

D. As a measured response inferred from the movement score alone

**3. What is an ethical attribution practice?**

A. Name documented influences and identify uncertain provenance

B. Attribute an entire tradition to the first video showing a similar movement

C. Credit the choreography but omit the source and permissions of borrowed music

D. List a practitioner as a collaborator based only on having watched their work

**4. What does an artist statement not replace?**

A. A precise count sheet showing how the stated motif is performed

B. The license or permission required to reuse someone else's recording

C. Evidence that the gesture actually appears in the submitted performance

D. Movement evidence and media permissions

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. B.** The sentence connects intent to a specific formal choice.

**2. C.** Reception cannot be guaranteed.

**3. A.** Attribution should be specific and verifiable.

**4. D.** The statement complements, rather than substitutes for, documentation and rights.

**Self-review:** Correct any missed concept in a sentence that names the evidence and its limitation. These are original knowledge checks, not an external examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit the lesson's specified artifact, a 250–350-word explanation of one artistic decision, and a dated revision record. **Lesson 04 final capstone:** a **2–4 minute original work** with a complete count score, dated draft and final versions, rehearsal journal, artist statement, staging plan, critical reflection, permissions and attribution notes, and either a private performance recording or a clearly labeled unperformed score.

**Worked example:** “My first draft returned to center on count 8, obscuring the planned stillness. In the final score I moved the return to 7 and kept 8 still. Two recorded trials show the revised count placement, though they do not establish universal audience response.”

**Rubric:** Original choreographic structure and executable score 25%; documented revision and rehearsal process 25%; evidence-based critique and academic integrity 25%; safe presentation, attribution, and professional communication 25%. The score is a self-assessment, not accreditation.
