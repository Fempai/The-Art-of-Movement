# MOV101 · Lesson 03 — Posture, Alignment, and Moving Stability

**Learner-facing self-study lesson.** Approximate session: 35–50 minutes including practice, retrieval, and writing. Requires no ballet barre. Source links and metadata are included, but live browser and authenticated learner testing remain outstanding.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this lesson, you should be able to explain posture as dynamic organization; identify base of support and center of mass; adjust a weight transfer without enforcing a universal body shape.

## Read and understand

Posture describes how body segments are arranged relative to one another and the environment. It is not a single perfect silhouette. In dance, useful alignment changes with the task: a deep plié, a traveling step, and a suspended reach require different arrangements. The base of support is the region defined by points of contact with the supporting surface. The center of mass is a conceptual location representing the body's mass distribution. Balance depends on how forces, motion, and support interact; simply keeping the torso upright is not a complete description of control. A dancer can lean deliberately while maintaining a planned transfer of weight. The question is whether the movement is intentional, repeatable, and suitable for the task.

**A useful comparison:** A person walking does not keep the center of mass fixed over both feet. The body continuously reorganizes support. Dance magnifies these choices, making weight transfer and recovery part of the choreography.

**Think critically:** A visible pose is evidence of an appearance, not automatic evidence of strength, anatomical structure, safety, or artistic intention. Compare what can be observed with what would require a second camera angle, an explicit movement score, a qualified physical assessment, or a scholarly source.

## Practice with no teacher or barre

**Preparation (3–5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip space, walk or march gently, and make small comfortable movements through the ankles, hips, and shoulders. Do not use a moving treadmill or rolling chair as support. A wall or securely fixed structure can provide light support. If a movement produces pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of control, stop or choose a seated marking alternative.

**Guided exercise (8–12 minutes):** Stand with feet comfortably apart. On counts 1–2 shift slightly toward the right foot; on 3–4 touch the left toes lightly to the floor; on 5–6 return weight toward both feet; on 7–8 pause. Repeat to the left. Then perform the same transfer with a small diagonal arm reach. Use a wall or securely fixed support if needed. Notice the pressure under each foot and the timing of your transition.

**Original choreography application (5–8 minutes):** On count 2 of Undercurrent, you settle onto the right foot. If you begin the next gather before the weight has transferred, the phrase may feel rushed. A deliberate pause can make the transition legible without requiring a rigidly vertical trunk.

**Repeatability test:** Perform the exercise twice with the same counts. Say which foot supports you, what segment initiates the movement, and one deliberate change between repetitions. If you cannot repeat it comfortably, reduce range and speed before trying again.

## Troubleshooting

| Observation | Possible explanation, not a diagnosis | Self-teaching adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| You wobble when one foot becomes light | The base of support has changed and your control strategy may need simplifying | Keep the toes of both feet down, make the shift smaller, or use fixed support |
| You lock the knees to feel stable | Rigidity may make the transfer harder to adjust | Keep knees comfortably unlocked and reduce movement size |
| You cannot tell where your weight is | Your attention may be focused only on the arm shape | Practice the transfer without the arms; observe pressure through the feet |

## Knowledge check — attempt before reading answers

**1. What is the base of support?**

A. The dancer's favorite stance

B. The region defined by contact with the supporting surface

C. The line from head to toe

D. The distance of the leg extension

**2. Which statement about posture is most accurate?**

A. There is one ideal posture for every dance movement

B. Useful alignment depends on the movement task

C. The trunk must always remain vertical

D. Leaning always indicates an error

**3. What is the strongest observation of a weight transfer?**

A. A dancer looks confident

B. Pressure and support shift toward the right foot

C. The dancer feels artistic

D. The pose resembles a photograph

**4. Why practice the feet before the arms?**

A. Arms never affect balance

B. It isolates a key task component before adding complexity

C. The upper body is irrelevant

D. It guarantees perfect technique


### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** The base of support depends on contact with the ground or other supporting surface.

**2. B.** Posture and alignment should be evaluated in relation to the movement's goals and constraints.

**3. B.** Changes in contact and support are observable components of weight transfer.

**4. B.** Simplifying the phrase makes the support change easier to notice and reproduce.

**Scoring:** Four correct means you can explain these introductory ideas. If you miss a question, reread its explanation, repeat the corresponding demonstration, and answer again without looking. These are instructor-authored checks, not psychometrically validated exams or certification of physical technique.
