# PRO703 · Lesson 04 — Final Portfolio: Creative Synthesis and Evidence of Growth

**Performance Portfolio & Capstone | independent final-year study | 60–90 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly audit, expert review, and live-browser acceptance testing remain outstanding. This is a nonaccredited self-study program.

## Capstone seminar

**Learning outcomes:** Produce a traceable creative decision, connect an artistic claim to movement evidence, document ethical attribution, and evaluate growth without inventing external validation.

A final performance portfolio is a curated argument about what the learner can currently create, explain, and revise. It is not simply a folder of unrelated files or a claim of conservatory accreditation. A strong portfolio includes an original performance or detailed non-video score, evidence of process, artistic context, ethical attribution, a critical evaluation, and a realistic future-learning plan. Evidence of growth requires comparison across dated artifacts with consistent criteria; a final polished take alone does not establish progress. For a self-taught learner, the portfolio should make independent feedback limitations visible rather than implying teacher assessment occurred.

## Guided portfolio studio

**Prepare:** Use a clear, level, permitted space and a comfortable movement range. No dangerous inversions, lifts, or forced stretching are required.

**Create and document (30–40 minutes):** Create a 2–4 minute original Undercurrent capstone with five to eight 32-count sections, choosing a tempo and calculating duration: at 80 beats/minute, five sections (160 beats) take two minutes; eight sections (256 beats) take 3.2 minutes. Plan introduction, motif statement, directional variation, interpretive contrast, compositional transformation, and resolution, extending sections as needed. Submit a dated first-draft score and final score, a revision log, a 200–300-word artist statement, an annotated production plan, a final performance video or complete count-level score, and a 500–700-word critical reflection. If recording, perform only safe, comfortable movements with a clear exit and stable camera. Cite verified sources and state any unverified references.

**Troubleshooting and integrity:** If the work runs shorter than two minutes, adjust the count plan rather than misreporting duration. If audio rights are unclear, use silence, a metronome, or licensed original audio. If a final recording is unavailable, provide a complete, executable score and label it as an unperformed proposal rather than claiming a live showcase.

**Evidence discipline:** Label every artifact as planned, rehearsed, recorded, or performed; identify dates and criteria. Distinguish an observed improvement from an interpretation or a claim about long-term learning.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which demonstrates growth most directly?**

A. A dated baseline and final artifact evaluated with the same criteria

B. A polished final video compared with an unrecorded memory of the first attempt

C. A final rubric score without any baseline using the same measures

D. Two performances assessed with different criteria and no documented explanation

**2. At 80 beats/minute, how long is 256 beats?**

A. 2 minutes, counting only 160 of the 256 beats

B. 3.2 minutes

C. 4 minutes, treating the tempo as 64 beats per minute

D. 5 minutes, using an assumed tempo of about 51 beats per minute

**3. What is an acceptable non-video alternative?**

A. A written summary presented as proof that a recorded performance took place

B. An outline that names sections but omits counts and movement instructions

C. A complete executable score labeled accurately

D. An existing artist's score reproduced as original without source credit

**4. What does this self-taught capstone not confer?**

A. A documented portfolio assembled from the learner's own work

B. A dated record of decisions and revisions during the project

C. A written statement describing the work's artistic intent

D. An accredited professional qualification

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. A.** Dated baseline and final artifacts scored with the same criteria provide the clearest available evidence of change in this self-study. Memory alone (B), a final score without a comparable baseline (C), or two incompatible rubrics (D) cannot isolate a defensible improvement claim.

**2. B.** At 80 beats per minute, 256 beats take 256/80 = 3.2 minutes, or 3 minutes 12 seconds. Two minutes (A) counts only 160 beats, while four minutes (C) and five minutes (D) assume different tempos than the stated 80 BPM.

**3. C.** An executable, clearly labeled count-and-movement score can document a proposed choreography when a video is unavailable. A performance claim without evidence (A), an outline missing executable instructions (B), or uncredited copying (D) does not meet that standard.

**4. D.** A self-directed capstone can produce a portfolio (A), process record (B), and artist statement (C), but none confers an accredited professional qualification. Distinguish the genuine artifacts earned from credentials that require an authorized accrediting process.

**Self-review:** Correct any missed concept in a sentence that names the evidence and its limitation. These are original knowledge checks, not an external examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit the lesson's specified artifact, a 250–350-word explanation of one artistic decision, and a dated revision record. **Lesson 04 final capstone:** a **2–4 minute original work** with a complete count score, dated draft and final versions, rehearsal journal, artist statement, staging plan, critical reflection, permissions and attribution notes, and either a private performance recording or a clearly labeled unperformed score.

**Worked example:** “My first draft returned to center on count 8, obscuring the planned stillness. In the final score I moved the return to 7 and kept 8 still. Two recorded trials show the revised count placement, though they do not establish universal audience response.”

**Rubric:** Original choreographic structure and executable score 25%; documented revision and rehearsal process 25%; evidence-based critique and academic integrity 25%; safe presentation, attribution, and professional communication 25%. The score is a self-assessment, not accreditation.
