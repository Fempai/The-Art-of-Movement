## Learning goal and explanation

Connect familiar slides, circles, and accents without rushing or adding unlearned movements. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** A transition is the purposeful connection between actions, not a requirement for speed.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** Counts 1–2 small slide right and center; 3–4 small slide left and center; 5–6 trace a small half-circle; 7–8 settle with two gentle rhythmic accents.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Stay seated and link a hand-traced slide to a hand-traced circle.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the adapted phrase twice at a steady count, identify one observable change, and explain one limitation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Which action occupies counts 5–6 in the original eight-count phrase?**

A. Two lateral slides

B. Two alternating knee accents

C. A small half-circle

D. A stationary hold before the accents

**2. The learner rushes from the left slide into the half-circle. What is the best correction?**

A. Practice only the half-circle without the preceding left slide

B. Mark counts 3–6 slowly and check the connection

C. Reassign the half-circle to counts 4–6 without documenting the change

D. Repeat the full phrase at the same speed, focusing only on the final accents

**3. Which seated variation still tests the lesson's transition objective?**

A. A hand-traced slide connected to a hand-traced circle

B. A hand-traced slide followed by a pause, without the circle

C. Two hand-traced circles without a connecting slide

D. A seated slide on counts 1–4 and unrelated arm accents on counts 5–8

**4. What establishes a successful beginner transition?**

A. Completing the movements in the right order while repeatedly missing the transition count

B. A smooth transition whose count timing cannot be repeated

C. A repeatable large half-circle that interrupts the planned support change

D. A deliberate connection on the intended counts with comfortable support

### Answer key and explanations

**1. C.** The guided phrase specifies a small half-circle on counts 5–6, between the lateral slides and final accents.

**2. B.** Practicing the exact boundary between slide and circle makes the timing and support of the transition observable.

**3. A.** Linking the two hand-traced pathways retains the sequence and connection without requiring standing pelvic motion.

**4. D.** The intended measure is an organized, repeatable connection between known actions; speed and historical attribution are separate claims.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
