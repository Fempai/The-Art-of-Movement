# PRO702 · Lesson 03 — Costume and Prop Safety: Design for Movement

**Stagecraft & Artistic Direction | independent production study | 50–70 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly passage verification, expert production review, and live browser testing remain pending.

## Production principle

**Outcomes:** Explain a stagecraft variable, plan it with a diagram or cue sheet, test an observable effect safely, and document a revision.

A performance costume must support the movement score as well as the visual concept. Loose hems can catch underfoot, restrictive fabric can limit reaching or bending, accessories can detach, and slippery soles can change traction. A prop introduces additional collision and handling risks. Costume evaluation should be task-specific: walking, gathering, reaching, turning, and recovering can reveal problems before a full run. A design can be expressive without requiring heavy, sharp, breakable, or unstable elements. The safest introductory production uses familiar clothing and no props; do not test theatrical effects without qualified oversight.

## Guided production laboratory

**Prepare:** Use a level, clear, permitted rehearsal area. Keep exits unobstructed. Do not install lighting equipment, alter wiring, climb rigging, or use hazardous props.

**Plan and test (20–30 minutes):** Choose two ordinary outfits suitable for exercise, or compare one outfit with a written alternative if changing is inconvenient. Make a checklist: hem clear of feet, secure sleeves, nonrestrictive shoulders, secure accessories, suitable footwear, no loose objects. Perform four slow movements in each: step, gather, diagonal reach, small turn. Record any restriction, snag, or traction change. Modify the design concept instead of forcing movement through restrictive clothing. For a hypothetical prop, draw its location and transfer points on the blocking map without handling it during rehearsal.

**Troubleshooting and safety:** If a hem catches, shorten or change the garment before full-speed movement. If shoes slip, use appropriate stable footwear or change the surface and test gently. Do not substitute appearance for a verified movement-safety check.

**Undercurrent application:** Link each staging choice to the original step–gather–reach–recover score. Record what the audience might be able to see, but distinguish a visibility prediction from tested reception.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is the primary costume test?**

A. Whether it safely permits the actual movement score

B. Whether the costume creates the intended silhouette in a still photograph

C. Whether the costume color coordinates with the lighting palette

D. Whether the costume looks polished while standing still, without a movement test

**2. Which is a common costume hazard?**

A. A fitted sleeve tested through the complete reach without catching

B. A loose hem catching underfoot

C. A securely attached accessory checked through the planned turns

D. A hem shortened to clear the feet during the entire phrase

**3. How should an untested prop be handled in a beginner plan?**

A. Include the prop in the full-speed phrase before testing grip or clearance

B. Add a toss without checking where the prop could land

C. Keep it hypothetical until risks are assessed

D. Test the prop first in dim lighting without checking the pathway

**4. What should happen if an outfit restricts a safe reach?**

A. Keep the restrictive sleeve and push the arm beyond the comfortable range

B. Preserve the original costume despite repeated interference with the reach

C. Speed up the reach to move through the restriction before it catches

D. Revise the outfit or movement design

### Correct answers and explanations

**1. A.** Function must be tested against the choreography.

**2. B.** Loose fabric can interfere with steps.

**3. C.** Prop safety requires prior planning and testing.

**4. D.** Design should adapt to safe movement.

**Self-review:** For any missed item, revise the relevant blocking, lighting, or risk-control entry. These original questions check planning concepts, not technical certification.

## Independent assignment

Submit a diagram or cue sheet, a safe-test log, and a 250–350-word rationale identifying one limitation and one design revision. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a **production book** for a 2-minute original Undercurrent showing, including an artistic statement, 160-count section score at 80 BPM, blocking map, lighting cues, costume checks, operator assignments, rehearsal schedule, permissions status, risk controls, and post-run reflection.

**Worked example:** “The side-facing reach was obscured by the torso from the proposed front-left view. I changed the facing slightly on count 5 and updated the blocking map. I have not tested reception with a live audience.”

**Rubric:** Technical documentation and cue clarity 30%; safe and executable design 30%; artistic coherence 20%; reflective testing, permissions, and limitations 20%. A production book does not establish venue approval or legal compliance.
