# SIT505 · Lesson 03 — Accessibility: Design Multiple Ways to Experience the Work

**Site-Specific & Cultural Performance | graduate-level independent study | 45–65 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Source verification, site-specific safety review, and browser testing remain outstanding. No public-space access or public performance is required.

## Lecture: site-responsive reasoning

**Learning goals:** Describe a site constraint, transform a movement choice in response, document access and permission limits, and distinguish verified context from artistic speculation.

Accessibility is a design responsibility, not a last-minute accommodation or a claim that one modification serves everyone. Consider accessible routes, seating options, sightlines, sensory conditions, communication formats, and whether participation is optional. A seated movement variation can preserve choreographic relationships such as gather–reach–recover, but it is not automatically equivalent for every performer. Accessibility needs vary, so plans should be tested with relevant participants when appropriate and feasible, with consent and without placing the burden on them to solve all design problems. Avoid describing an unverified site as ADA-compliant or universally accessible.

## Guided site-design laboratory

**Prepare:** Use only a private or explicitly permitted, level, unobstructed practice area. Never block exits, rehearse in traffic, or test uninspected elevated surfaces.

**Investigate and choreograph (20–25 minutes):** Create two versions of the eight-count Undercurrent phrase: standing version with a small step on 1–2; seated version with a comfortable weight shift or hand initiation on 1–2. Keep gather 3–4, reach 5–6, return 7, and stillness 8. Write what stays recognizable and what changes. On the site map, mark a step-free entry if verified, a possible seated viewing area, and an unobstructed exit. List three information formats for a hypothetical event: plain-language written description, visual route map, and spoken or captioned introduction. Mark all unverified features as 'needs confirmation.'

**Troubleshoot:** If a route's step-free status is unknown, do not mark it accessible. If seated movement causes discomfort, reduce the arm pathway or use an alternative chosen by the performer. Avoid making medical assumptions about participants.

**Undercurrent transfer:** Write a count-level step–gather–reach–recover score with the site's relevant orientation and a safer seated or reduced-travel alternative. Document which design choices respond to the site and which remain artistic preferences.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is an appropriate accessibility claim?**

A. A floor appears level in photographs, so the full arrival route is accessible

B. Step-free entry requires confirmation before advertising it

C. A seated version is available, so it will suit every participant without further input

D. A single ramp shown on a plan proves all audience access needs are met

**2. Which change preserves the motif's structure?**

A. Replace the whole phrase with an unrelated gesture while calling the motif unchanged

B. Retain the step only by requiring a jump from every performer

C. Replace the step with a seated shift while retaining gather–reach–recover

D. Preserve the exact standing footwork even when the performer uses a seated option

**3. Which is a useful communication option?**

A. A plain-language route description and visual map

B. Provide only a site name and assume visitors can find a suitable route

C. Provide a route video without captions or a written alternative

D. Share access details only at the entrance, after travel decisions are made

**4. What should a planner do about unverified access features?**

A. Describe the route as fully step-free based solely on an unverified diagram

B. Treat the presence of an access symbol as proof of legal compliance

C. Omit uncertain entry features from the plan while advertising easy access

D. Label them for confirmation before making promises

### Correct answers and explanations

**1. B.** Step-free entry is a factual access claim that needs confirmation before publication. A level-looking photo (A), one seated adaptation (C), or a ramp symbol on a plan (D) cannot establish the accessibility of the complete route for every visitor.

**2. C.** A seated weight shift can preserve the gather–reach–recover relationship while changing the physical action to suit the performer. An unrelated replacement (A), compulsory jumping (B), and standing-only footwork (D) do not provide the stated accessible adaptation.

**3. A.** A plain-language route description together with a visual map provides complementary ways to plan arrival. Only naming the site (B), providing an uncaptioned video alone (C), or delaying access information until arrival (D) leaves avoidable information barriers.

**4. D.** Flag unverified access features for confirmation rather than promising what has not been checked. An unverified diagram (A), a symbol mistaken for legal compliance (B), and omission of uncertain entry conditions while advertising easy access (C) all risk misleading visitors.

**Feedback cycle:** For each missed question, distinguish a verified condition, a design hypothesis, and an unsupported assumption. These are original concept checks, not a formal accessibility or permitting assessment.

## Independent assignment

Submit a labeled site sketch, a movement score, a constraints-and-decisions table, and a 250–350-word reflection. Identify at least two uncertainties and how you would verify them. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a **site-specific proposal** including artistic rationale, 32-count score, site and audience map, accessibility and safety plan, permission status, cultural-context source ledger, and contingency plan.

**Worked example:** “The room's side window creates glare toward the front-facing performer. I rotated the reach toward the interior wall on counts 5–6 and kept the gather unchanged. This is a design response to observed light, not proof that every spectator will prefer it.”

**Rubric:** Site-responsive movement design 25%; safe and legible spatial plan 25%; accessibility and permissions transparency 30%; cultural-context research and reflective justification 20%. No points for unverified claims of legal compliance.
