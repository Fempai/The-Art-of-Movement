# FLE101 · Lesson 02 — Adductor Mobility and Controlled Sideways Range

**Active Flexibility Foundations | 35–45 minutes | independent study.** This is a learner-facing revision, not a medical evaluation or a certified stretching prescription. Stop for sharp pain, tingling, numbness, dizziness, or unusual symptoms. Do not use forced splits, partner pressure, or aggressive stretching.

## Learning goals and anatomical reasoning

Explain the relevant movement concept, demonstrate a comfortable version, distinguish active control from passive range, and document observable progress without claiming that a particular range is necessary for dance.

The hip adductors generally bring the thigh toward the body's midline, although their functions depend on joint position and individual muscle. A wide stance or lateral shift may create a stretching sensation along the inner thigh, but more width is not automatically better. Hip joint anatomy varies, and forced straddle positions can be uncomfortable or harmful. This lesson uses modest lateral shifts and controlled returns to develop awareness of available range. A dancer should distinguish the sensation of gentle muscular tension from sharp joint pain.

## Guided mobility studio

**Follow along (10–15 minutes):** Warm up by walking and taking small side steps for three minutes. Stand with feet slightly wider than hip-width, toes pointing in a comfortable direction. Hold a stable counter or rail. Shift weight right by gently bending the right knee while keeping the left foot on the floor; return to center. Shift left and return. Perform six alternating shifts without forcing a deep lunge. Then take one small step to the right and bring the other foot toward it over counts 1–4; reverse over counts 5–8. For seated marking, move one knee gently outward and back without pushing on it with the hands.

**Troubleshooting and safety:** If the knee collapses inward during a shift, reduce depth and track it comfortably over the foot. If the inner thigh feels sharply painful, stop and do not increase stance width. If you cannot return to center smoothly, make the shift smaller and use stable hand support.

**Apply to Undercurrent:** Perform a comfortable step–gather–reach–recover phrase for eight counts. Note whether you can move and recover without compensatory back arching, unstable support, or forced range. Keep your musical intention separate from the physical observation.

**Independent readiness check:** Can you enter and exit the selected range twice smoothly, breathe comfortably, and describe a specific modification? If not, reduce the demand. A self-report cannot rule out an underlying injury.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which action is most closely associated with hip adduction?**

A. Moving the thigh outward from the body's midline

B. Bringing the thigh toward the midline

C. Rotating the thigh outward while keeping its distance from the midline

D. Bending the hip to raise the thigh in front of the body

**2. Which is the most appropriate way to increase a beginner's lateral shift?**

A. Increase the shift until the pelvis tilts and control is lost

B. Keep the same painful endpoint but slow the return

C. Increase only within a controlled comfortable range

D. Increase the stance width while ignoring whether the knee tracks comfortably

**3. What can help with an unstable return to center?**

A. A smaller shift and fixed hand support

B. Keep the same unstable distance but shorten the return count

C. Increase the reach while removing the hand support

D. Keep the original range and attempt the return with a narrower base

**4. Which observation belongs in a progress log?**

A. I reached farther than a video demonstrator, without recording my own conditions

B. I used a wide stance, so the exercise guarantees injury prevention

C. My shift looked deep, but I did not record repetitions or support

D. I completed six controlled shifts without sharp pain

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Hip adduction moves the thigh toward the body's midline. A is abduction, C is rotation rather than movement toward the midline, and D primarily describes hip flexion.

**2. C.** Increase a lateral shift only while knee tracking, comfort, and return control remain reliable. A accepts lost control, B preserves a painful endpoint, and D widens the stance without monitoring alignment.

**3. A.** A smaller shift with fixed hand support reduces the balance demand of returning to center. B shortens timing while retaining instability, C removes support and increases reach, and D narrows the base without reducing the problematic range.

**4. D.** Recording six controlled shifts and the absence of sharp pain describes a reproducible practice observation, not a guarantee of safety. A compares unlike setups, B asserts universal injury prevention, and C omits repetitions and support conditions.

**Review protocol:** For a missed item, explain why the distractor is wrong, then demonstrate the relevant idea in a smaller range. These are original conceptual questions, not validated clinical assessments.

## Independent assignment: range and quality

Submit a dated adductor and lateral-range log: six alternating supported side shifts, knee tracking, stance width, return-to-center control, and the small step-and-gather in both directions. Document any seated modification, one correction, and a 200–300-word reflection explaining how controlled active sideways range differs from forcing a deeper stretch.

**Worked example:** “I used a fixed rail and completed six alternating side shifts. On the third right shift my knee moved inward, so I narrowed my stance and reduced depth. I could then return to center without pulling on the rail. I will repeat the same width before testing a larger movement; this does not prove I can safely perform a deep side lunge.”

**Rubric:** Side-shift and knee-tracking description 25%; safe range and support adaptation 25%; repeatable bilateral movement log 30%; interpretation and conservative progression rationale 20%. Do not award points for extreme range or pain tolerance.
