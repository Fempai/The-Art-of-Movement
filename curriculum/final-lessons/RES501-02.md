# RES501 · Lesson 02 — Source Quality: Match Claims to Evidence

**Graduate-level dance research methods | 50–70 minutes | independent study.** This learner-facing revision has not completed scholarly passage verification, expert review, or browser acceptance testing.

## Lecture: research reasoning

**Outcomes:** Define the method in this lesson, use it in a small inquiry, separate evidence from interpretation, and document the limits of any conclusion.

Source evaluation is not a contest to collect the greatest number of citations. It asks whether a source is relevant, credible for a particular claim, methodologically appropriate, and interpreted within its limits. A peer-reviewed biomechanics article can help explain physical mechanisms, but it cannot automatically validate a particular dancer's emotional interpretation. A practitioner interview is valuable evidence of that person's account, though it does not prove the experience was universal. Check author expertise, publication date, methods, participants or materials, conflicts of interest, and whether a claim comes from the findings or from an author's speculation. An abstract alone may omit crucial limitations.

## Guided research laboratory

**Preparation (5 minutes):** Create a dated research notebook. State the research question before gathering observations. If movement is involved, warm up gently, use a safe space, and choose comfortable small-range movements.

**Apply the method (20–25 minutes):** Make a two-source evidence matrix. Source A: a scholarly article on movement analysis, motor learning, or dance biomechanics. Source B: a practitioner account, interview, or documented rehearsal note. For each, record full citation, exact claim, evidence type, what it directly supports, what it cannot support, and a question for follow-up. Then compare with a first-person observation from your own Undercurrent rehearsal. Label each sentence 'source reports,' 'I observed,' or 'I infer.' If you cannot access a source's full text, mark it 'abstract-only' rather than claiming you reviewed the full study.

**Troubleshoot your reasoning:** If a citation links to a publisher homepage instead of a specific article or book, record that verification is incomplete. Do not invent page numbers or pretend a general source proves a precise historical claim.

**Connect to Undercurrent:** Treat the original step–gather–reach–recover phrase as a bounded study object. Describe its count score, the exact feature under investigation, and whether your evidence comes from rehearsal, video, testimony, or published research. Do not call a personal impression a universal result.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is the strongest reason to cite a source?**

A. It directly supports the specific claim being made

B. It has a long title

C. It is frequently shared

D. It is from the same decade as the choreography

**2. What can a practitioner interview directly document?**

A. Every dancer's experience

B. The interviewee's reported experience

C. A universal biomechanics law

D. The cause of every injury

**3. How should an abstract-only source be described?**

A. As a fully reviewed paper

B. As verified evidence for every detail

C. As an abstract-only reading with limitations

D. As proof of the author's private intentions

**4. What is a warning sign in a bibliography?**

A. An author and date

B. A traceable DOI

C. A quoted page number checked against the text

D. A generic homepage presented as a verified article link

### Answer key and explanations

**1. A.** Claim-specific relevance matters more than appearance.

**2. B.** Testimony supports what that practitioner reports.

**3. C.** Access limitations should be explicit.

**4. D.** A generic homepage does not establish a claim-specific citation.

**Feedback cycle:** Explain why each missed alternative overreaches or misidentifies evidence. Then revise one claim in your notebook. These original questions check concepts, not graduate credit or standardized research competence.

## Independent assignment

Submit a research artifact specific to this lesson (question matrix, evidence matrix, observation log, or proposal) and a 250–350-word methodological reflection. State at least two limitations and one change you would make before repeating the inquiry. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a 600–800-word **research proposal** with a question, sources, procedure, operational definitions, analysis plan, reflexivity, ethics, and limits.

**Worked example:** “I defined reach onset as the first visible outward hand motion after the gather. In trial 2, the hand was outside the frame, so onset was coded 'not observable.' I will widen the camera framing before collecting more trials. This adjustment improves documentation; it does not establish a causal relationship.”

**Rubric:** Question and method alignment 25%; evidence quality and traceability 30%; transparent limitations and reflexivity 25%; organization and justified next steps 20%. Do not award credit for unsupported causal claims or fabricated source details.
