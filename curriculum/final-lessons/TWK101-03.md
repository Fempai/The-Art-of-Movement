# TWK101 · Lesson 03 — Small-Range Bounce and Recovery

**Standalone twerk foundations | learner-facing written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** This is not a verified instructor-led video class.

## Objective
Sustain a shallow bounce for two eight-count phrases while distinguishing controlled return from an uncontrolled drop.

## Cultural and technique context
Modern twerking is strongly associated with Black social dance and New Orleans bounce culture. Similar-looking actions across traditions should not be labeled identical. This is an original teaching exercise, not a named historical step. Historical sourcing and practitioner review are still pending.

## Follow-along practice
Warm up with four minutes of easy steps, ankle motion, and small knee bends. Stand in a comfortable stance, feet grounded, knees unlocked. Counts 1,3,5,7: lower a few centimeters through ankles, knees and hips; counts 2,4,6,8: rise to the starting height without snapping the knees. Repeat two phrases, rest 30–60 seconds, then repeat twice. Next, keep the same action but accent the rising counts. Practice 10–15 minutes including rests; breathe throughout.

## Accessible adaptation
Hold a stable counter lightly for balance and reduce depth. Seated option: mark down/up rhythmic pressure through the feet with small torso changes; it retains timing but not standing loading.

## Self-correction and safety
Dropping heavily: slow down and reduce range. Heels lifting unintentionally: use smaller motion and distribute pressure. Knee discomfort: stop, rest, and choose the supported or seated version. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness or repeated loss of balance.

## Apply and reflect
Demonstrate or describe two phrases, one down-accented and one up-accented, identifying the return counts.

## Four-question knowledge check
**1. What should happen on counts 2, 4, 6 and 8 in the first drill?**

A. Drop lower than before

B. Return upward without locking the knees

C. Rotate the planted knee

D. Hold the breath

**Key: B.** Return upward without locking the knees.

**2. Which change creates an up-accented version?**

A. Move all accents to the lowering counts

B. Eliminate the return phase

C. Accent the rising counts while retaining the pattern

D. Increase depth until uncomfortable

**Key: C.** Accent the rising counts while retaining the pattern.

**3. Why pause between short phrases?**

A. To make the beat disappear

B. To prove maximum endurance

C. To avoid learning timing

D. To recover and preserve movement quality

**Key: D.** To recover and preserve movement quality.

**4. What does a heavy uncontrolled drop suggest?**

A. Reduce depth and slow the movement

B. Add speed

C. Lock the knees

D. Force lumbar extension

**Key: A.** Reduce depth and slow the movement.

## Completion checkpoint
Perform or notate two comfortable eight-count phrases; identify the movement initiator, one correction, and an adaptation. A written response does not prove physical proficiency.
