# PRO703 · Lesson 03 — Critical Self-Evaluation: Score, Evidence, and Counterargument

**Performance Portfolio & Capstone | independent final-year study | 60–90 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly audit, expert review, and live-browser acceptance testing remain outstanding. This is a nonaccredited self-study program.

## Capstone seminar

**Learning outcomes:** Produce a traceable creative decision, connect an artistic claim to movement evidence, document ethical attribution, and evaluate growth without inventing external validation.

Critical self-evaluation applies explicit criteria to a recorded or scored performance. The writer separates description (what occurred), evaluation (how it met the target), and interpretation (what the choice may communicate). A credible assessment includes strengths and weaknesses, a competing explanation, and a next-step plan. Self-evaluation is valuable for learning but susceptible to memory and expectation bias; using the same camera angle, count definitions, and score across attempts improves comparability without eliminating those biases. A rubric score is not an externally certified grade.

## Guided portfolio studio

**Prepare:** Use a clear, level, permitted space and a comfortable movement range. No dangerous inversions, lifts, or forced stretching are required.

**Create and document (30–40 minutes):** Create a four-column table for a 32-count Undercurrent performance: criterion, target, observation, revision. Use four criteria: count accuracy, controlled weight shifts, readable A/A′ contrast, and recovery at phrase boundaries. Record one private run or annotate a count score. Give each criterion 0 (not met), 1 (partly met), or 2 (met), but mark unobservable evidence as N/A rather than zero. Write 250 words defending one high score and one low score with counts. For the low score, propose a correction and a competing explanation such as camera occlusion or unfamiliar tempo.

**Troubleshooting and integrity:** If your assessment depends on a camera angle that hides the reach, change the recording setup before the next comparison. If the movement produces pain, stop and revise rather than awarding a lower artistic score for choosing safety. Keep aesthetic judgments separate from mechanical observations.

**Evidence discipline:** Label every artifact as planned, rehearsed, recorded, or performed; identify dates and criteria. Distinguish an observed improvement from an interpretation or a claim about long-term learning.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is an observation rather than a judgment?**

A. The return occurred on count 8 rather than 7

B. The late return proves the dancer was not trying to follow the score

C. The delayed count makes the phrase emotionally moving for all viewers

D. The return occurred late because the artist lacks musical ability

**2. How should a hidden movement be scored?**

A. Mark the invisible foot placement correct because the arm timing was accurate

B. As N/A or unobservable

C. Score the unseen foot placement as incorrect based on the next visible pose

D. Infer the dancer's motivation from a limb that is outside the frame

**3. What improves the quality of self-evaluation?**

A. Compare the latest take from memory while changing the rubric wording

B. Change the scoring criterion whenever a new attempt produces a lower score

C. Applying consistent criteria and considering alternatives

D. Review only the best take and exclude earlier attempts without explanation

**4. What is a legitimate conclusion from a self-rubric?**

A. A high self-score establishes readiness for any professional audition

B. The rubric result predicts how every audience member will respond

C. A consistent score confirms that the dancer has no injury risk

D. Performance against the declared criteria

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. A.** The count discrepancy is observable.

**2. B.** Unknown evidence is not a zero score.

**3. C.** Consistent criteria and competing explanations reduce overclaiming.

**4. D.** The rubric has a defined, limited scope.

**Self-review:** Correct any missed concept in a sentence that names the evidence and its limitation. These are original knowledge checks, not an external examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit the lesson's specified artifact, a 250–350-word explanation of one artistic decision, and a dated revision record. **Lesson 04 final capstone:** a **2–4 minute original work** with a complete count score, dated draft and final versions, rehearsal journal, artist statement, staging plan, critical reflection, permissions and attribution notes, and either a private performance recording or a clearly labeled unperformed score.

**Worked example:** “My first draft returned to center on count 8, obscuring the planned stillness. In the final score I moved the return to 7 and kept 8 still. Two recorded trials show the revised count placement, though they do not establish universal audience response.”

**Rubric:** Original choreographic structure and executable score 25%; documented revision and rehearsal process 25%; evidence-based critique and academic integrity 25%; safe presentation, attribution, and professional communication 25%. The score is a self-assessment, not accreditation.
