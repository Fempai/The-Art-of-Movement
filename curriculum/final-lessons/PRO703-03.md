# PRO703 · Lesson 03 — Critical Self-Evaluation: Score, Evidence, and Counterargument

**Performance Portfolio & Capstone | independent final-year study | 60–90 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly audit, expert review, and live-browser acceptance testing remain outstanding. This is a nonaccredited self-study program.

## Capstone seminar

**Learning outcomes:** Produce a traceable creative decision, connect an artistic claim to movement evidence, document ethical attribution, and evaluate growth without inventing external validation.

Critical self-evaluation applies explicit criteria to a recorded or scored performance. The writer separates description (what occurred), evaluation (how it met the target), and interpretation (what the choice may communicate). A credible assessment includes strengths and weaknesses, a competing explanation, and a next-step plan. Self-evaluation is valuable for learning but susceptible to memory and expectation bias; using the same camera angle, count definitions, and score across attempts improves comparability without eliminating those biases. A rubric score is not an externally certified grade.

## Guided portfolio studio

**Prepare:** Use a clear, level, permitted space and a comfortable movement range. No dangerous inversions, lifts, or forced stretching are required.

**Create and document (30–40 minutes):** Create a four-column table for a 32-count Undercurrent performance: criterion, target, observation, revision. Use four criteria: count accuracy, controlled weight shifts, readable A/A′ contrast, and recovery at phrase boundaries. Record one private run or annotate a count score. Give each criterion 0 (not met), 1 (partly met), or 2 (met), but mark unobservable evidence as N/A rather than zero. **Before scoring, define 2 as the criterion met throughout the relevant visible counts, 1 as partly met with at least one count-specific deviation, and 0 as a clearly observed failure; N/A is excluded from the denominator. Report earned points out of twice the number of observable criteria, not automatically out of eight.** Write 250 words defending one high score and one low score with counts. For the low score, propose a correction and a competing explanation such as camera occlusion or unfamiliar tempo.

**Troubleshooting and integrity:** If your assessment depends on a camera angle that hides the reach, change the recording setup before the next comparison. If the movement produces pain, stop and revise rather than awarding a lower artistic score for choosing safety. Keep aesthetic judgments separate from mechanical observations.

**Evidence discipline:** Label every artifact as planned, rehearsed, recorded, or performed; identify dates and criteria. Distinguish an observed improvement from an interpretation or a claim about long-term learning.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is an observation rather than a judgment?**

A. The late return proves the dancer was not trying to follow the score

B. The return occurred on count 8 rather than 7

C. The delayed count makes the phrase emotionally moving for all viewers

D. The return occurred late because the artist lacks musical ability

**2. How should a hidden movement be scored?**

A. Mark the invisible foot placement correct because the arm timing was accurate

B. As N/A or unobservable

C. Score the unseen foot placement as incorrect based on the next visible pose

D. Infer the dancer's motivation from a limb that is outside the frame

**3. What improves the quality of self-evaluation?**

A. Compare the latest take from memory while changing the rubric wording

B. Change the scoring criterion whenever a new attempt produces a lower score

C. Applying consistent criteria and considering alternatives

D. Review only the best take and exclude earlier attempts without explanation

**4. What is a legitimate conclusion from a self-rubric?**

A. A high self-score establishes readiness for any professional audition

B. The rubric result predicts how every audience member will respond

C. A consistent score confirms that the dancer has no injury risk

D. Performance against the declared criteria

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. B.** A return on count 8 rather than the prescribed count 7 is a checkable event if the relevant movement is visible and the counts are audible or otherwise established. Motivation (A), universal audience response (C), and talent (D) are interpretations or judgments not established by that timing observation.

**2. B.** Mark an obscured movement N/A or unobservable, and identify the camera limitation before retesting. A visible arm does not prove the hidden foot was correct (A); the next pose does not necessarily prove a technical failure (C); and camera framing cannot reveal motivation (D).

**3. C.** Apply the same rubric to comparable takes, include failed trials, and consider whether framing, fatigue, or a changed score explains differences. Memory-only comparison (A), revising criteria after each take (B), and excluding failures (D) undermine the reliability of the conclusion.

**4. D.** A self-rubric supports statements about performance against its explicitly declared criteria and the evidence actually observed. It does not establish professional audition readiness (A), universal approval (B), or clinical safety (C).

**Self-review:** Correct any missed concept in a sentence that names the evidence and its limitation. These are original knowledge checks, not an external examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit the four-column **criterion / target / observation / revision** table for one 32-count performance or clearly labeled score-only analysis. **For a performed run**, score count accuracy, controlled weight shifts, A/A′ contrast and phrase-boundary recovery as 0, 1 or 2 where actually observable, marking unobservable items N/A rather than 0. **For a score-only analysis**, evaluate only what the notation establishes (for example, prescribed count placement and a written A/A′ contrast); mark execution-dependent criteria such as actual weight-shift control and performed recovery N/A, and never report planned counts as observed execution. State the denominator as twice the number of legitimately scored criteria. Include a **250–350-word critique** defending one strong rating and, **if a legitimately scored criterion is weak**, one weak rating, each with specific counts. If the score-only route or available evidence yields no defensible weak rating, **state 'no observed weakness in scored criteria' rather than inventing a failure**; identify an untested execution-dependent criterion as a limitation, not a low score. Discuss a competing explanation for any observed weakness (or for uncertainty in the evidence) and propose a safe retest plan. If using video, disclose frame limitations and preserve the camera position for comparisons.

**Worked example:** “I rated return timing 1/2 because my visible arm recovered on count 8 rather than 7. I marked foot placement N/A because it was outside the frame. My next test isolates counts 7–8; a camera angle change could explain apparent differences, so I will document it.”

**Rubric:** Explicit criteria and honest N/A handling 30%; evidence-linked ratings and count references 30%; competing explanation and safe retest 25%; coherent critical argument 15%. This is self-assessment, not accreditation.
