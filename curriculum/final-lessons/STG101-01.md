# STG101 · Lesson 01 — Pulling Strength: Rows Before Pull-Ups

**Independent dance conditioning | 35–50 minutes.** This lesson is an authored learner-facing revision. Final citation, professional safety, and live-browser audits remain outstanding. Beginners should choose comfortable loads and stop for pain, dizziness, chest discomfort, or unusual shortness of breath. Seek qualified medical advice when symptoms warrant it.

## Learn: movement and training principle

Pulling strength supports shoulder control and upper-body coordination, but a pull-up is not a prerequisite for expressive dance. A pull-up moves the body toward a secure overhead bar; a row pulls the hands or elbows toward the torso against resistance. They differ in body orientation and loading. For a beginner training without a coach, a controlled row using a purpose-built machine or securely anchored resistance equipment is a more accessible baseline than an unsupported pull-up. Do not use doors, loose furniture, or improvised anchors. Scapular movement is natural; do not pin the shoulder blades rigidly down through every repetition.

**Training principle:** Repetition quality and repeatable conditions matter more than a dramatic single attempt. A self-directed website cannot assess individual injury risk, prescribe rehabilitation, or certify technique from a self-reported score.

## Guided conditioning lab

**Warm-up and practice:** Warm up with two minutes of easy walking and shoulder movements. At a gym, choose a seated cable-row machine, set light resistance, place feet securely, sit tall without forcing the spine, and pull the handles toward the lower ribs over roughly two seconds. Return with control over two seconds. Do 1 set of 6–10 comfortable repetitions, rest 60–90 seconds, and consider a second set if the first was smooth. Stop two or more repetitions before you expect technique to break down. At home without secure equipment, practice a standing unresisted elbow-draw for eight slow repetitions; label it coordination practice, not equivalent strength training.

**Observe and troubleshoot:** Count completed controlled repetitions, note resistance and perceived effort (0–10), and identify whether the torso swung to move the load. If you must lean backward sharply to finish, reduce resistance. If you feel pinching or sharp pain, stop; do not diagnose it from the exercise.

**Transfer to dance:** Perform one eight-count Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover motif in a comfortable range. Ask whether your breathing, posture, and transitions remain controllable. Conditioning supports the phrase; the phrase is not a test of maximal strength.

**Ready for progression?** You can complete the chosen version without pain, loss of control, or compensatory rushing, and can explain exactly which training variable you would change. Do not progress solely because a numerical repetition target was met.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which movement is a pulling exercise?**

A. Seated cable row

B. Calf raise

C. Squat

D. Standing march

**2. Why is an improvised door anchor a poor beginner option?**

A. It always reduces effort

B. Its stability and load rating may be unknown

C. It makes counting impossible

D. It changes music tempo

**3. What is the best response when the torso swings to finish a row?**

A. Add resistance

B. Move faster

C. Reduce load and restore control

D. Hold your breath

**4. What should a baseline log include?**

A. Only a motivational adjective

B. Only the date

C. Only the dancer's height

D. Reps, resistance, effort, and movement quality

### Answer key with explanations

**1. A.** The row pulls resistance toward the torso.

**2. B.** Unverified anchors can fail; use designed, secure equipment.

**3. C.** The compensation indicates the chosen resistance may exceed controlled capacity.

**4. D.** Repeatable measures and quality notes support later comparisons.

**Self-review:** Correct answers test the stated training distinctions. For a missed question, explain why the chosen distractor is incorrect and repeat the relevant demonstration with a comfortable modification. These questions are not a validated health or fitness test.

## Independent assignment

Complete a personal baseline record: date, exercise variation, equipment/support, repetitions or work/rest duration, perceived effort (0–10), one visible quality cue, one modification, and one next-step decision. Write 200–300 words distinguishing observations from interpretations. For Lesson 04, submit a combined **personal baseline log** covering pulling, pushing, trunk control, and easy dance intervals, including any exercises not attempted and why.

**Worked example:** “I performed eight wall push-ups at an easy perceived effort of 4/10. My elbows moved comfortably, and I completed each repetition without stopping. Next time I will keep the wall distance unchanged and compare controlled repetitions; this record does not prove I can perform a floor push-up.”

**Rubric:** Accurate technique description 25%; safe and honest adaptation 25%; repeatable baseline data 30%; interpretation and progression rationale 20%. A self-grade is not a clinical or professional assessment.
