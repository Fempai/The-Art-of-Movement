## Learning goal and explanation

Explore gentle abdominal awareness before attempting visible rolling actions; avoid forcing the spine or breath. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** An abdominal awareness drill is preparation, not proof that a complete belly roll has been learned.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** Begin in a supported standing or seated position. Counts 1–2: notice an easy, unforced breath; **do not require an inhale to last exactly two counts**. Count 3: notice a very gentle abdominal engagement, without bracing or holding the breath; count 4: release the engagement fully while continuing to breathe. Counts 5–6: optionally mark a tiny comfortable torso wave, or remain neutral and continue breathing; counts 7–8: settle in a relaxed neutral position. If your natural breathing cycle does not match the musical counts, prioritize comfortable breathing and mark the counts with a hand instead. The optional torso wave is not a demonstrated belly roll. Do not hold your breath, brace forcefully, or push the abdomen outward against discomfort.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Remain seated and practice a comfortable breath-linked release without attempting a visible belly roll.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the adapted phrase twice at a steady count, identify one observable change, and explain one limitation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Which action belongs on counts 3–4 of the preparation drill?**

A. Hold a gentle abdominal engagement throughout counts 3–4 without releasing

B. Gently engage and release while breathing naturally

C. Move directly into the optional wave on counts 3–4

D. Repeat the inhale-exhale preparation from counts 1–2 without the engage-and-release

**2. A learner cannot comfortably mark the tiny wave on counts 5–6. What is appropriate?**

A. Skip counts 5–6 and describe the phrase as if the optional wave had been completed

B. Practice a shorter wave at the same uncomfortable range

C. Omit the wave and continue comfortable breathing awareness

D. Replace the wave with a larger pelvis-driven motion

**3. Which adaptation preserves the core lesson without a visible roll?**

A. Seated comfortable breath-linked release

B. Seated breathing with a held abdominal contraction rather than a gentle release

C. Seated hand-tracing of a large torso wave without the breath-linked release

D. Standing repetition of the optional wave while omitting the preparatory release

**4. After completing the exercise, what can the learner claim?**

A. They have repeated a gentle abdominal preparation, which demonstrates a complete visible belly roll

B. Their method matches the source video's precise abdominal technique without a source comparison

C. Their comfortable practice at one tempo confirms readiness for advanced flutters

D. They practiced an introductory abdominal-awareness sequence

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Counts 3–4 call for a gentle abdominal engage-and-release with natural breathing. A omits release, C moves the optional wave into the wrong counts, and D repeats the earlier breathing preparation without the target action.

**2. C.** Omit the optional wave and continue comfortable breath awareness if the movement is not tolerable. A falsely reports an omitted action as completed, B preserves the discomfort, and D adds a larger motion.

**3. A.** A seated, comfortable breath-linked release preserves the lesson's preparatory focus. B holds contraction instead of releasing, C substitutes a large hand pathway, and D insists on the optional standing wave.

**4. D.** The learner can claim practice of the introductory abdominal-awareness sequence, not a complete belly roll. A overstates skill, B asserts unverified video correspondence, and C infers advanced flutter readiness from one practice tempo.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
