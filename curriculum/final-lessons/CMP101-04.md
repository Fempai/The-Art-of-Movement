# CMP101 · Lesson 04 — Repetition, Contrast, and an Annotated 32-Count Score

**Independent composition study | 40–55 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; source metadata, live links, and browser acceptance remain pending. The site serves as your teacher for these foundational tasks, but no self-score guarantees how an outside viewer will interpret a work.

## Concept and learning outcomes

After this lesson you should be able to define the key compositional idea, apply it to an original phrase, record it precisely enough to repeat, and explain what the notation does and does not show.

Repetition creates a relationship between movement events by returning to something recognizable. Contrast introduces a deliberate difference in direction, timing, level, energy, or body organization. A–B–A′–C is one compositional pattern: A states an idea, B contrasts it, A′ returns in variation, and C provides a resolution or further change. It is a useful classroom structure, not a universal rule. A notation system must make the choreography reproducible; the score used here is an original plain-language count notation, not a claim to be Labanotation or Benesh Movement Notation. Established systems have specialized conventions that require separate training.

**Observation versus intention:** You can verify that an arm reaches on count five. You cannot prove that every viewer reads that reach as longing. Write the intended effect separately from the count and pathway.

## Guided studio and writing lab

**Preparation (3–5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip space, warm up with gentle steps or seated torso shifts, and decide which direction is the front of the room. Use a small comfortable range; avoid painful or unstable movement.

**Follow this score (10–15 minutes):** Write 32 counts in four phrases. A (1–8): 1 step right, 2 settle, 3–4 gather hands, 5–6 diagonal reach, 7 lower, 8 recover. B (9–16): 9 step left, 10 settle, 11–12 turn a small comfortable diagonal, 13–14 extend both arms horizontally, 15 lower, 16 return facing front. A′ (17–24): 17 step right, 18 settle, 19–20 gather, 21–23 sustain the diagonal reach one count longer, 24 recover. C (25–32): 25–26 shift left, 27–28 shift right, 29 reach forward, 30 gather, 31 lower, 32 finish. Mark support foot, facing, and one accent in each phrase. Seated marking may replace steps with shifts in torso orientation.

**Compare and revise:** A score that says 'dance dramatically' is insufficient for reconstruction. A score that says '21–23 right arm reaches northeast while weight remains right' is specific enough to test and revise. The latter still cannot guarantee identical expression across performers.

**Undercurrent connection:** Use the existing step–gather–reach–recover motif as material. Identify what you kept, changed, and why. Record two takes or write two complete count sheets. A single photograph of the endpoint is insufficient to document timing or support.

**Self-check:** Can you perform the phrase twice with the same counts and orientation? Does your score identify the support foot and the pathway? If you cannot reconstruct it, revise the notation before increasing complexity.

## Knowledge check

**1. What does A′ signify in an A–B–A′–C pattern?**

A. An identical repeat of A with no alteration in timing or pathway

B. A varied return of the A material

C. A second contrasting B phrase that does not refer back to A

D. A transition that removes all identifying features of the original A motif

**2. Which change creates a clear contrast?**

A. Repeat the diagonal reach with the same duration and direction

B. Replace a sustained diagonal reach with a short horizontal extension

C. Keep the reach direction and timing identical but change only its name

D. Repeat the reach at the same tempo and range with no changed movement feature

**3. What is the status of the lesson's count notation?**

A. An original plain-language teaching score

B. A formal notation system simply because the score includes counts and facing

C. A standardized movement notation because it identifies support and pathway

D. A verified measurement of joint angles because it describes movement direction

**4. Which item is essential to a reproducible 32-count score?**

A. Counts and a mood adjective without support-foot information

B. Start and end photographs without the intervening weight transfers

C. Counts, weight support, facing, and movement pathway

D. A music title and count total without movement directions or facing

### Answer key and rationale

**1. B.** A-prime revisits the original idea with a meaningful alteration.

**2. B.** Changing direction and duration provides a discernible contrast.

**3. A.** The score is intentionally simplified and does not claim to be a formal notation system.

**4. C.** Explicit timing and spatial information allow another attempt at reconstruction.

**Retry:** Revisit any missed concept, annotate the movement example, and answer again without looking. These are instructor-authored conceptual checks, not externally validated tests of choreographic talent.

## Independent assignment and rubric

Create an annotated composition score. Include count numbers, action verbs, support foot or seated support, facing, pathway, at least one intentional change, and a 250–350-word critical reflection. For Lesson 04, submit a complete **32-count A–B–A′–C score**.

**Worked example:** “Counts 3–4: both hands gather toward the sternum; weight remains on the right foot. Counts 5–6: right arm reaches northeast. In A′, the reach lasts through count 7. The duration changed; my intended meaning is an interpretation, not an objective property of the score.”

**Rubric:** Structural clarity 25%; precise, reproducible notation 25%; intentional variation or contrast 25%; critical reflection and source awareness 25%. A self-assessment should identify a concrete revision, not merely give a high score.
