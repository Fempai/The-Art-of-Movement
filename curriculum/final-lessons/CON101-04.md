# CON101 · Lesson 04 — Floor-to-Stand Transitions and Level Changes

**Self-directed contemporary dance | 35–50 minutes including movement, retrieval, and reflection.** No instructor or barre required for the foundational version. Advanced floorwork, inversions, falls, and painful movements require qualified instruction. Live browser acceptance and claim-level source auditing are still pending.

## Learning goal and core explanation

After this lesson, explain the concept, demonstrate a controlled low-risk version, compare two intentional choices, and document a movement phrase.

Floorwork involves choreographic relationships with the floor, including supported seated, kneeling, rolling, and traveling actions. Beginners should first learn planned changes in support and level rather than uncontrolled drops, rolls across the neck, or fast inversions. A floor-to-stand pathway can be studied as a sequence of stable contact points: floor or chair, hands if appropriate, feet, and upright stance. There is no single safe pathway for every body. Knee comfort, mobility, strength, floor traction, and medical constraints affect what is suitable. A seated-to-standing alternative can teach the same principles without kneeling.

**Think like a choreographer:** What do you actually observe—timing, direction, weight, contact points—and what are you inferring, such as the dancer's emotion or intention? A strong interpretation states its evidence and acknowledges alternatives.

## Follow-along studio instruction

**Warm up for 3–5 minutes:** Walk or march gently, move shoulders and ankles through comfortable ranges, and practice an easy weight shift. Clear the floor, avoid slippery socks or moving furniture, and keep a fixed support available if needed. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided eight-count study:** Use a clear, nonslip space and a secure support. Start seated on a stable, nonrolling chair with feet flat. On counts 1–2, bring your feet beneath you and lean forward comfortably from the hips; on 3–4, press through your feet and rise with support if needed; on 5–6, stand tall without locking knees; on 7–8, sit back down slowly, using your hands on a stable surface if needed. Repeat twice. Optional floor version only if kneeling is comfortable and you can rise independently: from a high kneel on a padded nonslip mat, step one foot forward into a supported half-kneel, use a fixed support to stand, and reverse carefully. Do not attempt this floor version if you have knee pain, poor balance, or cannot exit safely.

**Before starting:** Confirm the chair will not slide or tip and that you can stand up and sit down comfortably. Keep the chair directly behind you for the return; feel the seat against the backs of your legs before lowering, and do not drop onto it. If you cannot control the return, stay seated and mark the level change with a small torso rise and recovery. The optional kneeling variation is not required to pass this lesson.

**Independent practice:** Perform the sequence once while speaking counts, once while observing the initiation or support pathway, and once with a deliberate change of timing. If a version becomes unstable, reduce the range or use the seated alternative.

**Undercurrent application:** In a floorwork phrase, the expressive transition is not the fall itself; it is the deliberate pathway of support and timing. A seated-to-stand sequence can communicate a change in level while remaining accessible. Use the original step–gather–reach–recover motif, then change only one variable associated with this lesson. Write down the eight counts and describe the difference without claiming a universal audience reaction.

## Troubleshooting without a teacher

| What happens | Possible explanation, not diagnosis | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| You cannot rise without pulling on unstable furniture | The support or strength demand may be unsuitable | Use a secure fixed support or remain with seated marking |
| Kneeling produces knee discomfort | The surface or joint position may not be tolerable | Use the chair version and avoid kneeling |
| You rush the return to sitting | You may be losing control of the descent | Reduce tempo and use your hands on a stable surface |

**Ready to move on when:** You can repeat the comfortable version twice at a steady count, name the supporting surface or foot, and explain one deliberate choreographic change. This is a learning checkpoint, not medical clearance.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is the central beginner floorwork principle?**

A. Reach the floor quickly before identifying the next support point

B. Plan and control changes in support and level

C. Transfer weight to a new surface before confirming a stable exit

D. Maintain the same support points while changing only the arm shape

**2. Which alternative preserves the level-change concept if kneeling hurts?**

A. A deeper kneeling descent while keeping the painful contact

B. A fast unsupported descent to the floor and back

C. Seated-to-standing practice from a stable chair

D. A standing arm reach that changes height but not body support

**3. When should a beginner avoid the optional kneeling sequence?**

A. When they cannot exit it comfortably and independently

B. When the learner can enter and exit the kneeling position with stable, comfortable support

C. When the learner can coordinate the support transfer and repeat a controlled exit

D. When the learner has comfortable knee contact and a controlled, independently repeatable exit

**4. What is a useful observation to record?**

A. Whether the dancer's chosen support points guarantee safety for every body

B. Whether the dancer feels confident even when contact points cannot be seen

C. Whether a successful exit proves that the same pathway is appropriate for all learners

D. Which surfaces support the body at each transition

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Controlled support transitions are a foundational learning objective.

**2. C.** The chair variation teaches level change without requiring knee contact.

**3. A.** An exit strategy and comfortable movement are prerequisites for the optional floor version.

**4. D.** Contact points and timing are directly observable.

**Self-grading:** Four correct indicates understanding of the introductory distinctions tested here. For a missed answer, revisit its explanation and demonstrate the related idea at a smaller range before retrying. These original questions are not externally validated examinations.

## Independent assignment

Create two eight-count versions of the same phrase. Record the count, initiating segment, direction, support, and intended effect. Write 250–350 words comparing the versions, identifying one observable difference, one interpretation, and one limitation. Include at least one relevant reference and explain what that source can and cannot support.

**Worked example:** “In version A, I began seated with both feet on the floor, shifted my weight forward over counts 1–2, stood with the help of a stable support over 3–4, and sat down slowly over 7–8. In version B, I remained seated and performed a small forward-and-back weight shift instead. I can compare the contact points and timing of the two versions; I cannot infer that either version is safe for every person.”

**Rubric:** Concept and terminology 25%; accurate movement score 25%; comparison and critical reasoning 25%; source use and revision 25%. Written grades do not establish the safety or quality of unsupervised advanced technique.
