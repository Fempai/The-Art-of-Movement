# PRO702 · Lesson 04 — Artistic Direction: Assemble a Production Book

**Stagecraft & Artistic Direction | independent production study | 50–70 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Scholarly passage verification, expert production review, and live browser testing remain pending.

## Production principle

**Outcomes:** Explain a stagecraft variable, plan it with a diagram or cue sheet, test an observable effect safely, and document a revision.

Artistic direction coordinates choreography, visual design, rehearsal decisions, and communication into a coherent performance plan. A production book records choices so they can be repeated and revised: artistic concept, movement score, blocking map, cue sheet, costume notes, rehearsal calendar, risk controls, and post-run evaluation. A solo artist still benefits from clearly separating the roles of creator, performer, observer, and production manager. A cue should name its trigger and responsible operator; if no operator is available, choose a static design rather than relying on unsafe multitasking. A production book is a planning artifact, not evidence that a public event has been approved.

## Guided production laboratory

**Prepare:** Use a level, clear, permitted rehearsal area. Keep exits unobstructed. Do not install lighting equipment, alter wiring, climb rigging, or use hazardous props.

**Plan and test (20–30 minutes):** Assemble a production book for a 2-minute Undercurrent showing. At 80 beats per minute, five 32-count sections total 160 beats and 120 seconds. Use sections: introduction, original motif, directional variation, expressive contrast, and resolution. For each, record count range, stage position, facing, light state, costume/prop state, and safety check. Include a cue sheet with columns cue ID, trigger, action, operator, fallback; a rehearsal schedule with warm-up and rest; a permission status field; and a final run report. Perform one comfortable private run if safe, or submit an annotated simulated cue-to-score walkthrough.

**Troubleshooting and safety:** If a cue requires switching lights while dancing, make lighting static or assign an authorized operator. If a full run causes pain or loss of control, stop and revise. If a site or music license is unconfirmed, label it pending and do not advertise the production as cleared.

**Undercurrent application:** Link each staging choice to the original step–gather–reach–recover score. Record what the audience might be able to see, but distinguish a visibility prediction from tested reception.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is a production book for?**

A. A polished program that describes the show but omits technical cues

B. A repeatable record of creative and technical decisions

C. A finished cue sheet that automatically establishes audience approval

D. A collection of designs that makes a separate risk review unnecessary

**2. At 80 beats/minute, how long are 160 beats?**

A. 80 seconds, treating the stated BPM as the duration in seconds

B. 100 seconds, calculating with a faster 96 BPM pulse

C. 120 seconds

D. 160 seconds, assuming every beat lasts one second

**3. What is a safe cue plan for a solo performer without an operator?**

A. Use static lighting rather than multitasking during movement

B. Change an unfamiliar lamp setting during a moving turn

C. Place lighting equipment in the exit pathway to improve the frame

D. Remove visibility lighting during travel to emphasize the cue

**4. What does a completed production book not prove?**

A. That the production book contains a written cue list

B. That the artist recorded proposed performer positions

C. That the book records revisions to lighting and costume decisions

D. That a public venue and all rights are approved

### Correct answers and explanations

**1. B.** It coordinates production choices and revisions.

**2. C.** 160/80 × 60 equals 120 seconds.

**3. A.** Static design avoids unsafe simultaneous tasks.

**4. D.** Planning does not substitute for permissions or licensing.

**Self-review:** For any missed item, revise the relevant blocking, lighting, or risk-control entry. These original questions check planning concepts, not technical certification.

## Independent assignment

Submit a diagram or cue sheet, a safe-test log, and a 250–350-word rationale identifying one limitation and one design revision. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a **production book** for a 2-minute original Undercurrent showing, including an artistic statement, 160-count section score at 80 BPM, blocking map, lighting cues, costume checks, operator assignments, rehearsal schedule, permissions status, risk controls, and post-run reflection.

**Worked example:** “The side-facing reach was obscured by the torso from the proposed front-left view. I changed the facing slightly on count 5 and updated the blocking map. I have not tested reception with a live audience.”

**Rubric:** Technical documentation and cue clarity 30%; safe and executable design 30%; artistic coherence 20%; reflective testing, permissions, and limitations 20%. A production book does not establish venue approval or legal compliance.
