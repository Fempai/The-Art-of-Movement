# SCI503 · Lesson 02 — Retrieval and Spacing: Remember Without the Score

**Motor Learning & Dance Science | graduate-level independent study | 45–60 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; source audit, expert review, and browser testing remain outstanding. Do not intentionally train to exhaustion or push through pain.

## Lecture: explain the learning mechanism

**Learning outcomes:** Distinguish observed performance from inferred learning, apply one defined practice method, collect repeatable movement observations, and identify what the data cannot prove.

Retrieval practice requires attempting a movement sequence from memory rather than merely repeating it while reading. Spaced practice separates retrieval attempts over time; immediate recall and delayed recall measure different things. A useful dance protocol can schedule checks one, three, and seven days after initial learning, while acknowledging that sleep, other training, and missed sessions affect interpretation. Repeatedly testing a phrase can itself strengthen memory, so a simple self-study cannot cleanly isolate spacing from retrieval. The purpose here is to develop a transparent personal learning log, not to make causal claims.

## Guided movement-science laboratory

**Prepare:** Warm up with gentle walking or seated weight shifts. Choose a safe, small-range version of the eight-count Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover motif.

**Protocol (15–20 minutes):** Read the eight-count Undercurrent score twice and mark it once at half speed. Hide the page and perform one retrieval attempt. Check four chunks: counts 1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–8. Record each as accurate, inaccurate, or uncertain, and note whether you peeked at the score. Correct errors, rest, and retrieve again. Schedule a single unprompted retrieval on day 1, day 3, and day 7 after the initial session; if a day is missed, log the actual elapsed interval rather than backdating. Keep movement small and comfortable. The later sessions are instructions for the learner, not completed measurements.

**Troubleshooting and limitations:** If you can recall only the arms, say the supporting foot aloud while marking the first two counts. If you peek at the score, mark the trial as prompted rather than unprompted. Do not practice through pain or fatigue just to satisfy the schedule.

**Research integrity check:** Record the planned trial count, actual trial count, condition, prompts, and deviations. Do not change scoring definitions after seeing outcomes without documenting the change. A solo log can help direct practice but cannot establish general causal effects.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which attempt is retrieval practice?**

A. Copying the phrase while the count sheet remains in view

B. Performing from memory before checking the score

C. Repeating the sequence immediately after reading it while still consulting the score

D. Reviewing the video again instead of attempting an unaided performance

**2. What should happen if the day-3 check occurs on day 4?**

A. Record the attempt as day 3 despite its later actual timestamp

B. Omit the delayed check because the planned schedule was missed

C. Record the actual elapsed time

D. Compare it as an on-time day-3 result without noting the deviation

**3. Which is the most informative memory record?**

A. Count-level accuracy and whether prompts were used

B. A confidence rating without noting which counts were recalled

C. A correct ending pose without documenting the preceding recall errors

D. A single total score without stating whether the count sheet was used

**4. What is a limitation of this self-study?**

A. The written score proves that all improvement was caused by retrieval alone

B. The delayed check rules out any effect of ordinary rehearsal between sessions

C. Improved recall automatically establishes which component of practice caused it

D. Retrieval and spacing effects are not experimentally isolated

### Answer key and explanation

**1. B.** Retrieval requires producing the sequence without the cue.

**2. C.** Accurate timestamps preserve the integrity of the log.

**3. A.** Specific accuracy and prompting data make comparisons meaningful.

**4. D.** The design combines several influences.

**Self-review:** Explain why any missed alternative confuses outcome with process, immediate performance with learning, or observation with causal inference. These are original teaching questions, not validated cognitive or clinical assessments.
