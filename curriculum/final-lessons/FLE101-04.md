# FLE101 · Lesson 04 — Safe Stretching and a Range-and-Quality Journal

**Active Flexibility Foundations | 35–45 minutes | independent study.** This is a learner-facing revision, not a medical evaluation or a certified stretching prescription. Stop for sharp pain, tingling, numbness, dizziness, or unusual symptoms. Do not use forced splits, partner pressure, or aggressive stretching.

## Learning goals and anatomical reasoning

Explain the relevant movement concept, demonstrate a comfortable version, distinguish active control from passive range, and document observable progress without claiming that a particular range is necessary for dance.

Flexibility describes available movement range; active mobility adds control within that range. A useful program may combine comfortable dynamic movement, low-intensity static stretching, and strength work, depending on the dancer and the task. No single stretching method is ideal for every body, and pain is not evidence of effectiveness. For a beginner, the goal is to observe whether a position can be entered and exited smoothly, with breathing and balance maintained. Measurements taken on different days can vary with fatigue, warm-up, and technique, so compare like with like and avoid interpreting a single number as a permanent change.

## Guided mobility studio

**Follow along (10–15 minutes):** Warm up five minutes with easy walking and small side steps. Perform one round of the earlier lessons **using their documented baselines**: Unit 1 supported split-stance hip shift **three repetitions per side**; Unit 2 gentle lateral shift **six alternating shifts total** (three each side); and Unit 3 seated knee extension **four repetitions per side**. Use the same comfortable setup and support recorded in those lessons, or explicitly document any changed conditions. Do not silently replace all three protocols with four repetitions per side. Afterward choose one comfortable static position, such as a modest standing calf stretch against a wall, and hold 15–20 seconds while breathing normally. Do not force joints or use a foot stretcher to push the ankle beyond control. Finish with an eight-count Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover phrase and note whether you can reach and return without changing your balance.

**Troubleshooting and safety:** If the stretch feels more intense with each repetition, reduce range or stop. If your balance changes during a reach, keep the feet planted and lower the arm. If a comparison seems inconsistent, note differences in warm-up, footwear, support, and fatigue rather than assuming your flexibility has permanently decreased.

**Apply to Undercurrent:** Perform a comfortable step–gather–reach–recover phrase for eight counts. Note whether you can move and recover without compensatory back arching, unstable support, or forced range. Keep your musical intention separate from the physical observation.

**Independent readiness check:** Can you enter and exit the selected range twice smoothly, breathe comfortably, and describe a specific modification? If not, reduce the demand. A self-report cannot rule out an underlying injury.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which statement best distinguishes flexibility from active mobility?**

A. A passive assisted position proves the same range can be controlled actively

B. Active mobility includes muscular control within a range

C. Active mobility is defined only by how far another person can move the limb

D. Flexibility and active mobility can be compared without considering muscle control

**2. Which journal entry is most useful?**

A. I felt tighter than yesterday, but did not note the task or conditions

B. My leg seemed higher, but I changed the warm-up and support without recording them

C. Four controlled lateral shifts after a five-minute warm-up

D. I reached my deepest position once but did not record whether it was controlled

**3. What is the appropriate response to pain during stretching?**

A. Stop or reduce the range

B. Hold the same painful angle while reducing only the breathing rate

C. Increase the stretch duration without changing the painful position

D. Repeat the painful range more slowly to finish the planned count

**4. Which comparison is most meaningful?**

A. The same leg lift with different support surfaces and unrecorded warm-ups

B. A one-time maximum position without a consistent starting point

C. Your passive range compared with another person's active range

D. The same comfortable task under similar conditions

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Active mobility includes producing and controlling motion with muscular effort. A assumes an assisted position guarantees active control, C defines mobility by someone else's assistance, and D ignores the defining control distinction.

**2. C.** Four controlled lateral shifts after a specified warm-up records both the task and a repeatable condition. A omits the task, B changes unrecorded support and preparation, and D records an uncontrolled one-time extreme.

**3. A.** Stop a painful stretch or reduce the range and reassess comfort; do not force through pain. B, C, and D all retain the painful position while changing breathing, duration, or speed. Seek professional advice for persistent or worsening symptoms.

**4. D.** Repeating a comfortable task under similar support and warm-up conditions makes changes more interpretable. A changes unrecorded conditions, B lacks a repeatable starting point, and C compares different people and different range types.

**Review protocol:** For a missed item, explain why the distractor is wrong, then demonstrate the relevant idea in a smaller range. These are original conceptual questions, not validated clinical assessments.

## Independent assignment: range and quality

Submit a combined range-and-quality journal with dated entries for supported split-stance hip shifts, lateral adductor shifts, seated knee extension, and the final comfortable static stretch and eight-count dance transfer. For each task record warm-up, support, repetitions or hold duration, range quality, breathing, symptoms, and modifications. Add a 200–300-word reflection comparing active control with static stretching and propose only one conservative change while keeping other conditions similar.

**Worked example:** “My journal records three supported hip shifts per side, six alternating lateral shifts, and four seated knee extensions per side. After a five-minute warm-up I held a modest wall calf stretch for 15 seconds while breathing normally. During the eight-count dance phrase my support stayed steady. I will repeat the same setup before lengthening any hold; a single session does not establish permanent flexibility gains.”

**Rubric:** Completeness of four-task journal and concepts 25%; safe adaptations and comfortable breathing 25%; dated comparable observations and movement quality 30%; cautious reflection and single-variable progression 20%. Do not award points for extreme range or pain tolerance.
