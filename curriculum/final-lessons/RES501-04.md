# RES501 · Lesson 04 — Reflexivity and a Research-Informed Choreography Proposal

**Graduate-level dance research methods | 50–70 minutes | independent study.** This learner-facing revision has not completed scholarly passage verification, expert review, or browser acceptance testing.

## Lecture: research reasoning

**Outcomes:** Define the method in this lesson, use it in a small inquiry, separate evidence from interpretation, and document the limits of any conclusion.

Reflexivity is the deliberate examination of how a researcher's position, preferences, assumptions, and choices shape the inquiry. In practice-led dance research, the researcher may also be the performer and choreographer. This creates access to embodied experience but also risks treating personal intention as objective audience response. A defensible proposal distinguishes the artistic question, empirical or documentary evidence, creative method, and limits. It also states whether other people will participate; filming or interviewing others raises consent, privacy, and institutional requirements that do not apply in the same way to a private solo rehearsal. A self-study website does not substitute for ethics review when required by an institution.

## Guided research laboratory

**Preparation (5 minutes):** Create a dated research notebook. State the research question before gathering observations. If movement is involved, warm up gently, use a safe space, and choose comfortable small-range movements.

**Apply the method (20–25 minutes):** Draft a 600–800-word proposal titled 'How timing variation changes the observable structure of my Undercurrent reach.' Include: question; rationale; one scholarly source and one practitioner or documentary source; method (five slow and five moderate-tempo solo trials); operational definition of reach onset; a data table template; analysis plan comparing observed counts; reflexivity paragraph; limitations; and a planned creative revision. Explicitly state that the design is descriptive, not causal, and that no outside participants are involved. Optionally include a 32-count annotated score showing where the reach duration changes.

**Troubleshoot your reasoning:** If you claim a slower tempo causes greater emotional impact, you need an appropriate design and audience data; a solo timing log cannot test that claim. If you decide to film another person, obtain appropriate consent and reconsider privacy and ethics before proceeding.

**Connect to Undercurrent:** Treat the original step–gather–reach–recover phrase as a bounded study object. Describe its count score, the exact feature under investigation, and whether your evidence comes from rehearsal, video, testimony, or published research. Do not call a personal impression a universal result.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is reflexivity?**

A. A type of turn

B. Critical examination of how the researcher's position shapes inquiry

C. A fixed emotional meaning

D. A guarantee of neutrality

**2. What can a solo timing study directly assess?**

A. Every viewer's feelings

B. The cause of dance injury

C. Observed onset counts under stated conditions

D. The universal meaning of reach

**3. What changes when outside participants are recruited?**

A. Consent and privacy considerations become relevant

B. Ethical duties disappear

C. Source citation becomes unnecessary

D. The question becomes automatically causal

**4. What is an appropriate limitation statement?**

A. This proves universal expressivity

B. All viewers respond identically

C. The method eliminates bias

D. The findings describe one performer's trials and do not establish causality

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Reflexivity makes the researcher's influence visible.

**2. C.** The data concern visible timing, not audience response.

**3. A.** Involving other people introduces additional obligations.

**4. D.** The statement fits the sample and design.

**Feedback cycle:** Explain why each missed alternative overreaches or misidentifies evidence. Then revise one claim in your notebook. These original questions check concepts, not graduate credit or standardized research competence.

## Independent assignment

Submit a research artifact specific to this lesson (question matrix, evidence matrix, observation log, or proposal) and a 250–350-word methodological reflection. State at least two limitations and one change you would make before repeating the inquiry. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a 600–800-word **research proposal** with a question, sources, procedure, operational definitions, analysis plan, reflexivity, ethics, and limits.

**Worked example:** “I defined reach onset as the first visible outward hand motion after the gather. In trial 2, the hand was outside the frame, so onset was coded 'not observable.' I will widen the camera framing before collecting more trials. This adjustment improves documentation; it does not establish a causal relationship.”

**Rubric:** Question and method alignment 25%; evidence quality and traceability 30%; transparent limitations and reflexivity 25%; organization and justified next steps 20%. Do not award credit for unsupported causal claims or fabricated source details.
