# LMA201 · Lesson 04 — Space: Kinesphere, Pathways, and a Four-Variation Score

**Laban Movement Analysis | independent learner | 40–55 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; passage-level source checks and browser acceptance are outstanding. These lessons introduce descriptive tools; they do not confer LMA certification.

## Learn the analytical distinction

After this lesson, you should identify the featured category, describe a visible movement cue, contrast two performances of the same phrase, and avoid inferring internal psychological states from movement alone.

In LMA, **Space** concerns movement's spatial organization, including directions, levels, pathways, and the dancer's accessible surrounding space or **kinesphere**. Kinesphere is not simply the floor area covered by traveling steps; it describes the space reachable around the body without changing the base of support. Spatial directions are relative to the dancer's orientation, so label 'front-right diagonal' alongside facing. The four Effort factors can be explored within the same spatial pathway, helping separate where movement goes from how it is performed. A beginner score can combine Body, Effort, Shape, and Space descriptions, but it is not formal Labanotation.

## Guided movement-observation lab

**Warm-up (5 minutes):** Gently shift weight, mobilize shoulders within a comfortable range, and establish a clear front-facing direction. A seated version is acceptable for all arm and torso comparisons.

**Follow along (15 minutes):** Mark front, back, left, and right in your room. Keep feet planted. Reach right arm forward on counts 1–2, diagonally up-right 3–4, sideways right 5–6, and return 7–8. Now create four versions of the same step–gather–reach–recover motif: A with fingertip initiation (Body); B with a sustained versus sudden reach (Effort); C with expanding versus enclosing arm forms (Shape); D with a front-right versus side-right destination (Space). Keep the count structure consistent so each change can be identified. Write a four-row score specifying the variable, count, cue, and unchanged features.

**Observe and correct (5 minutes):** Do not claim that changing reach direction necessarily changes musical tempo or emotional meaning. If the destination lies beyond comfortable range, move it closer within your kinesphere. When comparing variants, alter one variable at a time.

**Transfer to Undercurrent:** Perform the original step–gather–reach–recover motif on counts 1–8. Change only the category featured in this lesson. Write the before/after score and describe what remains constant. A viewer's emotional interpretation is a hypothesis, not an observation.

## Knowledge check

**1. What does kinesphere refer to?**

A. The entire stage area a dancer can traverse by walking

B. Space accessible around the body without changing the base of support

C. The path traced while traveling several steps away from the starting base

D. The direction the torso faces without reference to reachable surrounding space

**2. Which is a spatial variable?**

A. The strength of a movement's dynamic emphasis

B. The duration of a sustained movement quality

C. Reach direction

D. The sequence of body parts initiating a reach

**3. Why keep counts consistent across four variations?**

A. To help isolate the changed movement quality

B. To make the variations appear identical even when their spatial pathways change

C. To guarantee every viewer will interpret the variations the same way

D. To remove the need to observe the difference between versions

**4. What is this lesson's written score?**

A. A formal Labanotation score because it includes counts and directions

B. A standardized clinical movement assessment because it names body regions

C. A validated prediction of audience emotion because it records movement qualities

D. An introductory plain-language movement-analysis score

### Correct answers and reasoning

**1. B.** Kinesphere concerns reachable surrounding space.

**2. C.** Direction is a Space observation.

**3. A.** Controlled comparison makes the variation easier to identify.

**4. D.** It is a teaching score, not formal notation or clinical evaluation.

**Self-review:** If you miss an item, state which category the distractor actually describes. Repeat the relevant movement with a smaller range if needed. The questions are original conceptual checks, not a standardized LMA competency examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit a written score with count, body initiation, dynamic quality, shape change, and spatial destination where relevant. Provide two factual observations, one artistic intention, and a 200–300-word explanation of how you distinguished them. For Lesson 04, submit a **four-variation score** using Body, Effort, Shape, and Space separately, plus a 300-word comparative reflection.

**Worked example:** “On count 5 the fingertips initiated the reach while the torso remained nearly stationary (Body). In the next version, the reach began suddenly (Effort/Time). Both arrived at the same front-right diagonal (Space). I intended urgency, but cannot infer that every observer perceived urgency.”

**Rubric:** Correct category identification 30%; observable, reproducible movement description 30%; controlled contrast and safe execution 20%; reflective distinction between observation and interpretation 20%. Do not score on emotional conformity or extreme range.
