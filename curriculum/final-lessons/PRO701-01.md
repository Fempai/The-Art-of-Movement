# PRO701 · Lesson 01 — Polish: Define and Repeat a Performance Standard

**Professional Repertory Laboratory | independent performance study | 50–70 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; scholarly verification, expert adjudication, and live browser testing remain pending.

## Performance principle

**Objectives:** Define a reproducible performance criterion, apply a deliberate rehearsal method, compare observed execution with intention, and revise safely.

Polish is not a vague demand for perfection. For an independent dancer it means choosing a small number of visible, reproducible performance criteria and evaluating them consistently. In Undercurrent, useful criteria include reach onset on count 5, a stable return on count 7, a deliberate stillness on count 8, and a transition that does not require an unscored recovery step. A phrase may look more expressive when timing is clear, but aesthetic interpretation remains subjective. A recording can reveal timing and spatial alignment, not hidden forces or universal audience responses.

## Guided repertory laboratory

**Prepare (5–8 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area. Warm up with easy steps, gentle reaches, and comfortable changes of direction. No unsupervised aerials, lifts, or extreme ranges are required.

**Rehearse (20–30 minutes):** Write a four-item quality checklist before performing: (1) step starts on 1, (2) gather finishes by 4, (3) reach starts on 5, (4) return and stillness resolve on 7–8. Warm up, then perform the eight-count phrase three times at comfortable tempo with at least 30 seconds of rest between. Record each trial as yes/no/unclear for each criterion, plus any discomfort. Select only one criterion to correct. Rehearse the relevant two-count transition slowly twice, then repeat the full phrase once. Compare the pre- and post-correction trials without changing the scoring rule.

**Troubleshooting:** If a camera misses the feet, mark the step criterion unclear. If balance requires an extra step, reduce reach distance or use support. A correction that introduces discomfort is not an improvement.

**Independent observation:** Keep an annotated score, recording the planned count, what actually occurred, the viewing or recording limitations, and a single correction. When using video, preserve the camera position for comparisons.

## Knowledge check

**1. What makes a polish criterion usable?**

A. It is measurable and specified before rehearsal

B. It depends only on confidence

C. It guarantees applause

D. It requires maximum range

**2. What should happen when the foot is outside the camera frame?**

A. Assume it was correct

B. Mark that criterion unclear

C. Invent a count

D. Score the entire phrase as perfect

**3. Which is the best focused revision?**

A. Change tempo, floor, and choreography simultaneously

B. Repeat without checking

C. Isolate the reach-onset transition, then retest

D. Perform through discomfort

**4. What does a consistent self-score establish?**

A. Professional accreditation

B. Universal audience response

C. Medical safety

D. Performance against the chosen checklist

### Answers and explanations

**1. A.** Specific criteria permit repeatable checks.

**2. B.** Missing visual evidence must be acknowledged.

**3. C.** A targeted correction is easier to evaluate.

**4. D.** Self-scoring has a limited, explicit scope.

**Self-correction:** For missed answers, demonstrate the corrected concept in a four- or eight-count example. This knowledge check is not a validated measure of professional performance ability.

## Assignment and assessment

Submit an annotated rehearsal score, an error-and-revision log, and a 250–350-word reflection with at least two observable observations. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a **2–3 minute repertory showcase** with a timed five-section score, one continuous performance or a documented non-video equivalent, and a critique of technical consistency, transitions, expressive choices, and recovery.

**Worked example:** “The reach began on count 4 in my first trial instead of count 5. I isolated gather 3–4 and reach 5–6, then repeated the full phrase. My second trial met the timing target; I have not established long-term retention.”

**Rubric:** Score accuracy and movement control 30%; rehearsal and correction process 25%; expressive clarity with evidence 25%; safety, recovery, and reflection 20%. Self-assessment does not imply certification.
