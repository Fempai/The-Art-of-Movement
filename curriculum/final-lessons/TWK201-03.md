# TWK201 · Lesson 03 — Controlled Hip Hinge

**Standalone twerk technique | written pilot | 40–50 minutes.** TWK101 foundational control recommended. Not a verified instructor-led video.

## Objective
Distinguish a small hip hinge from forced lumbar extension.

## Cultural context
These are original practice drills within a twerk pathway, not claims about named historical steps. Black social dance and New Orleans bounce deserve sourced, practitioner-reviewed context before public instructional release.

## Follow-along practice
Warm up with gentle walking, ankle mobility and comfortable standing shifts. Place hands lightly on thighs; hinge a few centimeters from the hips with soft knees and a long comfortable torso. Counts 1–2 hinge and return; 3–4 repeat; 5–6 add a small leg-led pulse without increasing the hinge; 7–8 return upright and rest. Repeat four rounds with breaks. Keep the spine comfortable and never force arching.

## Accessible alternative
Use a stable counter to reduce balance demands, or seated gentle forward inclination to study timing without standing hip loading.

## Self-correction
Back pinching: stop and reduce range. Excess hand pressure: return upright. Losing balance: use stable support. Stop for pain, dizziness or repeated loss of balance.

## Assignment
Notate the eight counts and identify how a hinge differs from lumbar extension.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. Which movement initiates a hip hinge?**

A. Small folding at the hip joints

B. Forced low-back arching

C. Twisting the planted knee

D. A shoulder shrug

**Key: A.** Small folding at the hip joints.

**2. What happens on counts 7–8?**

A. A required floor drop

B. Return upright and rest

C. A maximal squat

D. A jump

**Key: B.** Return upright and rest.

**3. What is the appropriate response to back pinching?**

A. Increase depth

B. Hold longer

C. Stop and reduce range

D. Add faster repetitions

**Key: C.** Stop and reduce range.

**4. How does a seated forward inclination differ?**

A. It guarantees equal loading

B. It proves muscle isolation

C. It requires impact

D. It does not reproduce standing hip loading

**Key: D.** It does not reproduce standing hip loading.

## Completion checkpoint
Perform or notate two comfortable eight-count phrases and explain the key technique distinction. Written completion does not prove physical mastery.
