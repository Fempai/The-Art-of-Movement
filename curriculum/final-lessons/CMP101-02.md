# CMP101 · Lesson 02 — Phrase: Shape Beginnings, Endings, and Direction

**Independent composition study | 40–55 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; source metadata, live links, and browser acceptance remain pending. The site serves as your teacher for these foundational tasks, but no self-score guarantees how an outside viewer will interpret a work.

## Concept and learning outcomes

After this lesson you should be able to define the key compositional idea, apply it to an original phrase, record it precisely enough to repeat, and explain what the notation does and does not show.

A dance phrase is a perceptible grouping of movement events. A phrase may be eight counts for classroom convenience, but its artistic boundaries are not determined by arithmetic alone. Initiation, development, arrival, and recovery can make a grouping coherent. A phrase can continue across a musical bar line or end before one. In notation for independent practice, specify the *starting orientation*, the *supporting foot*, the *pathway*, the *counts*, and the *end state*. Facing means the direction your torso generally points; pathway means how the body or a limb travels through space. These are not synonyms.

**Observation versus intention:** You can verify that an arm reaches on count five. You cannot prove that every viewer reads that reach as longing. Write the intended effect separately from the count and pathway.

## Guided studio and writing lab

**Preparation (3–5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip space, warm up with gentle steps or seated torso shifts, and decide which direction is the front of the room. Use a small comfortable range; avoid painful or unstable movement.

**Follow this score (10–15 minutes):** Mark the front of your room as north, right as east, back as south, and left as west. Phrase A: 1 face north, weight both feet; 2 shift right; 3–4 gather arms; 5–6 reach northeast; 7 lower; 8 return to center facing north. Phrase B: 1 step left; 2 settle; 3–4 turn a comfortable 45 degrees toward northwest; 5–6 extend both arms outward; 7 recover; 8 face north again. Draw arrows for the facing changes, and label any traveling steps separately.

**Compare and revise:** A diagram with only an arrow pointing east is ambiguous: does the dancer travel east or merely face east? Specify both. If a second attempt ends facing a different direction, the score may be incomplete.

**Undercurrent connection:** Use the existing step–gather–reach–recover motif as material. Identify what you kept, changed, and why. Record two takes or write two complete count sheets. A single photograph of the endpoint is insufficient to document timing or support.

**Self-check:** Can you perform the phrase twice with the same counts and orientation? Does your score identify the support foot and the pathway? If you cannot reconstruct it, revise the notation before increasing complexity.

## Knowledge check

**1. What makes a dance phrase a phrase?**

A. It must contain eight counts, even when its movement idea resolves sooner

B. It groups movement events into a coherent unit

C. It must begin on the first beat of a measure to have a boundary

D. It is defined by changing direction at least once

**2. What does facing describe?**

A. The direction the torso generally points

B. The direction the dancer travels across the floor, regardless of torso orientation

C. The beat on which the dancer changes weight

D. The direction the dancer's gaze points, even when the torso faces elsewhere

**3. Why label both start and end orientation?**

A. To improve reproducibility of the phrase

B. To ensure that the phrase returns to the same direction at every ending

C. To infer the weight-bearing foot without recording support

D. To make the phrase symmetrical even when its original design is asymmetrical

**4. Which is a valid observation about a phrase boundary?**

A. The dancer paused after count eight

B. The pause on count eight necessarily signals sadness to every viewer

C. The observed pause proves that this phrase follows a universal cultural convention

D. The pause establishes mastery regardless of the movement leading into it

### Answer key and rationale

**1. B.** Phrase length can vary; coherence is the defining idea.

**2. A.** Facing and travel pathway are separate spatial descriptions.

**3. A.** Orientation anchors how the movement is reconstructed.

**4. A.** A pause is observable; audience feelings and mastery cannot be inferred from it alone.

**Retry:** Revisit any missed concept, annotate the movement example, and answer again without looking. These are instructor-authored conceptual checks, not externally validated tests of choreographic talent.

## Independent assignment and rubric

Create an annotated composition score. Include count numbers, action verbs, support foot or seated support, facing, pathway, at least one intentional change, and a 250–350-word critical reflection. For Lesson 04, submit a complete **32-count A–B–A′–C score**.

**Worked example:** “Counts 3–4: both hands gather toward the sternum; weight remains on the right foot. Counts 5–6: right arm reaches northeast. In A′, the reach lasts through count 7. The duration changed; my intended meaning is an interpretation, not an objective property of the score.”

**Rubric:** Structural clarity 25%; precise, reproducible notation 25%; intentional variation or contrast 25%; critical reflection and source awareness 25%. A self-assessment should identify a concrete revision, not merely give a high score.
