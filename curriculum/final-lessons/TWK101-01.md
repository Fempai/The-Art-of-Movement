# TWK101 · Lesson 01 — Pulse, Stance, and Rhythmic Choice

**Standalone twerk foundations | learner-facing written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** This written lesson is not a verified instructor-led video class. Twerk is taught as a dance practice with cultural history and musical expression, not merely conditioning.

## Objective
Maintain a comfortable eight-count pulse and identify a weight shift versus a pelvic action.

## Cultural and technique context
Modern twerking has important relationships to Black social dance and New Orleans bounce. Similar-looking hip and pelvic actions across traditions should not be labeled identical. The exercises here are newly written introductory studies, not claims to reproduce a named historical dance. Historical citations and qualified practitioner review are pending.

## Follow-along practice
Start with four minutes of walking in place, ankle movement, small knee bends and gentle hip mobility. Stand feet comfortably apart, knees soft, pelvis neutral enough to breathe without bracing. Counts 1–2: make a shallow knee-led down-and-up pulse; 3–4: repeat; 5–6: shift weight slightly right and return; 7–8: shift left and return. Repeat four times at a conversational tempo, rest, then perform two rounds with a deliberate accent on count 1 and two with an accent on count 5. Keep torso comfortable and avoid forcing the low back. Practice 10–15 minutes including pauses.

## Accessible adaptation
Use a sturdy chair to mark four small seated pressure shifts and four upper-body accents over eight counts. This preserves timing and directional attention but does not replicate standing leg loading or pelvic mechanics.

## Self-correction
Knees hurt: reduce bend depth or switch to seated rhythm. Shoulders tense: relax arms and reduce tempo. Lose beat: count aloud and remove the lateral shift.

## Apply and reflect
Record two eight-count passes or write a count-by-count score. Explain which actions are leg-led and where you deliberately place the musical accent.

## Four-question knowledge check
**1. Which cue best describes a leg-led pulse?**

A. A shallow lowering and rising coordinated through the legs

B. Forcing the lumbar spine into extension

C. Holding breath and bracing

D. Moving only the shoulders

**Key: A.** A shallow lowering and rising coordinated through the legs is the correct interpretation for this introductory study.

## Completion checkpoint
Explain the action, reproduce a comfortable eight-count study twice, identify one self-correction, and describe the difference between personal practice and an attributed cultural technique. Written or video submission is optional; a written answer does not prove movement proficiency.

## Production gate
Requires original or authorized, technique-reviewed instructor demonstration, useful front/side views, captions, media playback and browser acceptance before publication as a video-complete class.
