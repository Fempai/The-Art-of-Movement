# SIT505 · Lesson 01 — Read the Site: Surface, Routes, and Spatial Affordances

**Site-Specific & Cultural Performance | graduate-level independent study | 45–65 minutes.** Authored learner-facing revision. Source verification, site-specific safety review, and browser testing remain outstanding. No public-space access or public performance is required.

## Lecture: site-responsive reasoning

**Learning goals:** Describe a site constraint, transform a movement choice in response, document access and permission limits, and distinguish verified context from artistic speculation.

Site-specific choreography responds to the characteristics and meanings of a particular location rather than merely placing a studio dance outdoors. A site survey records physical features such as floor condition, level changes, boundaries, light, sound, and available routes, alongside questions about permission and historical or community context. 'Affordance' describes what an environment makes possible for a particular participant; a low step may invite a seated gesture for one dancer but pose an access barrier for another. Do not assume a site is safe or available because it is publicly visible. Begin with a private, level, uncluttered indoor space for the practical exercise.

## Guided site-design laboratory

**Prepare:** Use only a private or explicitly permitted, level, unobstructed practice area. Never block exits, rehearse in traffic, or test uninspected elevated surfaces.

**Investigate and choreograph (20–25 minutes):** Draw a simple plan of a room you are authorized to use. Mark the entry, exit, furniture, clear movement zone, audience position, and any uneven or slippery surfaces. Assign three points A, B, C within a safe unobstructed area. Perform the Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover motif at A, then face B without traveling and repeat. Note how the reach aligns with a wall, window, or doorway. Write two observations about the site and one design decision. Do not rehearse on stairs, rooftops, roads, or unfamiliar terrain.

**Troubleshoot:** If the planned pathway crosses furniture, shrink the route rather than stepping over obstacles. If a window produces glare, change orientation. If the location requires permission, treat approval as pending rather than rehearsing there.

**Undercurrent transfer:** Write a count-level step–gather–reach–recover score with the site's relevant orientation and a safer seated or reduced-travel alternative. Document which design choices respond to the site and which remain artistic preferences.

## Knowledge check

**1. What makes a work site-responsive?**

A. It uses the site's conditions and context as compositional material

B. It uses a familiar phrase in a different room without considering that room's conditions

C. It is performed for an audience but uses identical staging regardless of location

D. It features a backdrop depicting a location without adapting to the actual site

**2. Which belongs on a site map?**

A. The color scheme of the costume without indicating the available movement area

B. Entrances, exits, obstacles, and movement zones

C. The intended atmosphere without identifying physical boundaries

D. The planned music cues without marking exits or obstacles

**3. What is the safest first practice site?**

A. A public walkway where the dancer would need to avoid moving pedestrians

B. An outdoor terrace with no confirmed permission or surface inspection

C. A permitted, level, clear indoor area

D. A stairwell with uncertain handrails and changing floor levels

**4. What should you do if a site needs approval?**

A. Treat a publicly visible location as permission to rehearse there

B. Assume an unlocked door means the site is available for performance

C. Publicize a performance date before confirming the site's access conditions

D. Obtain permission before use

### Correct answers and explanations

**1. A.** Site response concerns the relationship between choreography and location.

**2. B.** Physical routes and constraints are fundamental.

**3. C.** A controlled site reduces avoidable hazards.

**4. D.** Public visibility does not confer permission.

**Feedback cycle:** For each missed question, distinguish a verified condition, a design hypothesis, and an unsupported assumption. These are original concept checks, not a formal accessibility or permitting assessment.

## Independent assignment

Submit a labeled site sketch, a movement score, a constraints-and-decisions table, and a 250–350-word reflection. Identify at least two uncertainties and how you would verify them. **Lesson 04 capstone:** a **site-specific proposal** including artistic rationale, 32-count score, site and audience map, accessibility and safety plan, permission status, cultural-context source ledger, and contingency plan.

**Worked example:** “The room's side window creates glare toward the front-facing performer. I rotated the reach toward the interior wall on counts 5–6 and kept the gather unchanged. This is a design response to observed light, not proof that every spectator will prefer it.”

**Rubric:** Site-responsive movement design 25%; safe and legible spatial plan 25%; accessibility and permissions transparency 30%; cultural-context research and reflective justification 20%. No points for unverified claims of legal compliance.
