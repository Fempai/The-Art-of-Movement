# FLE101 · Lesson 01 — Hip Flexor Mobility and Pelvic Control

**Active Flexibility Foundations | 35–45 minutes | independent study.** This is a learner-facing revision, not a medical evaluation or a certified stretching prescription. Stop for sharp pain, tingling, numbness, dizziness, or unusual symptoms. Do not use forced splits, partner pressure, or aggressive stretching.

## Learning goals and anatomical reasoning

Explain the relevant movement concept, demonstrate a comfortable version, distinguish active control from passive range, and document observable progress without claiming that a particular range is necessary for dance.

Hip flexion brings the thigh toward the trunk; hip extension moves the thigh behind the pelvis. The hip flexors include the iliopsoas and other muscles with distinct roles. A forward hip stretch is not improved simply by arching the low back: lumbar extension can create the appearance of greater hip movement without meaningfully changing the hip angle. Active mobility means controlling movement using your own muscular effort; passive flexibility means reaching a position with outside assistance or support. Neither measurement by itself establishes readiness for dance leaps or splits.

## Guided mobility studio

**Follow along (10–15 minutes):** Warm up with three minutes of walking. Stand facing a fixed wall or rail and take a small split stance, right foot forward. Keep both feet supported, bend the front knee slightly, and gently shift the pelvis forward without arching the back. Hold for two comfortable breaths, return, and repeat three times per side. If kneeling is comfortable, an optional padded half-kneeling version may be used with stable support; do not kneel through pain. Then stand tall with a wall nearby and lift one knee a few inches on counts 1–2, hold 3, lower 4; repeat on the other side 5–8. Observe whether the torso tips backward.

**Troubleshooting and safety:** If you feel compression in the low back, reduce the forward shift and return to a neutral comfortable stance. If you wobble during the knee lift, touch the wall and lower the leg height. If you feel sharp pain, tingling, or numbness, stop rather than trying to stretch through it.

**Apply to Undercurrent:** Perform a comfortable step–gather–reach–recover phrase for eight counts. Note whether you can move and recover without compensatory back arching, unstable support, or forced range. Keep your musical intention separate from the physical observation.

**Independent readiness check:** Can you enter and exit the selected range twice smoothly, breathe comfortably, and describe a specific modification? If not, reduce the demand. A self-report cannot rule out an underlying injury.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is hip extension?**

A. Moving the thigh behind the pelvis

B. Bringing the thigh forward relative to the pelvis

C. Moving the pelvis backward while keeping the thigh stationary

D. Tilting the torso backward without changing the thigh–pelvis angle

**2. Why avoid exaggerating a low-back arch during a hip-flexor stretch?**

A. It makes the stretch appear larger without confirming additional hip extension

B. It may substitute spinal movement for the intended hip position

C. It proves that the front hip has reached its maximum range

D. It ensures that the same range can be controlled without support

**3. What best describes active mobility?**

A. A leg position reached with a strap but not maintained independently

B. A limb angle reached by another person moving the leg

C. Controlled movement produced by your own muscular effort

D. A stretch held at the largest visible angle regardless of muscular control

**4. What is a suitable balance modification?**

A. Keep the same unsupported height but slow the movement

B. Increase the lift while retaining the same unstable stance

C. Shift to a narrower stance while keeping the same lift

D. Use a stable wall and a smaller lift

### Answer key and explanations

**1. A.** Extension changes the angle of the thigh relative to the pelvis.

**2. B.** A spinal arch can obscure how much motion occurs at the hip.

**3. C.** Active control is the distinguishing feature.

**4. D.** Stable support and smaller range preserve the objective.

**Review protocol:** For a missed item, explain why the distractor is wrong, then demonstrate the relevant idea in a smaller range. These are original conceptual questions, not validated clinical assessments.

## Independent assignment: range and quality

Create a dated log with the movement tested, warm-up, support, comfortable range description, number of controlled repetitions, breathing comfort, symptoms (if any), and one next-step decision. Include a 200–300-word reflection on active versus passive range. For Lesson 04, combine all four lessons into a **range-and-quality journal** and propose one conservative progression while holding other conditions similar.

**Worked example:** “After five minutes of walking, I completed four small rightward lateral shifts while holding a rail. My knees tracked comfortably, and I returned to center without losing balance. I will repeat the same task before deciding whether to increase the shift distance.”

**Rubric:** Anatomical and conceptual accuracy 25%; control and safe modification 25%; repeatable observation log 30%; reflection and progression rationale 20%. Do not award points for extreme range or pain tolerance.
