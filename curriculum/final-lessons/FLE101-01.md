# FLE101 · Lesson 01 — Hip Flexor Mobility and Pelvic Control

**Active Flexibility Foundations | 35–45 minutes | independent study.** This is a learner-facing revision, not a medical evaluation or a certified stretching prescription. Stop for sharp pain, tingling, numbness, dizziness, or unusual symptoms. Do not use forced splits, partner pressure, or aggressive stretching.

## Learning goals and anatomical reasoning

Explain the relevant movement concept, demonstrate a comfortable version, distinguish active control from passive range, and document observable progress without claiming that a particular range is necessary for dance.

Hip flexion brings the thigh toward the trunk; hip extension moves the thigh behind the pelvis. The hip flexors include the iliopsoas and other muscles with distinct roles. A forward hip stretch is not improved simply by arching the low back: lumbar extension can create the appearance of greater hip movement without meaningfully changing the hip angle. Active mobility means controlling movement using your own muscular effort; passive flexibility means reaching a position with outside assistance or support. Neither measurement by itself establishes readiness for dance leaps or splits.

## Guided mobility studio

**Follow along (10–15 minutes):** Warm up with three minutes of walking. Stand facing a fixed wall or rail and take a small split stance, right foot forward. Keep both feet supported, bend the front knee slightly, and gently shift the pelvis forward without arching the back. Hold for two comfortable breaths, return, and repeat three times per side. If kneeling is comfortable, an optional padded half-kneeling version may be used with stable support; do not kneel through pain. Then stand tall with a stable wall within reach: **1–2** lift the right knee a few inches without leaning back, **3** pause with supported balance, **4** lower the right foot; **5–6** lift the left knee, **7** pause, **8** lower the left foot. Keep both feet grounded between sides. **Seated alternative:** In a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported, use counts 1–2 to gently lift the right foot a small distance, 3 pause, 4 lower, 5–6 lift the left foot, 7 pause, 8 lower; this checks seated active hip flexion, not standing balance or a hip-extension stretch. If split stance is unsuitable, skip it and log that the standing hip-extension component was not assessed. Observe whether the torso tips backward during each knee lift and whether light wall contact helps you maintain a comfortable trunk position. Record the right and left attempts separately; a higher lift is not automatically a better result.

**Troubleshooting and safety:** If you feel compression in the low back, reduce the forward shift and return to a neutral comfortable stance. If you wobble during the knee lift, touch the wall and lower the leg height. If you feel sharp pain, tingling, or numbness, stop rather than trying to stretch through it.

**Apply to Undercurrent:** Perform a comfortable step–gather–reach–recover phrase for eight counts. Note whether you can move and recover without compensatory back arching, unstable support, or forced range. Keep your musical intention separate from the physical observation.

**Independent readiness check:** Can you enter and exit the selected range twice smoothly, breathe comfortably, and describe a specific modification? If not, reduce the demand. A self-report cannot rule out an underlying injury.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is hip extension?**

A. Bringing the thigh forward relative to the pelvis

B. Moving the thigh behind the pelvis

C. Moving the pelvis backward while keeping the thigh stationary

D. Tilting the torso backward without changing the thigh–pelvis angle

**2. Why avoid exaggerating a low-back arch during a hip-flexor stretch?**

A. It guarantees additional hip extension without changing the spine

B. It may substitute spinal movement for the intended hip position

C. It proves that the front hip has reached its maximum range

D. It ensures that the same range can be controlled without support

**3. What best describes active mobility?**

A. A leg position reached with a strap but not maintained independently

B. A limb angle reached by another person moving the leg

C. Controlled movement produced by your own muscular effort

D. A stretch held at the largest visible angle regardless of muscular control

**4. What is a suitable balance modification?**

A. Keep the same unsupported height but slow the movement

B. Increase the lift while retaining the same unstable stance

C. Shift to a narrower stance while keeping the same lift

D. Use a stable wall and a smaller lift

### Answer key and explanations

**1. B.** Hip extension moves the thigh posteriorly relative to the pelvis. A describes flexion, while C and D can change the appearance of the pose without necessarily changing the thigh–pelvis joint angle.

**2. B.** Exaggerated lumbar extension can substitute for the intended hip position and make the stretch difficult to interpret. A incorrectly claims that a larger arch guarantees hip extension without spinal movement. C and D infer maximal range or active control without evidence.

**3. C.** Active mobility involves moving through a range using one's own muscular effort with control. A and B describe externally assisted positions, while D values visible angle without demonstrating active control.

**4. D.** A stable wall and lower lift reduce balance demands while preserving controlled hip movement. A retains unsupported instability, B increases the challenge, and C narrows the stance without addressing the unstable lift.

**Review protocol:** For a missed item, explain why the distractor is wrong, then demonstrate the relevant idea in a smaller range. These are original conceptual questions, not validated clinical assessments.

## Independent assignment: range and quality

Submit a dated hip-flexor mobility and pelvic-control log. Record the split-stance setup, which foot was forward, support used, three gentle shifts per side, breathing comfort, and whether the lower back arched or the pelvis moved without compensation. Include the supported small knee-lift check, one adjustment, and a 200–300-word reflection distinguishing comfortable active control from an externally assisted stretch.

**Worked example:** “With my right foot forward and one hand on the rail, I completed three small shifts without arching my back. On the left side I felt my torso tip, so I shortened the shift. During the standing knee lift I touched the wall and kept the lift low. These observations describe today's control, not a measurement of maximum hip extension.”

**Rubric:** Pelvic and hip-control description 25%; safe range and support adaptation 25%; repeatable side-by-side observation log 30%; evidence-based reflection and next-step decision 20%. Do not award points for extreme range or pain tolerance.
