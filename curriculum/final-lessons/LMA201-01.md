# LMA201 · Lesson 01 — Body: Initiation, Connection, and Sequencing

**Laban Movement Analysis | independent learner | 40–55 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; passage-level source checks and browser acceptance are outstanding. These lessons introduce descriptive tools; they do not confer LMA certification.

## Learn the analytical distinction

After this lesson, you should identify the featured category, describe a visible movement cue, contrast two performances of the same phrase, and avoid inferring internal psychological states from movement alone.

Laban Movement Analysis (LMA) commonly organizes movement observation through Body, Effort, Shape, and Space. **Body** concerns what parts move, their coordination, and movement organization. In a simple arm reach, the hand can initiate while the shoulder and torso remain relatively quiet, or a torso shift can precede the arm. These are different observable sequences even when the final pose looks similar. The Body category is descriptive; it is not a diagnosis of a dancer's psychology or a prescription for one correct technique. Movement analysis depends on trained observation and context, and an introductory lesson cannot certify professional LMA competency.

## Guided movement-observation lab

**Warm-up (5 minutes):** Gently shift weight, mobilize shoulders within a comfortable range, and establish a clear front-facing direction. A seated version is acceptable for all arm and torso comparisons.

**Follow along (15 minutes):** Warm up with small shoulder circles and gentle walking or seated shifts. Face front. Version A: 1 step right, 2 settle, 3–4 gather hands, 5 initiate a reach with the right fingertips, 6 let the elbow extend, 7 return the arm, 8 settle. Version B: same counts, but 5 initiate a small torso shift right, 6 let the right arm follow, 7 return, 8 settle. Repeat each version three times at a slow spoken count. Record the first visible moving segment and whether the torso shift preceded or followed the arm. Keep the shoulder in a comfortable range.

**Observe and correct (5 minutes):** If the two versions appear identical, mark them seated and isolate only the initiation on count 5. Do not infer a dancer's personality from an observed sequence. If the torso shift destabilizes you, reduce it and keep both feet grounded.

**Transfer to Undercurrent:** Perform the original step–gather–reach–recover motif on counts 1–8. Change only the category featured in this lesson. Write the before/after score and describe what remains constant. A viewer's emotional interpretation is a hypothesis, not an observation.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which question belongs most directly to the Body category?**

A. Which body part begins the reach?

B. What is the historical date?

C. How expensive is the costume?

D. Who composed the music?

**2. What distinguishes the two taught versions?**

A. The number of performers

B. The initiating body segment

C. The dance's cultural origin

D. The written meter

**3. What is a responsible observation?**

A. The dancer is anxious

B. The reach proves confidence

C. The torso moved before the arm

D. Every viewer feels tension

**4. What should you do if a torso shift destabilizes you?**

A. Shift farther

B. Close your eyes

C. Speed up

D. Reduce the shift and preserve support

### Correct answers and reasoning

**1. A.** Body analysis attends to organization and initiation.

**2. B.** One version begins at the fingertips and the other with a torso shift.

**3. C.** The movement order can be observed; internal states cannot be inferred reliably.

**4. D.** Smaller range maintains the learning objective.

**Self-review:** If you miss an item, state which category the distractor actually describes. Repeat the relevant movement with a smaller range if needed. The questions are original conceptual checks, not a standardized LMA competency examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit a written score with count, body initiation, dynamic quality, shape change, and spatial destination where relevant. Provide two factual observations, one artistic intention, and a 200–300-word explanation of how you distinguished them. For Lesson 04, submit a **four-variation score** using Body, Effort, Shape, and Space separately, plus a 300-word comparative reflection.

**Worked example:** “On count 5 the fingertips initiated the reach while the torso remained nearly stationary (Body). In the next version, the reach began suddenly (Effort/Time). Both arrived at the same front-right diagonal (Space). I intended urgency, but cannot infer that every observer perceived urgency.”

**Rubric:** Correct category identification 30%; observable, reproducible movement description 30%; controlled contrast and safe execution 20%; reflective distinction between observation and interpretation 20%. Do not score on emotional conformity or extreme range.
