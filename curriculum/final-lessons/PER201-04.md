# PER201 · Lesson 04 — Visual Clarity: Critique a 32-Count Performance

**Performance & Storytelling | 40–55 minutes | independent learner.** This is an authored lesson revision, not a professionally adjudicated performance assessment. Bibliographic and live-browser audits remain outstanding.

## Learn: the performance principle

By the end, identify a visible expressive variable, perform two contrasting versions of a phrase, and critique a recording without confusing observable facts with presumed audience emotions.

Visual clarity is the degree to which a movement choice can be identified and followed from a particular vantage point. It is not the same as technical virtuosity or a single correct emotional reading. In a short composition, clarity may depend on distinct phrase boundaries, visible contrast, consistent facing, and recoveries that prepare the next action. Critique should distinguish description, interpretation, and evaluation: 'the reach lasted three counts' is description; 'it suggested hesitation to me' is interpretation; 'the extended duration made the motif return more legible' is an evidence-based evaluation. Avoid universal claims about what an audience must feel.

## Guided performance studio

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area and choose a safe front-facing reference point. Warm up with easy steps, gentle arm reaches, and comfortable head movements. Seated or small-range marking is acceptable; no leaps or forced movements are required.

**Perform (15 minutes):** Perform the 32-count Undercurrent draft: **A (1–8)** step right 1, settle 2, gather 3–4, reach diagonally 5–6, return 7, stillness 8. **B (9–16)** step left 9, settle 10, sweep arms 11–12, turn a small comfortable diagonal 13–14, lower 15, return front 16. **A′ (17–24)** step right 17, settle 18, gather 19–20, reach and sustain 21–23, recover 24. **C (25–32)** shift left 25–26, right 27–28, reach forward 29, gather 30, lower 31, finish 32. **For a seated version**, use a stable nonrolling chair with supported feet and the count-by-count seated A–B–A′–C score from LAB101-04; label seated pressure shifts and gaze direction rather than claiming standing turns or steps. **Evidence route:** Record one take if feasible, or make an immediate count-by-count self-observation grid if recording is unavailable. For video, watch without sound to mark visible phrase boundaries, then watch with counts; for a written grid, first mark the visible or felt boundary actions without consulting the score, then compare to the count sheet. Note **up to two actual timing discrepancies**; if none are observed, explicitly record 'none observed' rather than inventing errors. Revise one transition or focus cue and record a second take under the same conditions.

**Observe and revise (5 minutes):** If the A′ variation is hard to detect, emphasize its longer duration while keeping the rest of the motif recognizable. If transitions require unscored steps, correct the count sheet. If the framing cuts off hands or feet, reposition the camera rather than assuming the movement is unclear.

**Undercurrent application:** Record the count, facing, gaze, pathway, and expressive intention separately. Ask what actually changed between versions. If using video, preserve the same camera position and note what falls outside the frame.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is a description rather than interpretation?**

A. The three-count arm extension proves the performer intended sorrow

B. The right arm extended for three counts

C. The dancer's lowered gaze reveals a mysterious personality

D. The delayed reach necessarily makes all viewers anticipate the same event

**2. What distinguishes A′ from A in the given score?**

A. A complete replacement of the motif with unrelated material

B. A different movement lineage inferred from the altered timing

C. A reach sustained for an extra count

D. A changed ending pose without retaining the reach motif

**3. What is a useful critique structure?**

A. Description, interpretation, and evidence-based evaluation

B. A list of impressions without any count-based observations

C. A numeric rating without explaining the movement evidence

D. A description of the intended feeling without examining the performed pathway

**4. What should you do if the camera cuts off the feet?**

A. Judge the unseen support foot from the arm position alone

B. Score foot placement despite the feet being outside the frame

C. Treat the cropped recording as proof that the movement score is wrong

D. Adjust framing before evaluating footwork

### Answers and rationales

**1. B.** The right arm extending for three counts describes visible action and duration without assigning motive. Claims about sorrow (A), personality (C), or a universal audience response (D) add interpretations not established by the observed movement.

**2. C.** A′ retains the original reach motif while sustaining it for an additional count, making a specific variation recognizable. Unrelated replacement (A), an inferred new lineage (B), and changing the ending without retaining the reach (D) do not describe the taught A′ variation.

**3. A.** Description, interpretation, and evidence-based evaluation distinguish what happened from what it might mean and how well it worked. Impressions alone (B), unsupported ratings (C), and intention without examining execution (D) omit essential critique evidence.

**4. D.** Reframe the recording before assessing footwork because cropped feet provide insufficient placement evidence. Inferring support from the arm (A), scoring unseen feet (B), or treating cropping as proof of a flawed movement score (C) confuses recording limits with performance evidence.

**Self-review:** For any missed question, label the alternatives as observation, interpretation, or unsupported generalization. This knowledge check is original and not a validated audience-response measure.

## Independent assignment

Submit two complete, annotated 32-count A–B–A′–C performance scores and a 250–350-word comparison. Label A 1–8, B 9–16, A′ 17–24, and C 25–32; document focus, facing, support, each phrase boundary (8→9, 16→17, 24→25), and the sustained reach on 21–23. Observe take one without sound, then with counts, identifying two timing discrepancies and at least three visible choices across the work. Revise one transition or focus cue, record or mark take two under comparable conditions, and explain whether the change is observable without assuming an audience's emotional interpretation.

**Worked example:** “In take one, A ended in stillness on 8, B returned front on 16, A′ sustained the reach through 21–23, and C finished on 32. Watching with counts revealed a late recovery at 24 and an unclear 16→17 transition. In take two I clarified the facing change before count 17 without adding a beat. The score records a visible revision, but it cannot establish how every viewer perceived the work.”

**Rubric:** Observable specificity 30%; reproducible performance choices 25%; clear distinction between intention and interpretation 25%; justified revision and reflection 20%. A self-score does not establish professional stage readiness.
