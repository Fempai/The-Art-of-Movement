# CON101 · Lesson 02 — Movement Initiation and Sequencing

**Self-directed contemporary dance | 35–50 minutes including movement, retrieval, and reflection.** No instructor or barre required for the foundational version. Advanced floorwork, inversions, falls, and painful movements require qualified instruction. Live browser acceptance and claim-level source auditing are still pending.

## Learning goal and core explanation

After this lesson, explain the concept, demonstrate a controlled low-risk version, compare two intentional choices, and document a movement phrase.

Initiation means the body part or action that begins a movement sequence. A reach can begin with the hand, the shoulder, the sternum, the pelvis, or a weight shift, and each choice changes how the movement unfolds. Sequencing describes the order in which body segments participate. The visible endpoint may remain similar while the route and dynamic quality differ. The goal is not to identify a universally correct initiator, but to make the chosen sequence intentional and repeatable.

**Think like a choreographer:** What do you actually observe—timing, direction, weight, contact points—and what are you inferring, such as the dancer's emotion or intention? A strong interpretation states its evidence and acknowledges alternatives.

## Follow-along studio instruction

**Warm up for 3–5 minutes:** Walk or march gently, move shoulders and ankles through comfortable ranges, and practice an easy weight shift. Clear the floor, avoid slippery socks or moving furniture, and keep a fixed support available if needed. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided eight-count study:** Stand in a comfortable parallel stance or sit securely. On count 1, move the right hand forward a few inches; on 2, let the elbow and shoulder follow; on 3–4, allow a small upper-torso turn; on 5–6, reverse the sequence; on 7–8, return to center. Repeat, initiating the same general reach from a gentle torso rotation instead. Keep the range small and avoid forceful spinal twisting.

**Independent practice:** Perform the sequence once while speaking counts, once while observing the initiation or support pathway, and once with a deliberate change of timing. If a version becomes unstable, reduce the range or use the seated alternative.

**Undercurrent application:** The Undercurrent reach may look abrupt if the hand travels first, or unfolding if the torso begins and the arm follows. Compare both without assigning a fixed emotional meaning. Use the original step–gather–reach–recover motif, then change only one variable associated with this lesson. Write down the eight counts and describe the difference without claiming a universal audience reaction.

## Troubleshooting without a teacher

| What happens | Possible explanation, not diagnosis | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Everything moves at once | You may not yet be separating the stages of the sequence | Mark hand, elbow, and torso one at a time |
| You twist beyond a comfortable range | The task may be too large or fast | Reduce rotation or do the exercise seated |
| You cannot explain what started the phrase | The movement may be remembered only as a final pose | Write the first two actions as a count-by-count score |

**Ready to move on when:** You can repeat the comfortable version twice at a steady count, name the supporting surface or foot, and explain one deliberate choreographic change. This is a learning checkpoint, not medical clearance.

## Knowledge check

**1. What does movement initiation describe?**

A. The body part or action that begins a sequence

B. The last pose of a dance

C. The volume of the music

D. The dancer's height

**2. Two reaches end in the same position but begin differently. What can differ?**

A. Their sequencing and dynamic quality

B. The laws of gravity

C. The number of joints in the body

D. The audience's location automatically

**3. Which method best teaches initiation independently?**

A. Practice the first action separately before combining stages

B. Only copy the final silhouette

C. Move as quickly as possible

D. Avoid naming body segments

**4. What is an appropriate limitation of observing a phrase?**

A. A video always proves the dancer's intention

B. An observer can see timing but may not know the artist's intended meaning

C. All observers interpret identically

D. The endpoint determines the entire movement

### Answer key and explanations

**1. A.** Initiation identifies how the movement begins.

**2. A.** Different initiations can produce different movement pathways and timing.

**3. A.** Segmenting the action clarifies the initiating event.

**4. B.** Description and intention are distinct.

**Self-grading:** Four correct indicates understanding of the introductory distinctions tested here. For a missed answer, revisit its explanation and demonstrate the related idea at a smaller range before retrying. These original questions are not externally validated examinations.

## Independent assignment

Create two eight-count versions of the same phrase. Record the count, initiating segment, direction, support, and intended effect. Write 250–350 words comparing the versions, identifying one observable difference, one interpretation, and one limitation. Include at least one relevant reference and explain what that source can and cannot support.

**Worked example:** “In version A, I reached for two counts and returned immediately. In version B, I held the reach for one additional count. The duration changed, which I can verify by counting; whether that pause suggests hesitation is an interpretation.”

**Rubric:** Concept and terminology 25%; accurate movement score 25%; comparison and critical reasoning 25%; source use and revision 25%. Written grades do not establish the safety or quality of unsupervised advanced technique.
