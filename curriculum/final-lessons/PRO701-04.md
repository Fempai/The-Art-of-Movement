# PRO701 · Lesson 04 — Stamina and the Repertory Showcase

**Professional Repertory Laboratory | independent performance study | 50–70 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; scholarly verification, expert adjudication, and live browser testing remain pending.

## Performance principle

**Objectives:** Define a reproducible performance criterion, apply a deliberate rehearsal method, compare observed execution with intention, and revise safely.

Performance stamina is the ability to sustain appropriate movement quality across a work, not a mandate to train to exhaustion. A reliable showcase protocol balances technical consistency, expressive intention, rest, and recovery. Repeated rehearsal can produce fatigue-related errors that should be logged rather than hidden. For a solo learner, a 'showcase' can be a private recorded performance with a clear score, a single-take run, and a reflective evaluation. A self-rated showcase does not constitute professional certification or a substitute for in-person instruction where advanced skills are involved.

## Guided repertory laboratory

**Prepare (5–8 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area. Warm up with easy steps, gentle reaches, and comfortable changes of direction. No unsupervised aerials, lifts, or extreme ranges are required.

**Rehearse (20–30 minutes):** Plan a 2–3 minute showcase using a safe tempo and original Undercurrent material. At 80 beats/minute, five 32-count sections equal 160 beats or 120 seconds. Structure the work: opening A–B–A′–C (32), variation (32), contrasting interpretation (32), retrograde-inspired return (32), and resolution (32). Warm up for 5–8 minutes. Mark the entire sequence at low intensity, rest 2 minutes, then perform one full take at a comfortable level. Log actual duration, two timing markers, visible transitions, perceived exertion (0–10), and discomfort. If any unsafe loss of control occurs, stop rather than completing the recording.

**Troubleshooting:** If quality drops in the final section, shorten the work, add an intentional stillness, or distribute effort more evenly. If a 160-beat plan is performed at a different tempo, recalculate the duration. Do not claim a full-length showcase from an isolated 32-count clip.

**Independent observation:** Keep an annotated score, recording the planned count, what actually occurred, the viewing or recording limitations, and a single correction. When using video, preserve the camera position for comparisons.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is a safe goal of stamina rehearsal?**

A. Completing repeated runs until control fails as evidence of endurance

B. Sustained controlled quality with appropriate recovery

C. Maintaining the target tempo despite discomfort that alters movement quality

D. Removing recovery intervals to prove the phrase can be performed without pauses

**2. At 80 beats/minute, how long is 160 beats?**

A. 60 seconds, treating the 160 beats as if the tempo were 160 BPM

B. 90 seconds, counting only three quarters of the 160 beats

C. 120 seconds

D. 160 seconds, assuming each beat lasts one full second

**3. What is a valid showcase artifact?**

A. A scored, timed, single-take performance with reflective log

B. A self-scored recording presented as proof of professional certification

C. A photograph of the final pose without a timing or movement record

D. A favorable audience quote without a traceable performance artifact

**4. What should happen if movement control becomes unsafe?**

A. Continue to the final count even after stable support is lost

B. Increase tempo so the unstable transition ends sooner

C. Omit the failed transition from the reflection while keeping the same rehearsal plan

D. Stop and adjust the plan

### Answers and explanations

**1. B.** The safe target is sustained controlled movement with recovery appropriate to the learner. Repeating until control fails (A), persisting despite discomfort (C), or removing recovery to prove endurance (D) prioritizes exhaustion over reliable quality.

**2. C.** At 80 beats per minute, each beat lasts 60/80 = 0.75 seconds; 160 beats take 160 × 0.75 = 120 seconds. Sixty (A), ninety (B), and 160 seconds (D) each assume an incorrect count or beat duration.

**3. A.** A timed single-take performance paired with a score and reflective log makes execution and self-evaluation traceable. Self-certification (B), a still photograph without timing (C), and an unsupported favorable quote (D) cannot document the same performance evidence.

**4. D.** Stop when movement control becomes unsafe, identify the unstable transition, and revise the plan before resuming. Continuing after support is lost (A), increasing speed (B), or concealing the failure (C) does not manage the observed risk.

**Self-correction:** For missed answers, demonstrate the corrected concept in a four- or eight-count example. This knowledge check is not a validated measure of professional performance ability.

## Assignment and assessment

Submit a **2–3 minute private repertory showcase** with five timed 32-count sections: opening, variation, contrasting interpretation, retrograde-inspired return, and resolution. At 80 BPM, five sections total 160 beats or 120 seconds; recalculate if tempo differs. Include a full count-level section score, one safe continuous take or a documented non-video equivalent, actual duration, two timing markers, transitions, perceived exertion (0–10), discomfort, and a 250–350-word critique of consistency, expressive choices and recovery. Stop or shorten the performance if control deteriorates; a self-score is not professional certification.

**Worked example:** “I planned 160 beats at 80 BPM, totaling 120 seconds, and marked the five 32-count sections before a single continuous take. I logged whether the A′ transition aligned with its entry count and whether my return in the final section required an extra step. A shorter or interrupted take would be documented honestly rather than labeled a complete showcase.”

**Rubric:** Complete correctly timed five-section score 30%; observable continuous execution or transparent alternative 25%; documented technical and expressive critique 25%; safe pacing, recovery and evidence limits 20%.
