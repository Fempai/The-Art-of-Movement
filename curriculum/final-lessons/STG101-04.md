# STG101 · Lesson 04 — Hip Control, Dance Intervals, and a Personal Baseline

**Independent dance conditioning | 35–50 minutes.** This lesson is an authored learner-facing revision. Final citation, professional safety, and live-browser audits remain outstanding. Beginners should choose comfortable loads and stop for pain, dizziness, chest discomfort, or unusual shortness of breath. Seek qualified medical advice when symptoms warrant it.

## Learn: movement and training principle

Hip control involves coordinating the pelvis and thigh while the trunk and standing leg provide support. For an introductory baseline, prioritize small repeatable movements over high leg lifts or forced turnout. **Stamina** is the capacity to sustain a chosen activity; an interval test records performance under specified conditions, not an overall diagnosis of fitness. The 40/20 structure used here means 40 seconds of easy movement followed by 20 seconds of rest, but the learner should shorten the work interval when needed. This is not a maximal test and does not prescribe an individual cardiovascular target.

**Training principle:** Repetition quality and repeatable conditions matter more than a dramatic single attempt. A self-directed website cannot assess individual injury risk, prescribe rehabilitation, or certify technique from a self-reported score.

## Guided conditioning lab

**Warm-up and practice:** Warm up for five minutes with easy walking and comfortable weight shifts. Holding a stable rail or standing near a wall, shift weight to the left foot and gently lift the right foot a few inches forward without leaning backward; lower slowly. Repeat 4–6 times, then switch sides. **If standing hip lifts are unsuitable**, remain seated with feet supported and alternately slide one foot a short distance forward and back for 4–6 comfortable repetitions per side; label this a seated hip-coordination adaptation, not an equivalent standing hip-strength test. Do not force turnout or lift through hip pinching. Next complete three easy 40-second intervals of the Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover motif, each followed by 20 seconds of rest. Speak counts or use a timer; maintain a pace at which you can still speak in short sentences. If that feels too hard, use 20 seconds of work and 40 seconds of rest, or seated arm marking. **Seated interval option:** On a stable nonrolling chair with both feet supported, use the same 40/20 or modified 20/40 timer: count 1 shift seated pressure gently right, 2 settle, 3–4 gather hands, 5–6 reach diagonally, 7 recover the arm, 8 settle; repeat the eight-count cycle at an easy pace for each work interval. This measures seated movement tolerance under the logged conditions, **not standing dance stamina or cardiovascular fitness**. Cool down with two minutes of easy walking or comfortable seated arm/ankle movements.

**Observe and troubleshoot:** Log date, interval length, number completed, talk-test comfort, support use, leg-lift control, and any symptoms. Reassess under similar conditions after sufficient recovery, not every day. Progress one variable at a time, such as an extra comfortable repetition or slightly longer interval, rather than increasing everything together.

**Transfer to dance:** Perform one eight-count Undercurrent step–gather–reach–recover motif in a comfortable range. Ask whether your breathing, posture, and transitions remain controllable. Conditioning supports the phrase; the phrase is not a test of maximal strength.

**Ready for progression?** You can complete the chosen version without pain, loss of control, or compensatory rushing, and can explain exactly which training variable you would change. Do not progress solely because a numerical repetition target was met.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is the main purpose of a personal baseline?**

A. Demonstrate that a single high-effort session represents long-term capacity

B. Record repeatable starting conditions for comparison

C. Establish that identical results will occur under every future condition

D. Replace movement-quality notes with the highest repetition count

**2. What does a 40/20 interval mean here?**

A. 40 seconds of work and 20 repetitions of recovery

B. 40 repetitions completed within 20 seconds

C. 40 seconds of work and 20 seconds of rest

D. 40 seconds of recovery followed by 20 seconds of work

**3. Which is an appropriate response if 40 seconds is too difficult?**

A. Shorten work and lengthen rest

B. Maintain the same work duration but remove the rest period

C. Keep the same interval while increasing movement speed

D. Add resistance to complete the original 40-second interval

**4. Which progression is most interpretable?**

A. Increase repetitions and resistance together while changing the exercise

B. Change the music tempo and work duration simultaneously without recording either

C. Change the support surface, exercise, and recovery time in one session

D. Change one variable while keeping other conditions similar

### Answer key with explanations

**1. B.** A baseline records starting performance under repeatable conditions for later comparison. A treats one hard session as long-term capacity, C promises identical future results, and D omits movement quality.

**2. C.** In this lesson 40/20 means 40 seconds of work followed by 20 seconds of rest. A mixes seconds with repetitions, B swaps units and sequence, and D reverses work and rest.

**3. A.** Shorter work and longer rest can make intervals manageable while maintaining controlled movement. B removes recovery, C increases speed despite difficulty, and D adds resistance to an already demanding interval.

**4. D.** Changing one variable while holding others similar makes changes easier to interpret. A changes repetitions, resistance, and exercise; B changes tempo and duration; C changes support, exercise, and recovery together.

**Self-review:** Correct answers test the stated training distinctions. For a missed question, explain why the chosen distractor is incorrect and repeat the relevant demonstration with a comfortable modification. These questions are not a validated health or fitness test.

## Independent assignment

For Lesson 04, submit a combined personal baseline log covering pulling, pushing, trunk control, supported hip lifts, and easy dance intervals. Include dates, equipment/support, reps or work/rest duration, effort and talk-test comfort where relevant, one observable cue per activity, modifications, and exercises omitted with reasons. Add a 200–300-word reflection distinguishing observations from interpretations and identifying one cautious next-step decision.

**Worked example:** “I completed three 40-second easy dance intervals with 20-second rests and could speak in short sentences throughout. I used the rail for four supported hip lifts per side. My combined log also includes last session's row, wall push-up, and trunk-shift records; I did not repeat the row today because secure equipment was unavailable. Next time I will repeat the same intervals before changing their duration. This baseline does not certify my cardiovascular fitness.”

**Rubric:** Accurate lesson-specific movement description 25%; safe support and honest adaptation 25%; complete repeatable baseline observations 30%; evidence-based interpretation and next-step rationale 20%. A self-grade is not a clinical or professional assessment.
