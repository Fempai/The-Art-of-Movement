# LMA201 · Lesson 03 — Shape: Opening, Closing, and Changing Form

**Laban Movement Analysis | independent learner | 40–55 minutes.** Learner-facing revision; passage-level source checks and browser acceptance are outstanding. These lessons introduce descriptive tools; they do not confer LMA certification.

## Learn the analytical distinction

After this lesson, you should identify the featured category, describe a visible movement cue, contrast two performances of the same phrase, and avoid inferring internal psychological states from movement alone.

The **Shape** category examines how the body's form changes and relates to itself and the environment. In introductory work, expanding and enclosing are accessible examples: arms can open away from the center or gather toward it. Shape is more than a still silhouette; the *process* of changing form matters. Two dancers may arrive at a similar arm position using different pathways and relationships between torso and limbs. Shape analysis can be combined with Effort or Body observations, but the categories answer different questions. 'The arms widened' describes shape change; 'the reach was sudden' describes dynamic timing.

## Guided movement-observation lab

**Warm-up (5 minutes):** Gently shift weight, mobilize shoulders within a comfortable range, and establish a clear front-facing direction. A seated version is acceptable for all arm and torso comparisons.

**Follow along (15 minutes):** Stand or sit with comfortable posture. Counts 1–2: bring hands gently toward the sternum, noticing the body's enclosing form. Counts 3–4: open arms sideways to a comfortable width, noticing expansion. Counts 5–6: gather hands again while keeping breathing natural. Counts 7–8: open one arm diagonally while the other remains near the center. Repeat with a smaller range and compare the visible relationship between the arms and trunk. For Undercurrent, perform the normal gather on 3–4 and reach on 5–6, then reverse the direction of the shape change without changing the musical counts.

**Observe and correct (5 minutes):** Do not force the chest or shoulders to expand beyond comfort. If you describe a movement only by its final pose, add what changed between counts. If you call a gesture 'free' or 'bound,' explain that this belongs to Effort rather than Shape.

**Transfer to Undercurrent:** Perform the original step–gather–reach–recover motif on counts 1–8. Change only the category featured in this lesson. Write the before/after score and describe what remains constant. A viewer's emotional interpretation is a hypothesis, not an observation.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is a Shape observation?**

A. The arms widened away from the center

B. The reach unfolded suddenly, describing its Time quality rather than form

C. The dancer intended to appear expansive, without an observable form description

D. The arms moved at a sustained tempo, describing timing rather than body shape

**2. Why observe the process of shape change?**

A. Two phrases ending in the same pose necessarily have identical shape-change pathways

B. Different pathways can lead to similar final positions

C. An endpoint photograph establishes the complete process of arriving there

D. The process can be inferred solely from the final arm width

**3. Which category primarily describes sudden versus sustained?**

A. Body, because the movement begins with the shoulders

B. Space, because the movement travels toward the diagonal

C. Effort

D. Shape, because the arms widen during the movement

**4. Which is a useful safe modification?**

A. Preserve the widest arm range even when shoulder discomfort appears

B. Maintain a rigid elbow position to make the opening more visible

C. Increase the range while keeping the same uncontrolled shoulder position

D. Reduce arm range while preserving the opening/closing contrast

### Correct answers and reasoning

**1. A.** Arms widening away from the body's center describes a visible Shape change. Sudden unfolding (B) and sustained timing (D) describe Effort's Time quality, while presumed intention without form evidence (C) is not an observable Shape description.

**2. B.** Two phrases can reach similar endpoints through different sequences and pathways, so the process of shape change matters. Identical-process assumptions (A), an endpoint photograph (C), and final width alone (D) cannot establish the preceding movement process.

**3. C.** Sudden versus sustained names the Time factor within Effort analysis. Body initiation (A), spatial travel direction (B), and widening form (D) are distinct observational categories, even though several categories can describe the same phrase.

**4. D.** Reducing arm range while preserving an opening-and-closing contrast keeps the Shape lesson accessible. Continuing through shoulder discomfort (A), locking the elbow rigidly (B), or expanding an uncontrolled range (C) introduces unnecessary strain.

**Self-review:** If you miss an item, state which category the distractor actually describes. Repeat the relevant movement with a smaller range if needed. The questions are original conceptual checks, not a standardized LMA competency examination.

## Independent assignment

Submit a written score with count, body initiation, dynamic quality, shape change, and spatial destination where relevant. Provide two factual observations, one artistic intention, and a 200–300-word explanation of how you distinguished them. For Lesson 04, submit a **four-variation score** using Body, Effort, Shape, and Space separately, plus a 300-word comparative reflection.

**Worked example:** “On count 5 the fingertips initiated the reach while the torso remained nearly stationary (Body). In the next version, the reach began suddenly (Effort/Time). Both arrived at the same front-right diagonal (Space). I intended urgency, but cannot infer that every observer perceived urgency.”

**Rubric:** Correct category identification 30%; observable, reproducible movement description 30%; controlled contrast and safe execution 20%; reflective distinction between observation and interpretation 20%. Do not score on emotional conformity or extreme range.
