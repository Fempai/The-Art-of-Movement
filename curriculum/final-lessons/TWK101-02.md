# TWK101 · Lesson 02 — Pelvic Tilt Versus Knee-Led Bounce

**Standalone twerk foundations | learner-facing written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** This written lesson is not a verified instructor-led video class. Twerk is taught as a dance practice with cultural history and musical expression, not merely conditioning.

## Objective
Differentiate small anterior/posterior pelvic tilts from a knee-led bounce without treating either as proof of isolated glute contraction.

## Cultural and technique context
Modern twerking has important relationships to Black social dance and New Orleans bounce. Similar-looking hip and pelvic actions across traditions should not be labeled identical. The exercises here are newly written introductory studies, not claims to reproduce a named historical dance. Historical citations and qualified practitioner review are pending.

## Follow-along practice
Warm up four minutes with walking, small hip hinges, and easy breathing. Place hands on the front of the pelvis to sense orientation. Over counts 1–2 explore a tiny posterior tilt and return; 3–4 explore a tiny anterior tilt and return without arching hard; 5–8 rest in a comfortable neutral position. Repeat four cycles. Next, perform a separate eight-count shallow knee-led pulse with the pelvis relaxed. Alternate the two studies, 10–15 minutes with breaks. Keep the range small; do not force lumbar extension.

## Accessible adaptation
Seated on a stable chair, explore a tiny pelvic rock with feet planted and breathing easy. This can clarify pelvic orientation but differs from standing load transfer and is not equivalent to the bounce.

## Self-correction
Lower-back pinching: stop and reduce range; use the supported alternative if comfortable. Pelvis seems fixed: slow down and use tactile feedback. Breath held: exhale gently on the change of direction.

## Apply and reflect
Write or record one eight-count pelvic tilt study and one knee-led pulse study; describe the different movement initiators and one observable cue.

## Four-question knowledge check
**1. Which study changes pelvic orientation intentionally?**

A. Small anterior/posterior tilt

B. Only a shoulder shrug

C. A static pose

D. A hand clap

**Key: A.** Small anterior/posterior tilt is the correct interpretation for this introductory study.

**2. What helps separate pelvic tilt from knee-led bounce?**

A. Practice each action slowly before combining

B. Move as fast as possible

C. Keep the knees locked

D. Force the lower back to arch

**Key: A.** Practice each action slowly before combining.

**3. Which cue describes a knee-led bounce?**

A. A neck snap

B. Small knee flexion and return

C. A full-body jump

D. A forced backbend

**Key: B.** Small knee flexion and return.

**4. If a pelvic tilt causes low-back discomfort, what is appropriate?**

A. Increase the range

B. Hold the end position

C. Reduce range or stop and use a comfortable alternative

D. Add weight

**Key: C.** Reduce range or stop and use a comfortable alternative.

## Completion checkpoint
Explain the action, reproduce a comfortable eight-count study twice, identify one self-correction, and describe the difference between personal practice and an attributed cultural technique. Written or video submission is optional; a written answer does not prove movement proficiency.

## Production gate
Requires original or authorized, technique-reviewed instructor demonstration, useful front/side views, captions, media playback and browser acceptance before publication as a video-complete class.
