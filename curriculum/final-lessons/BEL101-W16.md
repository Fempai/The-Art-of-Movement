## Learning goal and explanation

Demonstrate a comfortable original foundation phrase and compare documented movement choices with Week 1 and the Week 8 midterm. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** The final assesses self-directed progress, not expert certification, historical authenticity, or maximal range.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** Counts 1–2 small hip slide right and return; 3–4 slide left and return; 5–6 a tiny comfortable circle; 7–8 two slow alternating knee pulses. Repeat twice, documenting support, timing, and corrections.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** Use seated marking, a slower count, or omit any action that causes discomfort; document the adaptation.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the adapted phrase twice at a steady count, identify one observable change, and explain one limitation. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. What is the strongest evidence of progress between Weeks 1, 8, and 16?**

A. A larger movement in the final recording, even if the count structure changed

B. A smoother-looking final attempt without a record of timing or support

C. A longer final performance without comparing the same movement tasks

D. Recorded improvements in count accuracy, support, or repeatable corrections

**2. The learner uses a seated version for the final but stood in Week 8. How should they compare?**

A. Compare only the movement range without noting the support difference

B. Document the adaptation and compare observable timing and sequencing

C. Treat the two performances as directly equivalent without documenting the change

D. Compare only whether both performances ended on count 8

**3. A learner misses counts 7–8 while combining the circle and pulses. What is a useful next step?**

A. Slow down and practice the transition from the circle into two pulses

B. Rehearse the circle alone, without linking it to the final pulses

C. Reassign the final pulses to counts 6–7 without documenting the change

D. Repeat the full phrase at the same tempo while watching only the opening slides

**4. What does successful completion of the final establish?**

A. Self-documented completion of the phrase, which demonstrates mastery of every related hip technique

B. An accurate final count sequence, which confirms the video source demonstrates the same choreography

C. Self-documented performance of this original foundation sequence

D. Repeatability at the chosen tempo, which establishes the same result under every movement modification

### Answer key and explanations

**1. D.** Comparable observations about counts, support, and corrections show specific changes without treating visual size as the only measure.

**2. B.** Recording the change in conditions preserves an interpretable comparison and allows timing and movement-order observations to remain meaningful.

**3. A.** The error occurs at the change between movements, so slower rehearsal of counts 5–8 directly addresses it.

**4. C.** The final documents this learner's chosen adaptations and repeatable foundation phrase; it does not certify expert technique or cultural provenance.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.
