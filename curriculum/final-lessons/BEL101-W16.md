## Learning goal and explanation

Demonstrate a comfortable original foundation phrase and compare documented movement choices with Week 1 and the Week 8 midterm **only for actions and conditions that are actually comparable**. Week 1's right–center–left–center weight-shift study is **not** the same full phrase as Week 16's slides, circle, and pulses. Describe the weight support, pathway, rhythm, and difference between a beginner drill and a historically attributed movement.

**Concept distinction:** The final assesses self-directed progress, not expert certification, historical authenticity, or maximal range.

**Optional source-observation study (3–5 minutes, if accessible):** Observe one hip-work action and one alternating shimmy action in the two scheduled sources, recording the visible count structure and support separately. Compare only genuinely matched actions with the written final phrase; do not assume the video actions reproduce its circle-to-pulse transition. For progress claims, use documented like-for-like earlier trials at comparable support and tempo; if those records are absent, report **no matched baseline** rather than an improvement. If either source is inaccessible, mark that source **not observed** individually.

## Follow-along practice

**Warm up (4–5 minutes):** Walk or march gently, shift weight slowly, and mobilize ankles and shoulders in a comfortable range. Clear a nonslip space and keep a stable support nearby. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or repeated loss of control.

**Guided count-by-count study (8–12 minutes):** From a stable supported stance, count 1: small hip slide right; 2: return to center; 3: small slide left; 4: return to center. Counts 5–6: trace one tiny comfortable circle, starting and finishing near neutral; hand-trace first if the path is unclear. Count 7: one gentle right-knee pulse; 8: one gentle left-knee pulse. Practice the **5–6 circle into 7–8 pulses** separately before two full eight-count attempts, without adding an extra beat at the transition. Record count accuracy, support, and any corrections for each attempt; distinguish an observed performance from a claimed improvement.

**Three repetitions (5–8 minutes):** First mark slowly while saying each count; next identify the body part or contact point supporting each movement; finally vary one accent without increasing range. Rest between repetitions. If coordination breaks down, practice the components separately before recombining.

**Modification:** In a stable nonrolling chair with feet supported, mark rightward pressure or a rightward hand trace on count 1, return to neutral on 2, leftward pressure or hand trace on 3, and return on 4. Hand-trace a small complete circle over counts 5–6, returning toward its starting point by the end of 6. Mark a gentle **right-hand or right-foot** pulse on 7 and a **left-hand or left-foot** pulse on 8, without forcing a seated knee action. A slower count or omission of uncomfortable motion is acceptable; record exactly which actions were substituted or omitted. Hand tracing and foot-pressure timing document the adapted sequence, not standing pelvic circles or alternating standing knee mechanics.

**Self-correction:** If your knees twist, return to parallel and reduce range. If the back compresses or balance becomes uncertain, shorten the pathway or remain seated. If you lose timing, clap eight counts and add only one movement at a time. Do not force visible isolations, rapid abdominal work, or extreme spinal motion.

**Progression check:** Repeat the eight-count phrase twice at a comfortable recorded tempo and note support and any substitution for each attempt. Score **right 1 / center 2 / left 3 / center 4 / circle starts 5 and completes 6 / right pulse 7 / left pulse 8** separately as **on count / off count / unobservable / intentionally omitted**. Report on-count actions out of **observable, attempted actions**; do not count unobservable or intentionally omitted actions as successes or failures, and write **not assessable** when none can be observed. Record separately whether the circle-to-pulse transition occurred without an extra beat and whether the observed action was a seated hand/foot substitution rather than standing technique. Compare these two current trials first. For longer-term comparison, use only genuinely matched tasks: Week 1's right–center–left–center pressure-shift timing may be compared to a separately repeated version of that same Week 1 drill, **not directly to the different Week 16 full phrase**. Week 8's slide/circle/settle phrase differs from Week 16's circle/pulses ending. **Counts 1–4 use the same nominal slide-and-return order, but a comparison of counts 5–6 is valid only if the two recorded circles actually use comparable direction, start/end position, range, support, and tempo; sharing the label 'circle' is not enough.** If the circle pathways cannot be matched, compare counts 1–4 only and describe the circles separately without claiming progress. Without matched earlier evidence, report current performance rather than improvement. This is a self-study checkpoint, not a claim of injury prevention or expert skill.

## Four-question knowledge check

**1. What is the strongest evidence of progress across Weeks 1, 8, and 16 when the learner has records of genuinely matched movement tasks and comparable support and tempo?**

A. A larger movement in the final recording, even if the count structure changed

B. A smoother-looking final attempt without a record of timing or support

C. A longer final performance without comparing the same movement tasks

D. Recorded improvements in count accuracy or repeatable corrections for the same task under documented comparable conditions

**2. A learner has a standing Week 8 recording and a seated hand-traced Week 16 recording. Both show the slide-and-return order on counts 1–4, but support and movement execution differ. Which comparison is justified?**

A. Conclude standing pelvic technique improved because the seated hand traces are on time

B. Document the adaptation and compare observable count timing and sequence only, without claiming equivalent standing mechanics or directly comparable physical proficiency

C. Treat both complete eight-count phrases as the same task because they begin with right–center–left–center

D. Infer improvement in hip-circle range from the seated hand-traced circle without matched pathway evidence

**3. A learner misses counts 7–8 while combining the circle and pulses. What is a useful next step?**

A. Slow down and practice the transition from the circle into two pulses

B. Rehearse the circle alone, without linking it to the final pulses

C. Reassign the final pulses to counts 6–7 without documenting the change

D. Repeat the full phrase at the same tempo while watching only the opening slides

**4. What does successful completion of the final establish?**

A. Self-documented completion of the phrase, which demonstrates mastery of every related hip technique

B. An accurate final count sequence, which confirms the video source demonstrates the same choreography

C. Self-documented performance of this original foundation sequence

D. Repeatability at the chosen tempo, which establishes the same result under every movement modification

### Answer key and explanations

**1. D.** Evidence of improvement requires comparing the same movement task using documented, sufficiently comparable support and tempo, such as a separately repeated Week 1 pressure-shift drill or matching Week 8/16 opening counts. The different full phrases cannot be treated as one longitudinal measure. A changes the count structure, B lacks timing/support evidence, and C compares unlike tasks by length alone.

**2. B.** Counts 1–4 can be described for their observed order and timing with the seated adaptation explicitly labeled; changed support and symbolic hand tracing prevent a direct claim of improved standing pelvic mechanics or physical proficiency. A equates symbolic timing with standing skill, C conflates different complete phrases, and D infers hip-circle range without matched movement evidence.

**3. A.** Slowing the circle-to-pulse transition on counts 5–8 directly targets the missed final accents. B omits the link, C silently moves the pulses, and D focuses on the opening slides at the same problematic tempo.

**4. C.** The final supports a claim of self-documented performance of this original foundation sequence. A overstates mastery of related techniques, B claims unverified source correspondence, and D assumes success transfers to every modification.

**Assessment limit:** These original questions assess introductory understanding, not professional dance competence. Review the explanation and retry after any missed answer.

## Independent assignment

Write a count-by-count movement score for two Week 16 attempts and a 150–250-word reflection. For each count, mark **on count / off count / unobservable / intentionally omitted** and report the number on count **out of observable, attempted actions**. Exclude unobservable and intentionally omitted actions from the denominator; if no attempted action can be observed, report **not assessable**. Label seated hand/foot substitutions as adapted timing evidence, not demonstrated standing hip technique. Report each attempt's numerator and denominator separately, rather than silently treating all eight counts as observed. **Separately assess the 6→7 circle-to-pulse transition:** record whether the circle finishes by the end of count 6 and the right pulse begins on 7 without an extra beat; mark **on time / late / unobservable / intentionally omitted**. A transition is **not assessable** if either component is unobservable or omitted, and its result must not be added as a ninth movement count or used to inflate the eight-action denominator. Note any seated hand/foot substitute as adapted transition timing, not a standing-technique equivalent. Compare Week 16 evidence with available Week 1 and Week 8 records **only on matched actions**: optionally repeat Week 1's original right–center–left–center pressure-shift drill for a like-for-like comparison; for Week 8, compare the **slide-and-return timing on counts 1–4**. Compare the circles on **5–6 only when the recorded direction, start/end position, movement range, support, and tempo are sufficiently matched**; otherwise document each circle separately rather than calculating a progress score across unlike pathways. **Do not compare the different final counts 7–8**. Keep support conditions, tempo, and criteria matched where feasible. Record support, pathway, rhythm, one adjustment, and an observable difference for each comparable interval. Disclose changes in tempo, recording angle, or seated versus standing adaptation, and identify any missing baseline instead of inventing progress. Explain one claim you cannot verify from practice alone. Use a video only when you have permission to view it and can access the restored source link.

**Rubric:** accurate counts and vocabulary 30%; repeatable comfortable execution 25%; self-correction and modifications 25%; careful evidence and cultural attribution 20%. **Scoring interpretation:** The on-count/observable-attempted fraction describes only timing accuracy for attempted, observable actions; it is **not** the assignment grade or a completion percentage. Report separately (1) which of the eight planned actions were attempted, omitted, adapted, or unobservable, (2) the timing fraction for assessable actions, and (3) the separate 6→7 transition result. A high fraction with most actions omitted does **not** demonstrate completion of the full phrase. For the 30% count-accuracy criterion, evaluate documented count order and vocabulary alongside the extent of the attempted phrase; for the 25% execution criterion, evaluate only comfortable, repeatable actions actually evidenced. Do not penalize a safety-motivated adaptation as a failure of standing technique; instead label the standing-technique outcome **not demonstrated**. Do not grade speed, maximal range, or unsupported historical claims.
