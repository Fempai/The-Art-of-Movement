# PER201 · Lesson 03 — Stage Presence: Stillness, Commitment, and Recovery

**Performance & Storytelling | 40–55 minutes | independent learner.** This is an authored lesson revision, not a professionally adjudicated performance assessment. Bibliographic and live-browser audits remain outstanding.

## Learn: the performance principle

By the end, identify a visible expressive variable, perform two contrasting versions of a phrase, and critique a recording without confusing observable facts with presumed audience emotions.

Stage presence is often discussed as an overall impression, but solo learners need observable components to practice. Clear entrances, committed initiation, deliberate stillness, and complete recoveries are more useful feedback targets than 'be charismatic.' Stillness is an active compositional choice when the dancer holds a defined orientation and continues breathing. Commitment does not mean muscular tension or dangerous intensity; it means completing the intended movement pathway and timing without abandoning it midway. A camera angle changes what is visible, so evaluate multiple takes under the same framing.

## Guided performance studio

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Clear a nonslip area and choose a safe front-facing reference point. Warm up with easy steps, gentle arm reaches, and comfortable head movements. Seated or small-range marking is acceptable; no leaps or forced movements are required.

**Perform (15 minutes):** Begin facing front with feet comfortably supported. Perform A: 1–2 step and settle, 3–4 gather, 5–6 reach, 7 recover, 8 hold a balanced stillness. Perform B: repeat but let count 8 become an unplanned fidget or extra step. Compare the visible endings. Next practice a clear entrance: start outside the marked central performance area, walk into position over four slow counts, pause on **5–6**, then **remain comfortably supported on 7–8** before starting the motif on the **next count 1** (a separate eight-count phrase). If walking is unsuitable, begin seated in a stable nonrolling chair and mark the entrance with a comfortable arm pathway on 1–4, pause 5–6, settle 7–8, then start the seated motif on the next 1. The seated version marks entrance timing, not walking travel. Film two takes from the same position if possible, or use a written observation grid.

**Observe and revise (5 minutes):** If stillness becomes breath-holding, release unnecessary tension and breathe. If you add steps to avoid falling, prioritize balance and revise the choreography rather than suppressing needed support. If a take seems less 'present,' identify the exact observable difference before judging it.

**Undercurrent application:** Record the count, facing, gaze, pathway, and expressive intention separately. Ask what actually changed between versions. If using video, preserve the same camera position and note what falls outside the frame.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is an observable component of stage presence?**

A. The dancer's natural confidence inferred from a single held pose

B. A deliberate pause on count 8

C. The emotion that every audience member experiences during the pause

D. The performer's future stage success inferred from one rehearsal

**2. What does commitment mean in this practice?**

A. Applying maximal force even when the intended movement is small

B. Completing the intended pathway and timing

C. Preserving the original pathway despite losing safe support

D. Holding the breath to make a pause appear more intense

**3. Why use the same camera position for comparison?**

A. To ensure the audience interprets both versions identically

B. To make a movement look larger by changing the camera distance

C. To reduce differences caused by framing

D. To eliminate differences in choreography between recordings

**4. What if the performer needs an extra step to maintain balance?**

A. Keep the original sequence and hide the corrective step from the recording

B. Keep the same unstable transition but remove the following pause

C. Increase travel speed to avoid showing the balance adjustment

D. Adjust the choreography or use support

### Answers and rationales

**1. B.** A deliberate pause on count 8 is observable and can be compared across recordings. Natural confidence inferred from a pose (A), a universal audience emotion (C), and predictions of future success (D) cannot be established from one rehearsal.

**2. B.** Here commitment means carrying the chosen pathway and timing through with control. Maximal force (A), maintaining a pathway after losing safe support (C), and breath-holding for intensity (D) are unnecessary and potentially unsafe substitutes.

**3. C.** Using the same camera position reduces differences caused by angle and distance, helping compare the performed movement. It cannot guarantee identical interpretation (A), relies on no deliberate distance change (B), and does not erase genuine choreography differences (D).

**4. D.** If a recovery step is needed, adjust the phrase or use suitable support so the movement remains controlled. Hiding the step (A), keeping an unstable transition (B), or accelerating travel (C) avoids correcting the underlying balance problem.

**Self-review:** For any missed question, label the alternatives as observation, interpretation, or unsupported generalization. This knowledge check is original and not a validated audience-response measure.

## Independent assignment

Compare the guided eight-count ending A (balanced stillness on count 8) with B (unplanned fidget or extra step on 8), using a stable camera angle or written observation grid. Then score a four-count entrance, a pause on counts 5–6, **supported settling on 7–8**, and the following eight-count motif starting on the **next count 1**; clearly mark where the entrance ends and the new phrase begins. Identify at least three observable differences in entrance, recovery, stillness, or support, and explain one safe revision. Submit a 250–350-word critique distinguishing visible actions from a subjective judgment of stage presence; do not suppress a necessary balance step.

**Worked example:** “In take A I recovered on count 7 and remained still, supported, and breathing on 8. In the comparison take I added an unintended foot adjustment on 8, so the ending looked less defined. I also scored my entrance as four walking counts followed by a pause on 5–6, with the next eight-count motif starting as a new phrase. If the extra step is necessary for balance, I will change the score rather than force stillness.”

**Rubric:** Observable specificity 30%; reproducible performance choices 25%; clear distinction between intention and interpretation 25%; justified revision and reflection 20%. A self-score does not establish professional stage readiness.
