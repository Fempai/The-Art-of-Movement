# CMP502 · Lesson 03 — Accumulation: Building a Phrase One Event at a Time

**Advanced Composition | graduate-level creative inquiry | 50–70 minutes.** This is an authored learner-facing revision; bibliographic audit, expert review, and browser acceptance testing remain pending.

## Lecture: define the compositional device

**Learning goals:** Explain the featured device, construct a reproducible movement score, identify its difference from simple repetition, and justify a revision without claiming the resulting work has a universal emotional meaning.

Accumulation builds a phrase by adding material according to a declared rule. In one common version, a dancer performs A; then A+B; then A+B+C; then A+B+C+D. This differs from merely repeating A four times. Accumulation can emphasize memory, expectation, and the changing relationship of an action to what follows. The rule must specify whether each stage begins again from A or whether new material is appended without restarting. For a self-taught learner, a written score makes the pattern auditable and reduces memory confusion.

## Guided choreography laboratory

**Prepare (5 minutes):** Warm up with comfortable walking or seated shifts and gentle arm movements. Use an open nonslip area. No falls, forced ranges, or unsupported inversions are required.

**Build and rehearse (20 minutes):** Define four safe actions, each lasting two counts: A = step right and settle; B = gather hands; C = diagonal reach; D = return arm. Perform stage 1: A (2 counts). Stage 2: A+B (4 counts). Stage 3: A+B+C (6 counts). Stage 4: A+B+C+D (8 counts). Speak the action letters before each stage. Rest, then retrieve the four stages without looking. On a second pass, alter only C's direction, documenting whether the addition rule stays the same. If a standing step is unsuitable, replace A with a seated weight shift.

**Troubleshooting and critical editing:** If you accidentally omit B in stage 3, practice the stage at a slower count. If you add an unscored gesture, either remove it or revise the rule. Do not claim an accumulation is an exact eight-count phrase at every stage: the stages have different lengths.

**Analytical comparison:** Record the original motif and its transformation in separate columns: event, count, pathway, support, and changed compositional rule. Explain which elements remain recognizable and why. If a video is unavailable, a detailed count score is sufficient.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which is the taught accumulation rule?**

A. A, A+B, A+B+C, A+B+C+D

B. A, B, C, D, replacing each earlier event rather than retaining it

C. A, B, A, B, repeating two events without adding a third

D. A, A+B, B+C, C+D, dropping earlier events at each stage

**2. How many counts are in stage 3 if each action lasts two counts?**

A. Two counts, counting only the newly added C action

B. Four counts, omitting the third two-count action

C. Six

D. Eight counts, including D before it is added at stage 4

**3. What is a key distinction from simple repetition?**

A. Repeating A four times creates accumulation without adding material

B. New material is added under a stated rule

C. A new event may replace A entirely while still following this taught rule

D. Accumulation requires every stage to have identical duration

**4. What should a dancer do after accidentally omitting B?**

A. Treat the omission as the intended accumulation rule without documenting the change

B. Repeat the shortened stage until it feels familiar without checking the source

C. Increase tempo so the missing event is less noticeable

D. Rehearse the correct stage and compare with the rule

### Answer key with reasoning

**1. A.** Each stage begins with A and adds one event.

**2. C.** Three actions at two counts each total six.

**3. B.** The progressive addition creates the form.

**4. D.** Feedback depends on a defined target.

**Self-review:** For any missed item, correct the compositional rule and demonstrate the difference in a smaller four-count version. These questions are original instructional checks, not a validated professional assessment.

## Independent assignment

Submit a movement score, a performance or private rehearsal log, and a 250–350-word reflection explaining the transformation, an observable weakness, and a justified revision. **Lesson 04 capstone:** create a **2–4 minute original composition** integrating at least three devices (for example retrograde, accumulation, and fragmentation/chance), with count-level timing, section boundaries, and an artist statement. A private video is optional; the score must stand alone.

**Worked example:** “My source sequence is step–gather–reach–return. The retrograde score starts by extending the arm from center, then draws it inward. I replaced a backward weight shift with a small side shift because the literal reverse was unstable. I preserved the event-order reversal and documented the safety edit.”

**Rubric:** Device accuracy and transparent rules 30%; coherent, executable transitions 25%; originality and structural development 25%; critique, timing accuracy, and revision rationale 20%. The self-score is not a professional adjudication.
