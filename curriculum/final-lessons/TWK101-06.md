# TWK101 · Lesson 06 — Tempo, Subdivisions, and Accents

**Standalone twerk foundations | learner-facing written pilot | 35–45 minutes.** This is not a verified instructor-led video class.

## Objective
Perform a consistent movement pattern with two different accent maps without mistaking numbered beats for offbeats.

## Cultural and technique context
Modern twerking is strongly associated with Black social dance and New Orleans bounce culture. Similar-looking actions across traditions should not be labeled identical. This is an original teaching exercise, not a named historical step. Historical sourcing and practitioner review are still pending.

## Follow-along practice
Warm up with easy stepping and gentle pulses. Set a comfortable four-beat pulse by counting 1–2–3–4 aloud. Over eight counts, perform four small down/up pulses on counts 1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–8. Next keep the same timing but emphasize count 3 rather than count 1. Only when comfortable, say '1 and 2 and' while marking the 'and' with a small hand gesture, not a faster forced pelvic movement. Practice 10–15 minutes including rest.

## Accessible adaptation
Seated pressure pulses or upper-body accents preserve count placement; they do not reproduce standing mechanics.

## Self-correction and safety
Confusing offbeats: distinguish numbered beats from spoken 'and' subdivisions. Speeding up: return to the base pulse. Fatigue: rest rather than increasing range. Stop for pain, dizziness, numbness or repeated loss of balance.

## Apply and reflect
Write the eight counts for two accent choices and label any between-beat subdivision explicitly.

## Four-question knowledge check
**1. Which phrase names a subdivision between numbered beats?**

A. Count 3 alone

B. The spoken 'and' in '1 and 2 and'

C. Count 1 alone

D. Count 4 alone

**Key: B.** The spoken 'and' in '1 and 2 and'.

**2. How does the second drill change the first?**

A. It doubles squat depth

B. It replaces music with silence

C. It shifts the emphasis from count 1 to count 3

D. It removes the count

**Key: C.** It shifts the emphasis from count 1 to count 3.

**3. Why use a hand gesture for the 'and' at first?**

A. To prove glute isolation

B. To increase joint impact

C. To eliminate the pulse

D. To explore timing without forcing faster pelvic action

**Key: D.** To explore timing without forcing faster pelvic action.

**4. If the learner rushes ahead, what should they do?**

A. Return to the base pulse and count aloud

B. Increase speed further

C. Add more movements

D. Ignore the timing

**Key: A.** Return to the base pulse and count aloud.

## Completion checkpoint
Perform or notate two comfortable eight-count phrases; identify the movement initiator, one correction, and an adaptation. A written response does not prove physical proficiency.
