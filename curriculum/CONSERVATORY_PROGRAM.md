# The Art of Movement — Conservatory curriculum architecture
Status: proposed academic framework, not an accredited degree, instructor certification, or verified professional qualification.

## Educational design
Every class includes: essential question; historical/cultural context with sources to be curated; anatomy/biomechanics and safety; technical drills; a four- or eight-count combination; application to an original recurring choreography; improvisation or composition lab; written reflection; assessment rubric (alignment, precision, musicality, expression, retention); progression gate. Never award mastery from page views alone. Video demonstration requires technically reviewed, authorized footage.

## Three stages
### I. Foundations — bachelor's-level breadth (not a degree)
Terms 1–4: movement literacy; alignment, posture, safe stretching, joint structure, skeletal muscles, flexibility versus mobility, hip mechanics and turnout; ballet, jazz, contemporary, hip-hop, floorwork and acro prerequisites; Pilates and yoga for dancers; rhythm, meter, tempo, syncopation, phrasing; dance history and cultural origins; Laban Movement Analysis (Body/Effort/Shape/Space), improvisation and dance notation; choreography memory; basic dance criticism and aesthetics. Weekly technique, reading, short written response, creative task and filmed self-review (private only).

### II. Choreography & Research — master's-level inquiry (not a degree)
Terms 5–6: motif and variation, choreographic devices, canon, retrograde, inversion, chance choreography, narrative vs abstraction, site-specific work, performance studies, audience attention, storytelling, musical dramaturgy, Feldenkrais and Alexander principles, critical interpretation, research methods and cultural attribution. Capstone: original 2–4 minute work with artist statement, annotated score and revision journal.

### III. Performance Laboratory — advanced professional-style training (not certification)
Terms 7–8+: staged repertory, partnering prerequisites, performance endurance, spatial adaptability, rehearsal direction, audition simulation, audience engagement, technical polish and full-length capstone. Advanced inversions/contortion only with qualified in-person supervision and readiness screening.

## Spiral repertory: one original dance, evolving
Repertory title: **Undercurrent**. Theme: restraint becoming freedom. Meter: 4/4, adaptable to original/cleared music; 8-count phrase notation is descriptive, not audio playback.
- Level 1 motif: counts 1–2 step right and settle; 3–4 draw left foot in, arms gather; 5–6 reach diagonally and rotate torso; 7–8 recover to neutral and suspend.
- Level 2: vary direction, facing and levels while retaining recognizable reach/gather motif.
- Level 3: add counter-rhythm, weight transfer, controlled pivot and safe floor-to-stand transition.
- Level 4: use dynamics (bound/free, sudden/sustained), asymmetry, breath and pauses.
- Level 5: transform motif through retrograde, fragmentation and spatial pathways.
- Level 6: compose 4 phrases with introduction, development, climax and resolution.
- Level 7: refine musical phrasing, stamina, expressive intent and audience sightlines.
- Level 8: performance-quality version with original artistic interpretation; technical features only after individually demonstrated prerequisites.

## Three detailed classes — initial release
### Class 1: Choreographic Memory — Encode, Chunk, Retrieve (45 minutes)
**Focus:** learn an original 32-count phrase accurately and retain it the next day.
**Reading:** working memory and retrieval practice; distinction between procedural learning and explicit cues. Avoid claiming a fixed number of repetitions creates muscle memory.
**Technique (10m):** learn four 8-count units; label each with one action verb, one direction, one rhythmic landmark. Practice slowly with verbal counts, then without counts.
**Combination (10m):** perform Level 1 Undercurrent motif twice, mirrored once, then link with a 4-count walking transition; keep knees soft and steps comfortable.
**Choreography (10m):** assemble 32 counts by alternating motif and transition; mark floor pathways on paper.
**Recall (10m):** perform from memory; take a 2-minute break; retrieve without looking; record 3 missed transitions; retry only those.
**Homework (5m):** write cue sheet and repeat from memory tomorrow, then after 3 days.
**Assessment:** 4/4 phrase order, correct orientation, recognizable timing and controlled transitions at 70–80% tempo. No penalties for adapting movements to available range.

### Class 2: Choreography Composition — Motif to Narrative (60 minutes)
**Focus:** turn a single gesture into a coherent story without miming every lyric.
**Reading:** motif, repetition, variation, contrast, accumulation, climax, abstraction and literal narrative.
**Technique (15m):** take reach/gather motif through high, middle and low levels without dropping; change tempo, direction and effort quality.
**Combination (15m):** Phrase A: 8 counts of reach/gather; B: 8 counts of directional travel; C: 8 counts of suspension and reversal; D: 8 counts of resolution.
**Choreography (15m):** arrange A-B-A'-C-D; vary A' by changing level and tempo. Write intent for each phrase (desire, resistance, choice, release).
**Lab (10m):** improvise 60 seconds with one constraint: every movement must originate in a reach or a withdrawal.
**Assessment (5m):** motif legible, at least two deliberate variations, purposeful transitions, and beginning-middle-end. Journal: what changed emotionally when movement changed?

### Class 3: Musicality — Rhythm, Silence and Syncopation (50 minutes)
**Focus:** hear and embody the difference between beat, subdivision, phrase and accent.
**Reading:** meter (4/4 vs 3/4), tempo, phrasing, syncopation; cultural context varies by style, do not flatten diverse dance traditions into one technique.
**Technique (10m):** walk quarter-note pulse; clap eighth-note subdivisions; pause on count 4; keep internal pulse.
**Combination (15m):** Undercurrent motif at even counts, then repeat with a delayed reach on the 'and' of 5; maintain safe transfer of weight.
**Choreography (15m):** place one intentional stillness, one offbeat accent and one sustained movement across two counts in a 32-count phrase.
**Lab (5m):** perform without music using internal count, then with a metronome or cleared track.
**Assessment (5m):** beat stability, intentional rather than accidental syncopation, readable phrasing and dynamic contrast.

## Personalized learning and retention
The student benefits from a dual score: (1) vivid internal movement movie and (2) external written movement score. Use a five-column choreography log: count/time | body action | floor pathway/facing | musical cue | emotion/imagery. Add a quick overhead floor diagram and short private reference recording. Immediately after spontaneous composition, capture a voice memo of imagery and key movements before reconstructing exact counts. Study through chunking, retrieval, spaced repetition, and varied tempo; practice slow accurately before speed. 'Learn fast' means fewer errors and faster recall, not skipping foundations.

## Strength and flexibility tracks
- Strict pull-up: rows, scapular control, assisted reps, eccentric lowering; assess a controlled full-range single rep.
- Full push-ups: elevated reps, progressive lowering of incline, trunk control; assess 10 full-range continuous reps.
- Dance lower body: hip flexors, adductors, glutes, calves, hamstrings, trunk stability and controlled eccentric work; dance does not replace all resistance training.
- Advanced extensions: compare passive vs active range, supported holds, progressively higher controlled lifts; no ankle weights for ballistic kicks.
- Backbends: shoulder flexion, thoracic mobility, hip extension, glute/trunk strength; no forced lumbar hinging or unsupervised head-to-foot attempts.
- Inversions: wrist loading, shoulder stability, safe exits; progress under qualified supervision.
- Stamina: interval phrases and aerobic base; quality and recovery dictate dose.

## Assessment schedule
Baseline and every 4 weeks: pain-free active extension height and hold time; controlled kick quality; 2-minute phrase with timing consistency; floor-to-standing transitions; push-up and pull-up progression; recall of 32-count phrase at 24 hours and 7 days. Never force extreme range to meet a numeric benchmark.
