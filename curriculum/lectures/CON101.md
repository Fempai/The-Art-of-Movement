# CON101 — Contemporary & Floorwork

**Stage:** Foundation. **Course scope:** Breath, initiation, suspension, safe floor-to-stand patterns. **Learning outcome:** Control transitions between seated, kneeling and standing.

**Instructional design:** Four 60-minute seminars with additional independent reading and practice. These are original guided lecture notes and teaching plans, not a full-semester university transcript, accredited credit hours or externally validated examinations.

## Lecture 1 — Breath

**Essential inquiry:** Which quality distinguishes a deliberate artistic choice from an accident?

### Opening lecture: define the problem (0–12 minutes)

Contemporary dance encompasses diverse lineages; no single contraction-release system represents every contemporary form. In the context of **Contemporary & Floorwork**, this principle helps explain the practical question: Which quality distinguishes a deliberate artistic choice from an accident? Begin by defining the movement or concept, specifying the conditions in which it occurs, and distinguishing a visible result from the underlying process. A useful university-level explanation does not merely name a body part or aesthetic quality: it identifies relevant variables, articulates what evidence would support a claim, and explains when that claim might not apply.

### Analytical development (12–25 minutes)

Floorwork requires planning where weight transfers and how the dancer distributes pressure through hands, feet and other contacts. Consider the relationship between the central concept and **breath**. Compare two possible executions or interpretations. What remains constant? Which single variable changes? How might an observer describe the difference without assuming the dancer's intention? Distinguish the physical demands of a movement from its artistic interpretation. A dancer can perform a technically clear action without creating a particular audience response, and a compelling artistic decision does not eliminate biomechanical constraints.

Breath can support phrasing and attention but does not mechanically guarantee safety. Treat this as a testable proposition, not a universal rule. A meaningful critique identifies limitations such as prior training, individual anatomy, cultural context, tempo, surface conditions, fatigue, or observer perspective. Where empirical evidence is available, evaluate the sample and methods; where evidence is interpretive, make that framework explicit.

### Guided demonstration and movement laboratory (25–43 minutes)

**Instructor demonstration:** 3 slow side-sit → kneel → stand sequences per side

**Phrase to teach:** Counts 1–2 establish stance; 3–4 execute a comfortable version of the focus action; 5–6 repeat with a directional change; 7–8 recover and suspend.

**Technique cues:** Rehearse at a manageable tempo; name the initiating body segment, weight-bearing surface, direction of travel, and intended end position. Use a fixed support for balance if needed. Pause to compare your intended pathway with the actual pathway. Avoid forcing turnout, range, backbend depth, or unsupported inversions. When fatigued, reduce complexity rather than chasing a larger shape.

**Analytical exercise:** Perform the phrase twice. On the first repetition, record three observable facts (e.g., timing, direction, control). On the second, deliberately change only one parameter: speed, facing, effort, pathway or level. Explain whether the central movement idea remains recognizable. Apply breath to Undercurrent's reach-and-gather motif. Document counts, direction, musical accent, and artistic intention.

### Seminar discussion and research (43–53 minutes)

**Prompt 1:** Which quality distinguishes a deliberate artistic choice from an accident?

**Prompt 2:** How could two dancers or two observers reasonably disagree about the outcome? Identify what would settle a factual dispute and what would remain a matter of interpretation.

**Prompt 3:** Which assumption in today's discussion requires a credible source? Use a source from the bibliography below, identify its author or institutional publisher, and state whether it is primary research, a review, or professional guidance. Do not treat a general education page as equivalent to a controlled study.

### Retrieval, assignment and assessment (53–60 minutes)

**Recall without notes:** Explain the central concept in two sentences; describe the movement in eight counts; name one limitation of your explanation.

**Written assignment:** Write 150–250 words answering: Which quality distinguishes a deliberate artistic choice from an accident? Include an observation, a technical explanation, and one revision to the phrase.

**Assessment:** Academic: correct terminology and source awareness. Technical: pain-free control, consistent rhythm and repeatable transitions. Creative: intentional and legible variation. No pass/fail on extreme range. Academic criteria: conceptual accuracy (30%), applied analysis (25%), credible source integration (20%), clarity and reflection (25%). Technical mastery is assessed separately and cannot be inferred from written answers. **This rubric is instructor-authored and has not been externally validated.**
