# The Art of Movement — Academy Curriculum Standard (v1 draft)

## Scope and correction
Level I begins with dance, not prerequisite-only fitness. The core dance foundations are **ballet, jazz, modern/contemporary, hip-hop, and belly dance**. These are parallel introductory disciplines, not a claim that ballet is a universal prerequisite for all cultural dance forms. Ballet and jazz supply valuable technique for many concert/commercial dance pathways, while hip-hop and belly dance have independent histories and valid technique frameworks. Dancehall belongs to its own style-specific pathway, not a miscellaneous belly-dance exercise.

## Academic structure
- **Level I / Foundation:** novice technique, vocabulary, alignment, musical counts, combinations, basic expression and safe adaptation.
- **Level II / Developing:** longer combinations, dynamic control, stronger coordination and style-specific intermediate vocabulary.
- **Level III / Proficient:** complex sequences, performance dynamics, improvisation, endurance and cross-context application.
- **Level IV / Advanced:** high-complexity choreography, advanced performance quality and specialized technique.
- **Specialty tracks:** heels, dancehall, twerk, burlesque/showgirl, breaking, krump, acro and advanced floorwork have independent Level I entries where suitable. No requirement to finish all core styles before beginning a specialty; prerequisites are skill- and safety-specific.

## Level I video class production contract
A **class is not complete without a licensed or original, playable, instructor-led follow-along video**. The minimum standard for each module:
1. Visible instructor demonstration with audible cueing and named technique.
2. Warm-up and preparation appropriate to movement demands.
3. Movement breakdown with both front and useful side perspectives where needed.
4. Follow-along practice with counts and music where licensing permits.
5. A short combination or applied movement phrase; dance begins at Level I.
6. Accessible options and relevant floor/space/camera setup notes.
7. Playback pause, rewind, speed and mirroring where technically feasible; warn when mirroring reverses left/right instruction.
8. Clear learning objective, key terms, typical corrections, independent reflection and progression criteria.
9. Appropriate attribution and license compliance. Public YouTube embedding is **external instruction**, not academy-owned footage or licensed redistribution.
10. Captioning/transcript or accessible written alternatives where available.

## Level I term structure
Eight class modules per discipline, **40 syllabus modules total**. Each syllabus module requires its own verified follow-along instruction and cannot be marked 'video complete' merely because the written syllabus exists.

## Assessment rules
- Completion of video is not a proficiency grade.
- Formative feedback describes supported observations with timestamps and confidence.
- Skill names follow the selected tradition: omi/umi (interior belly-dance circle) differs from dancehall wine and exterior hip circles.
- 2D pose landmarks alone cannot prove three-dimensional pelvic circumduction, muscle activation, or musicality.
- Partial visibility, walking, turns and level changes are normal choreography; grade only observable body regions and do not penalize valid framing changes.
- Formal grades require calibrated criteria and benchmark agreement with qualified instructors.

## Video content sourcing and budget
Use (a) independently published free creator videos through their authorized playback pages or embeds, (b) licensed partner lessons with explicit permissions, or (c) newly produced original lessons. Do not download/rehost third-party class videos. Verify links, embedding rights, accessibility, and costs. A paywalled external catalog must never be represented as a free included academy lesson.

## Acceptance gate
An academic Level I curriculum can be considered structurally drafted when each discipline has objectives, sequence and assessment criteria. A **functional video academy** requires the actual video lessons, accessible player, course navigation, durable progress and validated assessment. Do not report '50% complete' from the existence of outlines, placeholders or unverified external links.
