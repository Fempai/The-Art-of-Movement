# Hip circles · Pelvic circumduction — provisional technical rubric

**Status:** Instructor-designed draft for calibration, not a validated biomechanical test or an automatic certification.

## Movement definitions
- **Hip circle:** colloquial dance name. **Pelvic circumduction** is a useful descriptive term for coordinated multi-directional movement, but avoid implying the pelvis is a ball-and-socket joint or that all hip-circle styles share identical mechanics.
- **Anterior / posterior pelvic tilt:** sagittal-plane pelvic rotation; often called release/tuck.
- **Lateral pelvic tilt / pelvic obliquity:** frontal-plane elevation of one side of the pelvis relative to the other.
- **Pelvic translation:** horizontal displacement of the pelvis relative to the trunk; not equivalent to lateral tilt.
- **Thoracic stability:** controlled upper-body movement appropriate to the selected style, not absolute immobility.
- **Circumduction trajectory:** coordinated motion through front, side, back, and opposite side; actual 3D motion cannot be reconstructed from one uncalibrated 2D camera.
- **Knee flexion / extension:** may facilitate motion; avoid asserting a particular muscle is active from video.

## Four provisional dimensions, 0–3 each
1. **Movement sequence and path**: 0 no discernible sequence; 1 partial/repeated shifts; 2 recognizable continuous circle with uneven sections; 3 continuous, repeatable trajectory. *Evidence*: observable front/side/back phases, ideally two camera angles.
2. **Pelvic-trunk dissociation**: 0 excessive trunk substitution obscures pelvic action; 1 marked trunk accompaniment; 2 generally independent pelvic motion with intermittent accompaniment; 3 controlled dissociation suitable for style. *Evidence*: shoulder-versus-pelvis relative displacement and tilt, with camera-angle caveats.
3. **Control and transitions**: 0 repeated interruptions; 1 frequent hesitations; 2 mostly smooth transitions; 3 consistent controlled transitions across repetitions. *Evidence*: trajectory continuity, pauses, repeated takes.
4. **Repeatability and tempo control**: 0 no comparable repetition; 1 large changes; 2 broadly consistent; 3 consistent across ≥2 cycles. *Evidence*: time-aligned repetitions. **Musicality is NOT graded without audio and beat analysis.**

**No final level or numerical grade until calibrated against qualified instructors.** Return *not gradable* when landmarks, framing, camera orientation, or sample duration do not support the requested judgment.

## 5-second foundational review
Five seconds can support a **basic** review if the pelvis, shoulders, and enough repetitions are clearly visible. Require evidence of actual movement, not merely elapsed time. A single 2D camera may not distinguish front/back pelvic movement, so do not award full correctness solely from an XY hip-centroid ellipse. Avoid definitive claims about lumbar loading, abdominal engagement, or injury risk.

## Feedback contract
Use **observed movement**, **what to adjust**, **specific practice drill**, **evidence timestamp**, **confidence/limitations**, and **what would demonstrate improvement**. No generic compliments, invented deficiencies, or unsupported muscle-activation claims. Provisional terms only until expert calibration.

## Calibration plan
Obtain instructor-rated reference clips covering camera angles, beginner errors, competent examples, and multiple body types. Compare rubric agreement, timestamp accuracy, false positives, and abstention rates. Version rubrics and retain human override.
