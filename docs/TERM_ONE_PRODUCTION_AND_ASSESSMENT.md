# Term I instructor production and AI assessment specification

## Rules
- Semester is 16 weeks, beginner through demonstrated early-intermediate prerequisites, not automatic intermediate certification.
- Zero new purchases. Free authorized classes and already-owned personal-access courses may be used. No cloud rehosting of purchased courses without redistribution rights.
- The academy must never label a syllabus card as a playable lesson if it has no working video.
- Advanced acro/inversions and extreme flexibility are supervised by a qualified human coach, not approved by AI alone.

## Video sourcing rubric (0–2 per criterion)
1. Qualified, clearly identified instructor and appropriate scope.
2. Explicit teaching and clear spoken or captioned cues (not a music-only demonstration).
3. Accurate progression from beginner prerequisite to application.
4. Camera angles expose feet, pelvis, torso and arm paths as needed.
5. Modifications, pacing and stop cues for relevant risk.
6. Video/audio resolution sufficient to see joint alignment and hear instruction.
7. Accessible at no extra cost or already owned for private use.
8. Verified access and stable original source URL.
9. Practice length matches target (short drill vs full class) and includes repeatable material.
10. Relevant musicality, terminology and performance application.

Score 17–20 = strong candidate; 13–16 = conditional with specific supplements; <=12 = reject for core class. **Safety failure, paywall requirement, or rights misrepresentation override score.** No score has yet been assigned to unviewed videos.

## 16-week source matching requirements
For every week, select:
- One full primary dance technique class with instructor explanation and practice.
- One secondary style technique class or structured drill.
- One performance-flexibility follow-along matched to chosen goal and active range.
- One strength/conditioning follow-along with regressions.
- One short musicality/phrase-learning practice.
- A defined optional video submission and observable feedback rubric.

Do not promise 80 distinct videos; repeats can be pedagogically valuable when progression is documented. Track source URL, exact class title, provider, price/access, running time, instructional level, camera/caption quality, right to embed, right to rehost, playback test date and reviewer.

## Assessment rubric (0–3 each; no arbitrary global AI proficiency claim)
- Alignment and joint organization.
- Controlled initiation, transition and exit.
- Timing/musicality relative to supplied audio.
- Balance, coordination and weight transfer.
- Range used actively without obvious compensation.
- Sequence retention and clarity.
- Individual style criteria (e.g. turnout, jazz lines, groove texture, belly-dance isolation).

**Data quality gate:** full body visible; stable camera; adequate lighting; known left/right orientation; at least one repeat; clear intended movement; explicit uncertainty when occluded. A 2D landmark estimate cannot reliably infer force, rotation in depth, pain, injury or full 3D technique. Report only observable facts, timestamps when video analysis supports them, and specific practice suggestions. Missing or unreliable evidence -> insufficient data, not failure.

## Completion gate
A learner advances by demonstrated, safe control and repeated skill evidence, not by splits depth, number of sessions, or a synthetic AI score. Review with human instruction where safety-critical. Intermediate classes may have style-specific prerequisites.

## Production backlog
1. Match and verify no-cost/owned videos against all 16 weekly competency sets.
2. Build in-app lesson objects referencing original creator players and optional authorized cloud media.
3. Create term-level lesson completion and competency journal with cloud sync.
4. Add actual recorded movement-analysis pipeline and video-specific, validated rubric.
5. Obtain expert review for deep flexibility and acro instruction.
6. Test streaming, permissions, accessibility and mobile behavior.
